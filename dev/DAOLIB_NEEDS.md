# What the turn-based mod needs from DAOLib

Written while building the mod (2026-09-30), for DAOLib's work list. Each item: what the mod wants it for, what is already known (leads read while building this: addresses, class names, strings), what the mod does meanwhile, and how a session in play would show it works. Most urgent first within each section.

## Already done

- **DAOLib as an extern.** Built into this repository from the `extern/DAOLib` submodule through `add_subdirectory` and `add_daolib_plugin`, as Skyrim plugins build CommonLibSSE: DAOLib `eb20478`, "DAOLib can be built inside a plugin's own repository".
- **The command's layout** (was "The command queue", item 3): DAOLib `9b9e067`, read from the script constructors, not yet checked in play. `dao::Command` has `Target()`, `Position()`, `Ability()`, `Static()`, `PlayerIssued()` and its parameters. Types 39 (sheathe or draw) and 41 (turn) are named, and the mod now counts both as free. `DestroyStructure(Command)` returns a command to the pool `AddCommand` itself uses (0x0066DE30), which settles the freeing of a refused script command. The mod does not use the fields yet. They are what the reach check needs (below, "The world under the cursor", item 4).
- **The command queue** (was its items 1, 2 and 4): DAOLib `b660e43`. The queue always takes a command it is given and no caller frees one after, so the gate frees every command it refuses, whoever queued it. The player's orders carry `Command::PlayerIssued`, which the gate now reads for the source instead of guessing from return addresses; the ten callers are named in DAOLib's `Addresses.h`, and the gate warns of any other. `CCObject::CurrentCommand`, `CommandAt` and `CommandQueueSize` read the queue directly; the mod still asks through the script commands (a later change). All 43 command types are named from `commands.gda`, and the mod's core has them all, checked against DAOLib's at compile time.
- **The GDA string and bool getters** (was the UI's item 6): DAOLib `0516b73`, with each table's rows and columns.
- **Sending a message as the engine does** (was the camera's item): DAOLib `c6a0810` and `bc5e718`, `dao::messages::Send`. The camera's follow now sends a `CenterOnObjectMessage` to the current mode through it (`src/game/Camera.cpp`); the registry looks through base classes, so combat mode takes it. Still off by default until seen in play.
- **Calling into a movie** (was the UI's item 2): DAOLib `8c6b9d7` and `d74b27c`: `dao::gui::Movie`'s `SetVariable`, arrays and `Invoke`, and the game's movies found by name. The turn bar pulls instead for now (`dev/UI.md`, "The protocol"), since which thread a movie may be called on is not known.
- **Melee reach** (was "The world under the cursor", item 4): answered by DAOLib's reading of the exe (2026-10-02, its `dev/TURN_BASED.md`, "How close a melee attack strikes from"): both creatures' `PerSpace` and 1.2 m, with the swing's 0.15 m of play; ranged, the weapon's `Range` (at most 60 m) and both. The scripts' 3.5 m, which the mod had used, is the AI's judgement of a melee target, and the game walked a metre further than the mod said (play test 23). The mod read the tables through `dao::gda` and summed them itself until 2026-10-04; since then it asks the engine ("The command queue", 4, below: the reading was wrong in two places, and the mod's sum in a third).
- **Hearing a movie** (was the UI's item 3): DAOLib `fd3bd7f`, `dao/Gui.h`: `OnFSCommand`, `OnExternalInterface`, answering with `Value`s. The turn bar's clicks (end turn, focus a portrait) and its questions (the cards) can come this way once a movie can be shown.
- **A portrait of any creature** (was the UI's item 3, the user's priority for enemies): DAOLib `ce27567`, `CCCreature::RenderPortrait()` and `PortraitImage()`, through the engine's own `CCCreature::UpdatePortrait`. The turn bar renders one per combatant the game has none for, one per view published, once per fight, and uses the creature's existing one when it has one (`src/core/Portraits.h`, `src/game/Bar.cpp`; `dev/UI.md`, "Portraits"). Checks 15 to 17 below. No leak: `CCCreature::~CCCreature` (0x006BAFE0) destroys the renderer at `+0x78C` (0x007F3D00) and frees it (DAOLib session, 2026-09-30), so an enemy's lives as long as the creature, a corpse's until the area unloads. Nothing frees one early on purpose: a movie may still be showing its `img://` name.

19. **Seen 2026-10-05 for a manual save and an autosave, written and read back (`dev/TESTING.md`, "The co-save"); a quicksave not tried, a new game not yet: `dao::saves::OnSaved` and `OnGameLoaded`** (`dao/Saves.h`; the mod's `src/game/CoSave.cpp`, built 2026-10-05 for issue 46). The mod keeps what belongs to a playthrough in its file in each save (`save.Write("turnbased", text)` at `OnSaved`, `save->Read("turnbased")` at `OnGameLoaded`): so far, whose turns their tactics play, a choice a companion. It is the first plugin to depend on the two callbacks, and the header's own cautions are the mod's checks in play (`dev/TESTING.md` here, "The co-save"), each with a line in `turnbased.log` so that a save or a load with none is seen:
    - **the callbacks themselves**, not seen running when the header was written: a manual save, a quicksave and an autosave each bring `whose tactics play its turns, written with <character>'s <kind> save, slot N: ...`, and a load of each `..., read from <character>'s <kind> save, slot N: ...`;
    - **the quicksave written without the hooked `SaveGame::Write`** (one early quicksave in play): such a save would bring no `written` line and would keep the file its slot had before, so a load of it would bring back older choices with no word of it. Wanted from DAOLib if it is seen again: what writes a save on that path, hooked as well, or a way for the plugin to know on a load that its file is older than the save (the save's own time against the file's would do, if `Slot` gave the save's);

    - **the load event more than once for one module**: the mod takes each `OnGameLoaded` as a playthrough come into the world and puts its choices in place of the session's, so a second call for the same load is harmless, and a call with no load behind it would not be.
    What the mod relies on beside: both callbacks on the game's thread, one at a time (it takes its machine's lock in each, and holds it only while the machine answers); `Write` replacing the file whole and never leaving half of one (the file has an `end` line all the same, and a file cut short is read as nothing); and `Read` answering nothing for a save with no file of the plugin's.

## The command queue

1. **The top byte of the command's first word**, not read yet (the rest of the layout is done, above).
2. **An event when a command starts and when it ends**, for any creature. `COMMAND_COMPLETE` reaches `OnScriptEvent` only sometimes (DAOLib's `dev/LIBRARY.md`, the fifth session). The mod polls instead: busy for two frames running means done.
3. **Done** (DAOLib issue 16: `dao::commands::RunningSubAction`; the mod's `ActorObservation::approaching`, `src/game/Bridge.cpp`). **What an attack command is doing now: walking up, or striking** (issue 11, 2026-10-04). The mod had to tell an attacker that swings or aims from one that stands at an attack it cannot begin, and judged it from outside by a walk it had reckoned: wrong 67 times in one fight. Now it reads the sub-action the command is at: 4 (a blow's approach) or 15 (a shot's) is walking up, and what comes after (50, and the swing, 1) is the blow. DAOLib's reading with it: the swing sends `EVENT_TYPE_COMMAND_PENDING` only once the target is alive, the attacker faces it within 60 degrees and has a line of sight, so never as the command starts; a shot's line is the engine's own test, and with none the ranged approach keeps walking; and a melee attacker at the edge of its reach stands when its place on the target's melee ring is somewhere it cannot walk to (the stone golem's doorway).
4. **Done** (DAOLib issue 16: `dao::attack::IsRanged`, `ApproachOf`, `Walk`; the mod's gate, preview and kept attacks, with `tb::Reach`, `kMeleeGap`, `ReachOf` and the walk's sum deleted). **The engine's own reach and approach, called, in place of the mod's sums** (issue 11; the user, 2026-10-04: "Let's invoke the engine code if possible to compute these, and get rid of our own code"). Every test is an engine call: the target's place for the attacker on its melee ring (asked without claiming it) and its in-reach test; a ranged weapon's range by the engine's own lookups, its band test and its line of sight; the walk by the engine's path to where the approach goes. Where a ranged approach ends its walk the engine decides as it walks (its move update asks, each frame, whether it is within the stop distance and has a line of sight from where it stands), so there is no function to call for the answer ahead: DAOLib asks those same two engine tests every 0.25 m along the engine's path, and copies two rules from that update's code, named in its header (without a line it comes 1 m nearer and walks on, and gives up when that would bring it inside both personal spaces). Its ray starts at the path's point, where the engine's starts a little to the attacker's side. On the game's thread, which in play is the scripts'. `Walk` gives the path's points too; the movement preview does not draw them yet (it draws the game's path to the target, cut at the walk's length: two searches a look).
5. **Done** (DAOLib issue 19, `151e624`: `Approach::strikesAtOnce`, `roomOnceFilled` and `findsRoom`, the goal on the filled ring, `Walk` answering nothing for a blow that will find no room, and `ApproachOf` answering nothing only where it was not asked, the two not creatures both; the mod's gate refuses a blow that will find no room, `src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WalkToTarget`, and a kept attack is turned only on one it will find room at, `src/game/Bridge.cpp`, `Measure`; validated in DAOLib's own play of the staged crowd, the mod's use of it not yet seen in play). **Whether the engine's attack will find room on its target's melee ring** (issue 27; seen in play 2026-10-04). `ApproachOf` asked the target's ring for the attacker's place without claiming one, and the mod took a place answered for what the engine's own attack would get. Play said otherwise. In a crowd staged round one party member (twelve dragonlings in a ring, 49 of their turns) the gate's question refused 3 attacks, rightly, and the engine failed 16 more that it had answered a place for (`in its reach by the engine's melee approach: nothing to walk`, or `out of its reach ...: 0.5 m to walk`), 45 to 180 ms later: 7 at the blow (sub-action 1, the log's `is to be struck ... at sub-action 1` in the same millisecond) and 9 with the command still at its approach (4). What DAOLib found from those lines: a ring nobody has claimed is emptied by its creature's update every frame, not filled, so the attack's first ask claims on an empty ring and finds room; the claim makes the ring active, the next frame's refresh records who stands round the target and wipes the claim, and the approach's next ask or the blow's finds no free run. An attacker that strikes in its command's first update (in reach, facing its target already, the target near level and in sight) strikes on its fresh claim, however crowded the ring. Its question now fills an unclaimed ring as the engine fills an active one, asks, and empties it again, and gives both answers. In the staged crowd on it: no room 24 times, all 24 then failed by the engine; room 160 times, none failed for want of it (3 ended `COMMAND_FAILED_INVALID_PATH`, a walker stuck against bodies, which nothing asked beforehand foresees); the 39 that strike at once all landed. The mod kept no memory of its own in the question's place meanwhile: one was built, tried in that fight and withdrawn the same day (`dev/DESIGN.md`, "Every attack is put to the engine afresh").
6. **Why `Walk` answers nothing** (issue 27, 2026-10-04; lower: the mod works without it). The mod now refuses an attack by the engine's own "no" (`src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WalkToTarget`), and a nothing stands for more than one thing that it has to tell apart itself. `ApproachOf`'s did too and is answered (DAOLib `151e624`: nothing only where it was not asked, and a ring with no place is an approach with `findsRoom` false; the gate's `GetObjectType` of each is gone). `Walk`: a blow that will find no room (the gate has that from `findsRoom` before it asks), no path to where the approach goes, a shot the engine would give up on the way, or a search that gave no answer (not asked, or stopped at its 40 ms); the gate finds out with a second search of its own (`dao::world::Pathfind` to the target, toward it), up to 40 ms more on an order that is then refused, and for a blow that is not the search `Walk` made, which goes to the ring's place: the answer the log gives (`the search to its target answers 1`) is the second search's. Wanted: the reason with the nothing (say an `expected`-like answer, or a `Why` beside each), and for `Walk` the `PathSearch` it made. **And a reading, for whoever has the exe open:** why a creature can have no path to anyone from where it stands. A party member on the forest road's spider ambush (`ran210ar_forest_spiders`) stood so through four fights and 22 turns, 39 searches answering 1 with no path that arrives, and walked normally once the area was left; the log now has the search's own trace (`start free ..., destination free ...`) on each such line.
7. **Done as far as the engine keeps it** (DAOLib issue 18: `dao::commands::SubActions`, `HeldFor`, `dao/SubActions.h`, on DAOLib's master as `5e0108a`; this tree's submodule is at `151e624`, the master after it; seen in play 2026-10-04 through this mod's log, at `57e1540`, and corrected from what it showed). **How long the engine still holds a creature at an effect's command, for any effect** (issue 30, built 2026-10-04; the user: "we should not make a special case for it, but fix the way effects cost time by reading the actual amount of time they cost"). A dragon's knockback, 1.2 s by its effect's count, was no kind of hold the mod listed and cost its victim the whole turn, 37 of the party's 176 turns in one fight. The mod now counts a hold's time as it runs on its creature's turn and takes that of the bar, with no list (`dev/DESIGN.md`, "Conditions in turns"), and reads `HeldFor` only to say it in the log beside what the hold took. What the dragon's fight showed (63 rounds, 50 holds): a knockback is an `ExecuteEffect` with one sub-action, 51, timed to the effect's 1,200 ms and found spent as the turn begins, the fall and the rise then holding the fighter 0.6 to 3.2 s with no sub-action left; after the roar the count was about 0.4 s short of the hold. So the engine times only part of a hold ahead, and `HeldFor` since `5e0108a` answers "at least" (known false) for a sub-action that is untimed, or spent and still there, and for a command still current with none left. What is still wanted, in order:
    - **What the other effects' commands are made of**, a knockback's being seen. The mod logs it at debug for the creature whose turn it is (`src/game/Bridge.cpp`, `SayEffectCommand`, as DAOLib's session asked): `0x... is at an effect's command: sub-actions 37 (120 of 1700 ms), 37 (untimed, or not begun); its parameters: ints [...], objects [...], floats [...]`, said again as a type or a duration changes. To read off it: which sub-actions a knockdown, a stun and a grab's victim run; which are timed; whether one waits on the effect itself, untimed, for as long as the effect stands.
    - **What holds a creature when no sub-action is left**: the fall and the rise of a knockback, 0.6 to 3.2 s that the engine keeps on no list the mod can read. If their length is anywhere ahead (the animation's, say), a hold could be read whole.
    - **Which effect the command runs**, read from the command (its parameters are the lead: DAOLib's header says "Which effect an ExecuteEffect runs is not read yet"). With it the time of a hold that lasts while its effect lasts is that effect's own time left (`dao::effects::On` has it, and `Standing::id`), list or no list, and the whole of a hold can be read ahead: the effect's time left and the timed sub-actions after it. That is what would let a turn the hold outlasts be lost at once in place of standing the bar out, and what would let the mod's last list of kinds go for the time (the five conditions, `kHolding` in `src/game/Bridge.cpp`, whose comment says why it is still there).
    - **A count to lose a turn on.** Nothing is decided by the count yet. At `57e1540` it answered known and 0.00 s for every knockback, whose holds then ran 0.61 to 3.19 s (`Sten is held by an effect's command, 0.00 s more by the engine's count`, then `Sten is free of the effect's command after 2.30 s`), and about 2.0 s where the hold ran 2.25 to 2.53 (the getting up after the roar); since `5e0108a` the first reads "at least" nothing. A timed sub-action is not always over at its duration either (the aim, 6, "holds while the target is out of sight", `dao/Conjure.h`), and a command whose effect has just been removed is still there for a frame with its count unchanged. The mod's log sets the count as the hold began beside the time it then ran (`..., at least 0.80 s more by the engine's count`, `..., 2.00 s more by the engine's count`, or `..., how long the engine does not say ahead`), for whatever is read next.
    - **Sub-actions' time under the game's speed and under a creature's own clock.** The reading is that a sub-action counts its creature's own milliseconds, "the same figure its effects age by" (DAOLib's `dev/TURN_BASED.md`, "A command's timed phases"). The mod holds effects' time through a fight and moves each fighter's by its own turns: whether a timed sub-action of a held creature runs with the world while its effects stand still was assumed. For a knockback it does: its sub-action, timed to 1,200 ms, was spent as its victim's turn began (2026-10-04), the mod having moved nothing of the victim's clock meanwhile. A hold whose command waits on its effect, untimed, has not been met in the log yet; that is the case the mod's moving of its creature's clock as the bar pays is for.

## The UI (`dev/UI.md`)

The turn order bar, the movement preview and the on/off switch are made of the game's own Scaleform UI. The bar's side is read now (above), not yet seen in play. In order of need:

**The user's ask of 2026-10-01, first here: a "Turn-based" section in the game's own settings.** "we should add a section, Turn-based, to the game settings. there toggle turn-based combat or not. not just hotkey." Today the switch is a key (Ctrl+T), a line of `turnbased.ini` and a test tool. Wanted: how the options screen is made (its movie, its tabs or sections, how the engine fills one and hears a control change, where it saves), and a way for a plugin to add a section of its own with controls: a section's name, and in it a checkbox to begin with ("Turn-based combat", bound to `[turnbased] enabled`); a slider and a choice of a few would carry the rest of the ini later (the AI's turn speed, who plays the party, the camera following). Each control wants a label, its value now, and a callback when the player changes it, on the game's thread; the mod keeps the value in its own ini, so the game need not save it. The switch may be refused (in a fight it cannot be changed), so a callback that can put the control back, or a way to grey it, would let the screen say so. - **A per-creature chronological clock advance: had, DAOLib `04b3be3` (`dao::clock::OwnClock`, `Advance(id, seconds)`), and used since 2026-10-02 (`[turns] own_clocks`).** And a resource's bytes as the game resolves it: had, `ea4e3f9` (`dao::resources::Read`), used for `rules_core.ncs` at each fight's start. **Next asked, 2026-10-02: a creature's current command held in its conjure phase on its own clock** (the engine's sub-actions' delay, duration and elapsed time, `0x0073CB60`), so that a pending spell is the engine's own conjuring and the installed `rules_core` interrupts it; DAOLib's probe first. As it was asked. **The user's priority, 2026-10-02: "things on each creature's own clock seems like the most important next thing."** Asked of the DAOLib session that hour as three things: one object's effects and regeneration advanced by seconds while the rest stand (`dao::effects::Advance(owner, seconds)`), which the mod would call in small steps as that creature's bar is spent; delayed script events of a second or more held through a fight and advanced by their target object, so a burn ticks on its victim's clock and an area's pulses on the round's; and nothing new for what stands on a creature (`dao::effects::On`). Advance only one creature's durations, regeneration and script timers, stopping at the next event so the mod can inspect death/holds before paying more time. `dao::clock::Advance(float)` currently schedules global ageing for later updates and explicitly excludes `DelayEvent`. Needed validation: advance A while B stays unchanged; a burn that kills or stuns A prevents the rest of the requested action payment and its impact; animation playback does not age either clock again.

As it was asked. **The user's own finding, playing, 2026-10-02:** "there's a environmental poison trap that keeps hitting the party even during turn-based combat. that needs to be put on the timer as well." The world runs while the player chooses and through every creature's action, so an area's pulses come in real time: many a round, and while the player thinks. Asked of the DAOLib session the same hour: what drives an area of effect's pulses (the engine's heartbeat for the object, a `DelayEvent` chain, or both; on which clock), and a hold and an advance for them as `dao::clock::Advance` has for effects, by object if it can be had, so an area pulses once a round's worth at each round and a creature's own ticks on its own turn. Keep the area's script, first heartbeat and later-heartbeat behavior, but run its `DelayEvent` chain on a mod-controlled round clock, independent of its caster's lifetime. Creature DOT chains instead follow their victim. Investigate ownership and dispatch rather than guessing duration versus sequencing solely from the delay length.
- **Command lifecycle before irreversible effects.** Can the library expose rejection/acceptance, resource commitment, and first impact distinctly, or stop a command between validation and effects? The pinned `dao::commands::OnStart` already observes a command before it runs, but its callback contract forbids clearing or queueing commands; the mod also uses `OnEnd` for known no-op failures. Needed is a safe interception/commitment contract, not another start notification. Queueing or observing it busy is not sufficient evidence. Probe an attack, instant ability/mode, potion, multi-hit talent and spell. Document which failures happen after resources or damage; the mod must not promise a free rollback after timers have fired.
- **Actual action/conjuring durations.** The gate currently mistakes conjuring loop counts for seconds. Needed: animation start and loop lengths for the creature, whether conjuring applies, cast/recovery duration, and the weapon's effective timing. Return absence explicitly rather than a zero-duration guess. Preserve the distinction between full cost, first impact and resource commitment.

What has come of these so far (2026-10-02). DAOLib's order, agreed with its session: a creature's effects with time left first, then damage as it is taken, then durations, then one creature's clock, then a command's life. **A creature's effects with time left**: `dao::effects::On(object)` (DAOLib master `a893f20`, `dao/Effects.h`) gives each effect's id, type, duration type, ability and creator, and for a temporary one its duration and seconds left, a snapshot from the frame's thread; not yet seen in play, nor used here. Meanwhile the machine's time mode (`[turns] clock = time`) works on stand-ins: a swing by the game's formula from the weapon (`SwingSeconds`, read through script calls), a talent's or a spell's cast 2 s, its conjuring by `ConjureSeconds`, a drink 1.23 s. Each duration DAOLib gives replaces a stand-in and holds nothing up. The first wanted: a plain attack's length as the engine will play it (non-humanoids' and bows' animations especially, which the formula leaves out).

**Durations, as DAOLib read them in the exe (2026-10-02, its messages; the header `dao/Animation.h` written there and not yet committed).** A humanoid's melee swing is the scripts' own formula, which the mod already charges: `SetAttackDuration(CalculateAttackTiming(...))`, the engine playing the swing sped or slowed to take exactly that. A creature that is not a humanoid, or unarmed, takes its attack animation's own length (`anim_combat` 107, less a fifth for the blend), and the attack speed modifier (property 31) does not reach it: Haste shortens a person's swing, not a bear's. A shot is a draw (a staff 0.30 s; else 0.8 s a follower and 1.5 s the player, 2.0 and 2.5 s in heavy or massive armour, 0.8 and 1.5 s with Master Archer), an aim loop (`GetCreatureRangedDrawSpeed`) and the release animation. A talent has a cast animation alone (`CastAnim`: Dirty Fighting 146, Shield Pummel 2003), a spell a conjure start that passes into a loop (244 into 264, 245 into 265) and a cast that passes into an end (246 into 290, 247 into 291), a potion 257. To come: `dao::animation::Length(creature, animation)`, `AttackDuration`, `AimDuration`, `OfAbility(creature, ability)`; then a creature's swing, a shot and an ability's cast are charged by them, and the stand-ins go.

**`dao::effects::On` is in use and seen in play** (DAOLib `eb2ade1`, play test 26): a stun of 6 s and one of 2 s read as 6000 and 2000 ms. `dao::effects::OnAgeing` (each temporary effect's ageing decided by the plugin) is the lead for effects on their owner's own clock, not taken yet: with every creature at a turn's seconds a turn, ageing a round at a time is close, and is kept until play shows it wrong.

**Since (2026-10-02, DAOLib's messages):** `dao/Animation.h` is in DAOLib master (`a420055` on) and has had a play test of its own: a creature's swing takes about 0.81 of `Length(creature, kAttack)` (a giant spider's 1.83 s, 1.49 s taken); a cast about 0.84 of `OfAbility(...).cast` (Dirty Fighting's 1.80 s, 1.51 s); a humanoid's melee swing the scripts' formula exactly; a longbow's or shortbow's shot 1.4 s for a follower and 1.6 s for the player, which `Length` does not give; `AttackDuration` reads only once a swing is under way, and abilities with no `CastAnim` (Pinning Shot, Overwhelm) read nothing.
