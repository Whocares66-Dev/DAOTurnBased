# Turn-based combat: the design

Dragon Age: Origins fights in real time with pause. This mod makes a fight a sequence of turns: everyone in it acts one at a time, in an order rolled when the fight begins, and the world waits between actions. Outside fights nothing changes. This page is what the mod does and why, for the code (`src/core/TurnMachine.cpp` implements it phase by phase) and for someone who has not played many turn-based games.

**What a turn holds, since 2026-10-02.** By default a turn is a bar of time (`[turns] clock = time`): 4 s that actions and walking both spend, each at its own length, so that a dagger strikes more often than a greatsword, a long spell lands on the next turn, and a little of what is not spent rolls over. `dev/REBALANCE.md`'s "Current specification" has those rules and their reasons, and `dev/REBALANCE_IMPLEMENTATION.md` what is built of them. The turn this page describes below, one action and meters of movement, is the mod's first rule and is still there as `clock = actions`. Everything else here holds for both: who fights, the order, the rounds, what holds the world, what the player sees.

## The words

- **Combatant**: a creature in the fight. Party members, allies that are not in the party (soldiers at Ostagar), enemies.
- **Initiative**: a number rolled for each combatant when the fight starts. Highest acts first. The order is fixed for the fight; newcomers are slotted in.
- **Turn**: one combatant acting. It has one **action** and some **movement** to spend, then passes the turn on.
- **Round**: everyone's turn, once. Round 2 starts when the last combatant in the order has had its turn.
- **Action**: the one thing a turn is for: an attack, an ability or spell, drinking a potion. One per turn for now (see "Action points"). Opening a door or a chest, picking something up and the like are free ("only combat actions ... should" end it, the user, 2026-10-01).
- **Movement**: meters a combatant may walk in its turn, spent as it goes, before or after its action or both.
- **Free**: costs nothing (switching weapon sets).
- **End turn**: giving up what is left: the turn bar's End, or its key (Enter until bound in the game's controls, under Turn-based). The game's pause key did it until 2026-10-02; it pauses now, as it does everywhere else in the game (the user: "space bar should still pause the game i think, instead of end turn. that's what players would expect"). The machine holds the game's pause through a fight, so it takes the key and pauses for the player; an order given while paused runs the game again, since its action has to play out.

## What the player sees

1. A fight starts (the game switches to combat mode). The world freezes, everyone's initiative is rolled, and the order is logged. "Turn-based combat" floats over the hero.
2. **A party member's turn.** The game stays paused and that character is selected ("Alistair's turn" in gold over them). The player gives an order exactly as in the base game: click an enemy, pick an ability, click the ground. The game unpauses while it plays out, then pauses again. The player can move and act in either order until both are spent, or press the pause key to end the turn early. Ordering anyone else gets "Not Morrigan's turn".
3. **An AI combatant's turn** (enemies, allies, and companions under the `tactics` setting). Its name floats over it (red for enemies), the game runs, its AI chooses and acts, and the turn passes. Nothing else moves meanwhile. An enemy that has not noticed the party has "Unaware" over it instead, for a second, and its turn is skipped: the party has the initiative until it notices ("Who is in the fight", below).
4. A combatant who falls loses its turns. Fallen enemies leave the order at the end of the round; fallen party members stay in it, marked down, since they can be revived. A creature that joins mid-fight (reinforcements) stops whatever it was doing and waits for its first turn: this round if its initiative place is still to come, next round if it has passed.
5. When no enemy is left standing, the fight ends, the game unpauses, and real time resumes, the hero selected again (the last turn's companion stayed selected in play, 2026-09-30). The game then leaves combat mode on its own, as it always does. A fight the party runs from ends as the game's own does: when neither side perceives the other any more ("Who is in the fight", below).

## Movement: how far

This was an open question: a constant, or stats? The answer here is **9 meters (BG3's default) times how fast the creature moves against a person**, clamped to 3-20 m, so what the game already treats as speed matters. The pace comes from the creature's appearance: its combat speed in the appearance table (`Game_CbtSpeed`) over a person's 4.66 m/s. People, genlocks and hurlocks get 9 m, a mabari 9.6, a shriek or werewolf 14.2, a dragon 13.2, an ogre 7.6. Haste and slows are effects (`EFFECT_TYPE_MOVEMENT_RATE`, a potency of 1.5 or 0.5) and will multiply it once DAOLib reads a creature's effects. The scripts' movement speed property is no use: it is the flanking angle in the shipped table (the first session in play, 2026-09-30). Dexterity can add meters too (`movement.per_dexterity`), off by default because DAO's Dexterity already carries attack and defence. `movement.rule = constant` gives everyone the same.

Movement is measured, not predicted: the distance walked while the creature is moving under its own command, frame by frame, teleports ignored. When the budget runs out mid-walk, the creature is stopped and "Out of movement" floats over it. If it was walking to an attack that it never reached, the action is given back, once a turn: it may still attack something in reach. An AI creature that runs out ends its turn.

**A player's attack that cannot reach its target this turn is refused before a step is taken**: "Too far" floats over the attacker, and the action is not spent, as BG3 refuses a red target. Whether it reaches, and how far its creature would walk first, is the engine's own answer, asked for the pair as they stand ("An attack's reach and walk are the engine's own", below). It is asked only when the order reaches the queue on the scripts' thread, where script commands may be called and which in play is the game's own; the log says which the first time.

**An attack's reach and walk are the engine's own** (issue 11, 2026-10-04; `src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WalkToTarget`; DAOLib's `dao::attack`, its issue 16). The mod keeps no rule of its own for either. The user's direction that day, after the mod's own rule had been wrong: "We should invoke the engine functions where necessary. If we absolutely can't, we need to decompile the code and replicate the logic ourselves very carefully." The attack command queues an approach and then the blow, and the approach decides on its first run whether there is anything to walk. For a blow the target gives the attacker a place on its melee ring, and the attacker is in reach when the centres are at most both personal spaces and 1.2 m apart and it stands within 1.25 m of that place; else it walks to the place. For a shot (a bow, a crossbow, a staff) it is in range when the distance on the ground is at most the weapon's range, 60 m at most, and at least both personal spaces, and it has a line of sight; else it walks toward the target until that holds. The gate asks the engine those questions through DAOLib (`ApproachOf`: in reach or not, where the approach goes and how near it stops; `Walk`: the engine's path there, its length) and charges and refuses by the answers: in reach there is nothing to walk, nothing to pay for a walk and nothing to refuse. The movement preview shows the same number. Where the engine's path must not be searched (its creature is at a command already) the walk is the line to where the approach goes; a shot in range with no line of sight walks for one, and how far is not known before it has. Until then the mod had a sum of its own, written from notes of the exe: the path's length to the target less a reach it worked out. The path goes round whoever and whatever stands between, and the engine does not go by it. On the highway where Zevran lies in wait the archers stand on slopes above the road, 20 m from their targets and 75 m by the path: each was told it had 30 m to walk, shot from where it stood, and was stopped a second into a shot of four to six as an attacker "standing short of where its walk was to end", 67 times in one fight; the party's own mage was refused "Too far" at 15, 19 and 25 m with a staff that reaches 45. In melee the same: a skeleton 1.8 m from its target, 3.1 m by the path round the others, stopped four times in one turn a second into a swing of two and a half. The notes were wrong besides: the personal spaces are not added to a shot's range, and a blow's reach asks for the ring's place too.

**What the engine answers no to is refused before a step** (issue 27, 2026-10-04; `src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WalkToTarget`; `TurnMachine::NoAttackToMake`; an AI's seen in play that day, the player's and the no-path and no-line answers not yet). Both of those questions have a "no" among their answers, and until then the gate went on past it with a guess of its own, which is what the user's rule forbids: where the engine itself decides, ask it and act on its answer, and put no figure of the mod's in its place. The game layer now tells the machine which no it had (`CommandRequest::noAttack`), and the machine refuses the attack before it is let through, nothing spent:

- **No room at the target.** A blow needs a place on its target's melee ring, and whether the engine's attack will find one is asked of the engine through DAOLib ("Whether the attack will find room is the engine's own answer too", below). No room (the approach's `findsRoom` false) is one of two things. The ring as it stands has no place for the attacker, and the engine's approach fails at its first run. Or the first ask would have a place and the attack would still find none: it does not strike in its first update, and the ring once filled has no room. The first, the gate used to let through as "the engine has no approach for it; its walk is not known", and the engine failed it within two milliseconds, `COMMAND_FAILED_NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING`: 6 of 6 times in the caverns above Haven, dragonlings swarming one party member. The second nobody could tell beforehand until DAOLib found how the ring works. (`ApproachOf` answers nothing only where it was not asked, the two not creatures both: an attack on a crate or a door, let through as ever with its walk not known.)
- **No path.** Out of reach, the path searched (its creature at rest) and `Walk` answers nothing, with no door in the way: the engine has no path to where its approach goes. The gate used to say "the game finds no path to it (1); judged by the straight line", reckon a walk by that line and let it through, and the engine failed it four tenths of a second on, `COMMAND_FAILED_INVALID_PATH`: 15 times for one party member who stood through four fights on the forest road at a spot the pathfinder found no way from, and 4 more of his orders were refused "Too far" by the reckoned walk, the wrong reason. Why he had no path from there has not been read; the log now has the search's own account of itself beside the answer.
- **No line for a shot.** The same answer from `Walk` for a bow or a staff whose path to its target does arrive: the engine would walk its archer toward the target for a line of sight, find none from anywhere along the way, and give the shot up beside it (DAOLib follows the move update's own tests along the path). Not in the issue's evidence, and rare by what it takes (no line even from beside the target: one on a ledge above, say); refused by the same rule, in its own words.

The player's own order is told why, in the words the failure under way would have had: "No room at the target", "No way through", "No line of sight" over the character in grey, as "Too far" is, the `refused` event with the engine's answer as its note, and on the bar's caption for five seconds "No room at the target: Sten's attack is refused" (refused, not given back: nothing was taken). An AI's is given back before a step, and what it does then is its own, the mod not choosing for it: it is told to choose again, once, as after a failure under way, and a second action given back in the turn, before a step or under way, ends it, "nothing it can get to" ("The AI's turn", below), with nothing of its bar rolled over: the mod ended that turn for it, and time rolls over only from a turn its actor ended (issue 29; "Time rolls over only from a turn its actor ended", below). A hostile that has noticed nobody to fight is not told its turn has come (issue 29), so it queues no attack to refuse. One its own script queues in its beat that the engine has no way to make is refused and no more: not counted as given back, and its AI not told again, which would wake it; the beat goes on to its end and the turn is skipped "unaware". One that has noticed is told by the next step, as issue 29 has it, and its choice is then asked of the engine like any. Seen in play (2026-10-04: twelve dragonlings staged in a ring round one party member, 49 of their turns): three attacks given back so, each followed by `Dragonling chooses again, its action given back`, with no attack let through and no failure of the engine's between. Not yet seen: the player's refusal on screen (in five fights no order of the party's met a ring the question called full, and none was refused wrongly), the no-path and no-line answers, and the refusal by the fuller question the gate asks since (`findsRoom`, below).

**Every attack is put to the engine afresh, and nothing of a no is remembered.** The engine's no is for where the two stood when it was asked; once its creature's script has moved it, the engine is asked again. The first build of this kept a memory beside the question: a target the engine had refused, or whose attack it had failed under way for want of a place or a path, was "ruled out for the rest of its turn", and an AI's next attack on it that turn was refused unasked. It was built, tried in the staged fight and withdrawn the same day: it was the mod's own reckoning standing in for the engine's answer, the very thing this rule is against, and wrong about four times in ten. The game's own AI answers a failed attack with a move closer and the same target again (`ai_main_h_2.nss`, `AI_ExecuteAttack`), and that second attack is often good: in three logs from before the rule, 17 turns had a failure for want of a place, and in 7 of them a later attack on the same target that turn went through and did not fail (let through, failed, moved, let through, done). The rule refused exactly those. The commonest failing turn of the staged fight, 9 of 49, read: let through; failed 45 to 180 ms on, at the blow; `move to object`; the same target "given back before a step (ruled out this turn)"; `turn ends: nothing it can get to`, with the gate's own line just above the refusal saying "in its reach by the engine's melee approach: nothing to walk". Nor did it get an AI onto another target: its script chooses by threat, and after a rule-out it chose the same one 13 times of 16. What is kept of it is the failed command's target, passed to the machine for the log's line alone (`Dragonling's attack on Lyna failed (COMMAND_FAILED_NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING): its action is given back`; right in each of 18 such lines of that fight): in a crowd the names do not say which attack on whom.

**Whether the attack will find room is the engine's own answer too** (DAOLib issue 19, `151e624`: `Approach::findsRoom`; built against it 2026-10-04, the gate's use of it not yet seen in play). The first build took a place answered by `ApproachOf` for the engine's promise, and the staged fight showed it was not one: the gate's question refused 3 attacks, rightly, and the engine failed 16 more that it had let through as "in its reach: nothing to walk" or "0.5 m to walk", 45 to 180 ms on, 7 at the blow (the command's sub-action 1) and 9 with the command still at its approach (4). The cause, found by DAOLib in the exe from those lines: a ring nobody has claimed is emptied by its creature's update every frame, not filled, so the attack's first ask claims on an empty ring and finds room; the claim makes the ring active, the next frame's refresh records who stands round the target and wipes the claim, and the approach's next ask or the blow's then finds no free run of places. An attacker that strikes in its command's first update (in reach, already facing its target within a degree, the target within 60 degrees of level and in sight) strikes on its fresh claim, and lands however crowded the ring is. So DAOLib asks both, by the engine's own code and claiming nothing: whether the blow comes at once (`strikesAtOnce`), and whether the ring has a place for the attacker once filled as it will be a frame after the claim (`roomOnceFilled`); `findsRoom` is either (and false where the ring as it stands has no place at all), and the walk's goal is the place on the filled ring. The gate refuses a blow that will find no room, as no place, before a step. In the same staged crowd on that DAOLib (its own validation, 184 attacks): no room 24 times, and all 24 then failed for want of a place; room 160 times, and none did; the 39 that strike at once all landed. What still fails under way is what nothing asked beforehand foresees: 3 of the 160 ended `COMMAND_FAILED_INVALID_PATH`, a walker stuck against bodies after a path was given, which by DAOLib's reading cannot be known before the walk (the engine's own stuck timer: under 2 cm an update for a second of the walker's own time). Those are given back as before ("The AI's turn", below) and answered by the AI's own script, and a turn that keeps failing is ended by what ended it before: the count of actions given back (two, where its creature comes to rest between them), the cap on a turn's commands (`max_commands_per_turn`, where its script queues the next thing as each ends: three attacks and three moves), and since issue 31 the wait its script queues after a failure, which costs its own time. A kept attack turned on another, its own target fallen, goes by the same answer: only on one in its reach at which it will find room (`src/game/Bridge.cpp`, `Measure`).

**A kept attack is asked as it is given** (`TurnMachine::KeptAttackNotMade`; the gap seen in play 2026-10-04 on the build before, closed that day, not yet seen in play). With a turn a bar of time an attack that does not fit is kept, and given to its creature as its next turn begins, through the gate; and the gate let it through whatever the engine answered: paid for, it was to land. In the staged crowd on that build 328 attacks were let through with room and none of them failed for want of it; the only two that did were kept attacks, each with the gate's own line above it saying "no room once its ring is filled". The engine failed them 50 ms on, and what their turns had paid for them (0.01 s and 0.84 s) stayed spent: a landed attack's time is its turns', not that command's to give back. So a kept attack the engine has no way to make as its turn comes (no room, no path, no line for a shot) is not made. It is dropped as one whose target is gone is ("Casting time", below): what this turn paid for it is given back and the turn is handed over whole, with the next look at everyone; what the turn before spent on it is spent, as it is then too. The player is told as for a refused order, the words over the character and on the caption "No room at the target: Sten's attack is not made". An AI's counts as an action given back, so that the same answer to what it chooses next ends its turn, and its being handed the turn is its being told. The event is `action_dropped`, its `detail` the engine's answer. A kept talent or spell carries no answer of the engine's and is given as before.

The straight line is kept for what it was meant for, where the engine has not been asked: its creature is at a command already, so no path may be searched, or `path_check` is off; and where it was asked and gave no answer (a search DAOLib stopped when its 40 ms ran out). A shut door in the way stays its own rule ("A shut door in the way", below).

An AI's attack at something far away is not refused: it walks what it can and is stopped, which is how an AI closes in over a turn or two. Each later try of an AI to reach something out of reach still takes one step before it is stopped, and so may a player's where the walk could not be worked out. The once-a-turn refund bounds that: a creature can end at most the stopping distance plus a few frames' walk (a fraction of a meter) past its budget, and the random fights (`tests/test_fights.cpp`) check the bound.

Abilities and spells are not checked for reach yet: an ability's range is in its own table (`abi_base`), not read so far.

The engine's pathfinder is reachable since DAOLib `8819b7c` (`world::FindPath`, for an idle creature; built 2026-09-30, not yet seen in play): a click on the ground whose path is longer than the meters left is refused before a step ("Too far"), as an attack out of reach is, and the walk of an attack out of reach is measured along the engine's path to where its approach goes rather than a straight line (in reach it has none: above). `[movement] path_check` turns it off, back to a straight line and a move that walks until its meters run out. The gold/red arc preview still needs a way to draw in the world (`dev/UI.md`, `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`).

## Time

The world runs only while someone acts. Everything that ticks in seconds ticks only then: effect durations, damage over time, cooldowns, stamina and mana regeneration. So a round of eight combatants, each acting for about a second and a half, is about twelve seconds of game time, and a 30-second cooldown comes back in two or three rounds. That keeps the base game's numbers meaning roughly what they meant.

One distortion follows: a round in a big fight lasts longer than a round in a small one, so cooldowns recover faster, per round, in big fights. `turns.round_min_seconds` evens it out by giving every round at least that much game time: if the turns ran short, the world runs on, nobody acting, before the next round. Off by default. Converting durations and cooldowns to rounds outright needs DAOLib (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Rules").

### Time rolls over only from a turn its actor ended (issue 29, built 2026-10-04)

With a turn a bar of time, what a turn leaves unspent rolls over to its creature's next, up to its cap. Until 2026-10-04 it did so whoever had ended the turn: a skeleton that never noticed the party read `Skeleton's turn ends: chose nothing; 1.00 s rolls over` every round, and a held companion `Alistair's turn ends: held by an effect; 1.00 s rolls over`. The user: "When turn is skipped, due to unawareness, incapacity, etc, there should be no time rollover, because the actor did not explicitly save any time."

So time rolls over only when the actor itself left it unspent. Every reason the machine ends a turn for is in one of two classes, in one place (`src/core/TurnEnd.h`, `EndedByItsActor`; `TurnMachine::EndTurn` asks it and nothing else decides), and a reason that table does not have rolls nothing over, with a warning in the log (`tools/test_turnend.py` reads the machine's source and fails on one that is not classed):

| Why the turn ended (the log's words, and `turn_ended`'s `detail`) | Class | What rolls over |
|---|---|---|
| `ended by the player` (End, its key, a tool) | its actor's | what is left, to the cap |
| `done` (an AI that acted and chose to wait, or chose nothing more as its action settled) | its actor's | what is left, to the cap |
| `waits` (an AI told its turn had come that chose to wait) | its actor's | what is left, to the cap |
| `nothing left to spend`, `its action pending`, `charging`, `its spell conjuring`, `its spell cast at once` | its actor's | nothing is left: the actor spent the bar |
| `unaware` (a hostile that has noticed nobody to fight: "An unaware hostile's turn", under "Who is in the fight") | skipped | nothing |
| `chose nothing` (its AI queued nothing in `ai_think_ms`), `took too long`, `nothing it can get to`, `out of movement` | ended for it | nothing |
| `fell`, `gone`, `down` | ended for it | nothing |
| `stunned`, `held by its condition`, `held by another's action`, `held by an effect` | lost | nothing |

The log says which: `Genlock's turn ends: done; 1.00 s rolls over`, or `Skeleton's turn ends: chose nothing; nothing rolls over, its actor not having ended it (1.00 s would have)`; with nothing left, `...; nothing rolls over`. Three of these are judgement calls, the user's to change: an AI stopped where its movement ran out (`out of movement`: with time its bar is spent by then, so the class changes nothing seen); an AI whose actions were given back twice (`nothing it can get to`: the time of what it could not do is back in its bar, and it did not choose to keep it); and `done` reached because an AI hit `max_commands_per_turn` with time left, which is the mod's guard ending the turn, not the AI, but is not told apart from an AI that is done.

### Conditions in turns (built 2026-09-30)

The user's request after the first fights in play: a stun, measured in seconds, lasted more turns than it read as, and should be measured in turns, derived from its time, with a saving throw at the start of each turn. As BG3 and D&D do it:

- **A condition's length in turns** is its duration over a round's worth of seconds, rounded up: 6 s a round (`turns.seconds_per_round`), so the mabari howl's 5 s stun is one turn, Pinning Shot's 8.5 s paralysis two, Holy Smite's 10 s two.
- **It costs the creature its turns.** At the start of each of its turns while it lasts, a disabled creature (stunned, paralysed, asleep: `GetEffectsFlags` has `EFFECT_FLAG_DISABLE_COMBAT`) loses the turn at once, with "Stunned" over it and on its card, and the world does not run for it.
- **A save at the start of the turn** can end it early: a roll against the creature's physical or mental resistance (the attributes the game rolls its own resistances with; which one by the effect's kind), as BG3 rolls a save at the end of each turn.
- **It ends on the creature's own turn.** When its turns are spent, or the save succeeds, the mod removes the effect (`RemoveEffect`, wrapped) at the start of the creature's turn, so it acts that turn. The engine's own timer no longer matters.
- **Knockdowns and pins** are conditions like the rest (below).

Built with `condition_turns` turns per condition (1), the fallback while each disabling effect's full duration is out of reach (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Rules", item 2); saves are still to build. As built:

- a stun, paralysis, sleep, pin (Pinning Shot's root) or knockdown is seen at the next status read, every half second and at every turn start, and the creature owes that many turns; each is taken at its start, "Name loses the turn" floating, the world still;
- a creature stunned in its own turn loses the rest of it at once, and whatever was queued for it since is cleared; it still owes its turns, so a hold landing mid-turn costs the rest of that turn and the next, where one landing just before costs one. Intended (the user, 2026-09-30, through the fourth play test: "if you fuck up on your own turn, you are extra punished");
- a hold that ends before its victim's turn: run out on the clock (effects' time moves only as a round begins, so a stun shorter than a round ends there) it keeps the turns it owes, or a short stun would cost nothing; taken off by something else (a cure, a dispel; with effects' time held, any time but just after a round's aging), it gives them back (the fourth play test's addendum: a lifted stun still cost the turn);
- a pin was first built to take only the movement, the action kept, as the root's script reads (`effect_root_h`: the AI's stationary flag). In play (2026-09-30, 13:25) the engine held the pinned creature in the effect's command (`ExecuteEffect`, queued at the front as the pin landed: DAOLib's probe log); its attack queued behind and never ran, and with effects' time held the command outlasted the turn, which ended on the 12 s limit. So a pin costs the turn, as the user had put it ("turns of no action"); a knockdown too, which the engine handles the same way (not yet seen). Daze is only a penalty (`effect_daze_h`) and costs nothing; a grab (`EFFECT_TYPE_GRABBED`, ogres) is none of these conditions, and its victim is held as anyone at an effect's command is (below);
- End pressed while the engine holds the creature in something the gate did not see (an effect's command) ends the turn at once; its own action it still finishes first;
- **what holds a creature at an effect's command costs the time the engine holds it** (issue 30, built 2026-10-04 and seen in play that day, below; the user, on reading a dragon's knockback as one more kind of hold: "we should not make a special case for it, but fix the way effects cost time by reading the actual amount of time they cost"). A creature at an effect's command it did not choose, as its turn comes or before it has acted in it, is held, whatever the effect: a knockback, an ogre's grab, the getting up from a stun the machine ended. No list of kinds says so; the engine does, by where it has the creature. The hold runs on its turn, the world running, and the time it runs is the turn's. With a bar it takes that share of it by the turn's ratio, as a condition's seconds are taken, and the creature's own clock moves with it, so that an effect which lasts while its time lasts wears off as its creature pays for it; where the fight holds one clock for everyone's effects, that clock runs with the hold, a quarter second at a time and off the round's worth, as it does with an action's own effect. As built, with no bar, the turn waits, and is lost once the hold has run a turn's worth: `effect_seconds_per_round`, or `seconds_per_turn` with effects on the game's clock. Let go, the creature has the rest of its turn: an action it kept is given then (an attack asked of the engine as it is given, like any: one the engine has no way to make by then is not made, and what this turn paid for it is given back, issue 27; the hold's time is the hold's all the same), an AI is told to choose, a party member's turn waits for an order (let go is two frames idle, as any command's end is: one frame can fall between two commands the engine queues, a fall and then a getting up if those are two, and what was kept, given into it, would queue behind the second). **The turn is lost only when the hold outlasts the bar**, and nothing of the bar is then left to roll over. The time is the game's: under a menu or the player's pause, where the game stands and the effect's command with it, nothing is taken. ### Cooldowns in turns (built 2026-09-30)

DAO's cooldowns run in real time, paused or not: passing turns quickly left a talent cooling, and waiting brought it back (the user). An ability used is held on cooldown in the engine (3600 s, where waiting cannot reach) once its action has run, and released at the start of its user's turn that many turns later: its cooldown in the ability table over `cooldown_seconds_per_turn` (4), rounded up. At 4: Shield Bash and Pinning Shot 4 (seen in play, play test 5), the mabari howl about 10, Overwhelm about 15. An action taken with the movement spent is at something in reach, and its creature may shift its feet up to a metre to strike before "out of movement" stops it: Eleanor's Shield Bash at a soldier 2 m away was cleared 9 ms after it was let through, the action gone (the seventh play test). An ability its user was walking to use when the movement ran out never ran, so it is not held (the user, 2026-09-30: chosen, not reached, and wasted on cooldown). It was 10 until the fourth play test, where the archers' stun came back every other turn and the dog acted every other round; a stun or a pin costs its victim a whole turn however short it is, so at 4 it keeps about the share of the time it has in real time (the howl's 5 s stun on a 40 s cooldown, an eighth; the user's call, 2026-09-30). A lost turn counts. At the fight's end what still waits goes back to seconds. **The quickbar's sweep shows the turns** (the user, 2026-09-30: with 2 of 3 turns left to wait, a third recovered): at each of its user's turns a held ability is set to its turns left over the turns it was held for, on the hold's 3600 s scale, through DAOLib's `dao::abilities::SetCooldown(creature, ability, remaining, total)`; the scripts' `SetCooldown` sets both numbers as one, which showed a full sweep that never moved. **Sustained abilities** (the ability table's `UseType` 2: Shield Wall, Powerful Swings, Flaming Weapons, Rock Armor, Stealth) cost nothing to turn on or off, and do not end the turn; turned off, one waits for its user's next turn, so it cannot be turned on, off and on again in one turn (the user's rule). Action points will put a price on them.


### Everything in turns: the overhaul still to do

The user's rule (2026-09-30): in turn-based mode everything runs on turns (or, later, action points), nothing on time. Built so far: conditions (stun, paralysis, sleep, pins) and cooldowns. Still on the game's clock, running whenever the world runs:

- **Effect durations**: buffs, debuffs, damage and healing over time (burning, poison, bleeding, regeneration potions, auras), each a duration in seconds on a `CGameEffect`.
- **Regeneration**: stamina, mana and health coming back each second, in and out of combat.
- **Sustained abilities' upkeep**: their drain, if any, per second, and their stamina or mana reserved.
- **Areas that last**: spell areas on the ground (Grease, Blizzard, Inferno), traps' areas and lures, a duration each.
- **Summons and transformations** with a duration.
- **Casting and attack timing**: an action's own animation time, which is fine as it is (the world runs while an action plays).

**Built for effects (2026-09-30, DAOLib `4fee0fa`):** every temporary effect ages through one engine function, which DAOLib hooks (`dao/Effects.h`). A fight holds that clock from its start (`HoldTime`), spends `effect_seconds_per_round` on every effect at each new round (`AgeTime`), and lets it go when the fight ends or is dropped: buffs, debuffs, damage and healing over time, timed summons and, it is thought, lasting areas count rounds. Regeneration too, since DAOLib `a42be67`: each creature's stats tick it every 2 s in combat (`UpdateRegeneration`), and `dao/Clock.h` holds and moves on effects and regeneration together; a 6 s round is three ticks. Still on the clock: timers scripts set themselves with `DelayEvent`. Not yet seen in play.

**An action's own effect gets its time** (built 2026-10-01). Effects' time is held through a fight and spent a round's worth at each round. But some actions are themselves an effect that must run out: a giant spider's Overwhelm is one effect on the spider and one on its prey, each ended by its time, and held, the pounce never ended: the world stood twelve seconds until `max_action_ms` stopped it and the turn ended "took too long" (the eighth play test). So while the acting creature is at an effect's command after its own action, effects' time runs with the world, and what it took comes off that round's worth. An effect that held a creature before its turn began costs the time it holds it, effects' time running with it the same way (issue 30: "Conditions in turns", above; until 2026-10-04 it cost the turn, and effects' time stood still for it); a stun in one's own turn still ends it.

The direction: the world's clock stops being the fight's clock. A round (or each creature's turn) advances what belongs to its creatures by a fixed amount, as conditions and cooldowns do now: durations counted in turns and ended at the start of the owner's turn; regeneration given per turn; areas lasting so many rounds. Two ways to get there, which the DAOLib work decides between:

- **Hold the clock and step it**: the game's time scale, or its effect timers, frozen outside actions, and advanced by a round's worth at each round (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Time and input": the time scale, advancing game time without running the world).
- **Own each timer**: read and set an effect's remaining duration (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Rules": an effect's duration and remaining time), hold it as cooldowns are held, and count it down per turn; set regeneration to nothing in fights and give it per turn.

Until then, `pause_on_player_turn = true` keeps what ticks from ticking while the player chooses; off (the default, the user's choice), the world idles on and those timers run.

**The scripts' own timers count rounds too** (issue 26, 2026-10-04; `script_timers_from`, 1 s; DAOLib `dao::timers::HoldFrom`). A round is 4 s for each fighter, but as many times 4 s of the world's clock as there are fighters, since the world runs through each one's turn in sequence. So a plot's timer left on the world's clock runs that many times too fast against what the fighters do. In the Harrowing Chamber Uldred turns the captive mages into abominations one at a time: a light comes on the mage, and the Litany of Adralla read while it is on stops it. His script (`cir240cr_uldred.nss`, read in the toolset's source) begins a turning when he is hurt below 90, 65, 40 and 20 per cent, or on a timer of his own every 35 s, and gives it ten seconds. On the world's clock all four turnings fell in the first three rounds, with one or two fighters' turns between the light and the death: a mage was lost across one abomination's turn before any of the party had a turn to read in. The user, 2026-10-04: "We should probably figure out a way to suspend the time so that no matter where an actor is in the round, they get to react to it. Recall that a round is supposed to be the same 4 seconds for everyone for things that apply over time or are environmental. Direct damage like attacks take place immediately, which is affected by order in the round. But something like interruption with the litany of adralla should be allowed any position in the round." So through a fight every script event sent with a delay of a second or more waits on the clock effects wait on, and comes as the rounds spend it, whatever its kind; one already waiting in the engine's queue as the fight begins is taken by the same rule. Under a second the scripts are putting one thing after another (Uldred's change of shape, 0.01 s) and run as they do, and the core scripts' return to explore mode after the last enemy falls is left out by DAOLib, or no fight would end. Seen in that fight (DAOLib's trace of delayed events in the log, `script_timers_trace`): the 35 s came due in round 10, each turning's ten seconds were two to three rounds, the Litany was read in the round after each of four turnings, and nobody was turned, where five earlier plays had lost one to four mages. **What it cannot mend** is the script's own race, which real time has too: the Litany's break is one flag, and a turning that begins and is broken while an earlier broken turning's ten seconds are still to come has its break used up by the earlier one. In one test with these timers held by hand two mages died that way. `script_timers_from = 0` is the old way: only a damage over time's ticks and a lasting area's pulses count rounds.

## Who is in the fight (built 2026-09-30)

The game says who is in combat, not with whom. In Castle Cousland Howe's men and the castle's guards fight in rooms the party has not reached, all "in combat" and within `combat_radius`: a fight took in nine enemies, the turn order filled with creatures nobody could see, and the camera went to rooms that are not drawn (the user's picture, 2026-09-30). The user did not want a rule of distance alone: it cuts out enemies who should be in the fight and takes in ones who should not.

So an enemy or ally in combat within the radius takes turns only once it is **fighting where the player can see it** (`join_by_sight`, on): it stands in a room the game shows, or it or a party member is the other's attack target. The room is the engine's own answer and the user's own rule ("suspend the ones in areas that aren't loaded"): an area is made of rooms, and the game hides every room no party member has yet seen, drawing it black and leaving its creatures inactive, even through an open door, until one of the party walks in (lib-agent's reading and play test, DAOLib `1d99f54`, `dao::world::RoomOf(creature)->Shown()`). What is seen goes by the party, not the camera, so the mod's camera going to such a room shows nothing. The first rule built here was a party member's line of sight, which is clear through that open door; it remains only for an area with no rooms, or one where the party leader's own room does not read as shown (the rooms cannot be gone by there). Until then it **waits outside the fight**, where it stands: its commands are held like a joiner's, it is not in the turn order, and nothing of it is shown. When the party sees its room, or one attacks the other, it joins as any creature joining mid-way does, with a roll of initiative. **Once in, it stays in**: stepping behind a pillar does not take a creature out of the fight. The fight is over when the last enemy in it falls; one still waiting outside is the game's again, in real time, and begins turns again when it finds the party.

**And near, if it is not on the party** (`join_radius`, 30 m; the user, 2026-10-03: "50/30 split seems reasonable to me"). Out of doors the room is no answer: every room within the radius is one the party has seen. In the Korcari Wilds the party set on four darkspawn, and a hurlock, an archer and four wolves fighting each other 45 m off, in plain sight, joined: thirteen in the order, eighteen rounds (issue 6, the playthrough of issue 3). The game's own aggro had set creatures on the party from 17 to 23 m in those fights, so it is not the radius that brings enemies in, and a smaller radius would not do: a creature in combat beyond it is held, and an archer shooting from 35 m would stand frozen under the party's own bows. So the two are split: one that **attacks a party member or is attacked by one** joins from anywhere in `combat_radius`, seen or not, as before; one that is only in combat joins if it is seen **and within `join_radius` of a party member**. The rest wait outside as the unseen do, a bystanders' battle standing as it is until the party's fight is over, when it goes on in real time. Those of the fight itself that still stand when it ends, enemy or ally, are told so too: an idle AI lives on a loop of waits, each one's end running it again, and in a fight its wait is its pass and is blocked, so with the turns over it had nothing queued and nothing to wake it. At Redcliffe's windmill the game ended combat with one walking corpse alive up the path; it stood there, and the night's second battle, which waits on the first's dead, never began (issue 12, 2026-10-03). The facts are the game layer's (`Sight`, `src/game/Bridge.cpp`), the rule core's (`InThePartysFight`, `src/core/Rules.cpp`).

**And, with nobody else to fight, whether or not it has noticed** (issues 20 and 24, 2026-10-03). The game counts a creature as in combat once it has noticed an enemy. A hostile that has not (its back turned, a script keeping it at a door) left the party "in combat" by the game's reckoning with nobody to take turns against: combat music, no saving, the world running on in real time and the party standing, until someone struck it (three corpses clawing at a cell's door 20 m off in Redcliffe's dungeon; a second band down the road after an ambush was won). So with the party in combat and no enemy in its fight, a living hostile in a party member's line of sight within `join_radius` is in the fight though it is not in combat itself (core: `FightsUnawares`; `Bridge::Scan`). Its first turn is its own AI's, which may be to go on with what it was doing. Sight is a party member's line here whatever the rooms say, and whatever `join_by_sight` says: a hostile behind a wall has not been met. With the party at peace nothing changes: walking past a hostile that has not noticed is the game's to make a fight of. **Only with nobody else to fight:** for an evening the rule drew in every hostile in sight within the radius whenever the party was in combat (the user, of issue 20: "If they're within range and hostile they're fighting"), and in the open that made one fight of several bands. On the Fade's Darkspawn Invasion a pull of three became seven, three of them drawn in unawares, and the hero alone lost it five times running; with the bands taken as they noticed her the same stretch was six fights of two or three, all won. A fight in turns gives every fighter a whole turn each round, so one against seven is seven turns to one, where in real time the far ones would still be standing idle. The user, of drawing them in only when there is nobody else to fight (issue 24): "Yeah, let's do that". A fight that has an enemy grows as the game's own perception makes it grow; one already drawn in stays in while the party is in combat, so that its card does not go and come again as its creature notices.

**An unaware hostile's turn** (issue 29, built and seen in play 2026-10-04: the Brecilian ruins' lower level, 47 turns skipped so and none `chose nothing`, where one fight there had 54; "Unaware" in grey over each, `images/unaware-skeletons-2026-10-04.jpg`). One drawn in so has not noticed the party, and was told its turn had come like anyone: its AI, which chooses among the hostiles it perceives, chose nothing, the machine waited `ai_think_ms` for it, and the turn ended `chose nothing`. In the Brecilian ruins' lower level three skeletons that never noticed the party took 54 such turns in one fight (`... chose nothing: combat state 0, ...`). Making them notice as they are drawn in (the engine's `TriggerPerception`) was proposed, and the user: "If they don't notice the party that gives the party the initiative to close in and attack. I think that's a feature, not a bug"; and of their turns, "We can show text unaware above the enemy's head, pause for about a second, then skip their turn." So a hostile in the fight that has noticed nobody to fight is not woken, and not told its turn has come: "Unaware" floats over it in grey, in place of its name; the world runs for a second of the fight's clock (`TurnMachine::kUnawareMs`, the world's time as an AI's thinking is: at twice the speed half as long on the wall); and its turn ends `unaware`, nothing rolled over. The scripts are told of the turn as of any (events 77002 and 77003), and its clock moves a turn's seconds.

- **Unaware is the engine's word, not the mod's.** The game layer asks the engine and hands core the answers (`Perception`, `src/core/Types.h`: a party member perceives it; it perceives a party member; it perceives anyone hostile to it; the game has it in combat), from the scripts' `IsPerceiving`, either way with each party member, `IsPerceivingHostiles`, the test the game's own scripts take a creature out of combat by, and `GetCombatState`. `Bridge::Scan` reads them of everyone as a turn begins, and `Bridge::Actor` of the acting creature every frame. Unaware is an enemy that the game does not have in combat and that perceives no party member and nobody else hostile to it (`Unaware`, `src/core/Rules.h`): all three, so that a turn is skipped only where the engine says on every count that there is nobody for it to fight. One that perceives an ally of the party's it is fighting is not unaware: it has someone to choose, and plays its turn. Nor is one the game has in combat, whatever its perception holds at that moment: the scripts run a creature's combat AI on a command's end only while it is in combat (`rules_core.nss`, `EVENT_TYPE_COMMAND_COMPLETE`), and set its combat state as it perceives a hostile, so one not in combat has nothing to choose when told; but one in combat with an AI of its own script's (a boss between two phases, the party gone from its view) may act on being told, and skipped it would stand for good. It is told as before, and at worst chooses nothing, as before. An ally's turn is never skipped so: nobody has asked for that.
- **It acts from the moment it notices.** Struck, it perceives its attacker (the game's own `rules_core.nss`), or the party comes into its perception as the world runs, or the game takes it into combat. If that is inside its beat, the frame's look says so and its AI is told, on a look at everyone taken with that step, so within a frame or two (`has noticed someone to fight: its turn is its AI's`); else its next turn begins as anyone's.
- **Nothing is asked of an AI that has nobody to choose.** A creature that perceives nobody to fight is never told to choose, at its turn's start or after a command of its own; so no turn waits `ai_think_ms` on it. A command of its own that comes through the gate in its beat (a script's order, a patrol's next step) is let through as on any turn of its own, and the turn is then not skipped under it; it ends `done` as that command settles. A wait of its script's in the beat is not a choice of its turn (its AI was not told): it is refused, neither a pass nor, with a turn a bar of time, taken of its bar as an AI's wait is (issue 31), and the turn still ends `unaware`.
- **Not mended by this, and as it was:** a creature not in combat that is damaged by one it does not perceive is told by the game's own script to walk to where its attacker stands (`rules_core.nss`, `EVENT_TYPE_DAMAGED`). That command comes in the attacker's turn and is held as any command out of turn is, so it stays where it is; struck with a weapon it perceives its attacker by the script before, and plays its next turn. And one the party perceives that is not in combat though it perceives a party member (one in stealth that passed its check) is told its turn has come as before, and chooses nothing.

**Leaving the fight is the engine's perception** (issue 29, built and seen in play 2026-10-04: two skeletons stayed in the fight with the party's leader walked more than 50 m off, "it and the party perceive each other"; three that had never noticed dropped out as the one who had seen them fell, "neither it nor the party perceives the other"; and a fight ended with the game's own end of combat while three unaware ones still stood). The game itself ends combat by perception, not by a distance: when a hostile drops out of a party member's perception its scripts ask whether the party still perceives any hostile (`IsPartyPerceivingHostiles`), and if not the game is back in explore mode three seconds later; a creature that is not the party's leaves combat when it perceives no hostile (`combat_h.nss`, `Combat_HandleCreatureDisappear`; `module_core.nss`, `EVENT_TYPE_DELAYED_GM_CHANGE`). The mod had a distance of its own both ways: a combatant further than `combat_radius` (50 m) from the party's leader was out of the fight four seconds later, and stood frozen where it was, as any creature outside the order does, whoever it was chasing. The user: "I think the 30 m is for pulling hostile enemies into the fight right at the beginning? Once they're in the fight I think they should stay in the fight for the distance that the game uses. The game allows you to run away and exit a fight. Whatever distance that is, that's the one we should use for fight exit, for consistency." So the 50 m takes nobody out of a fight any more. With each scan the machine names who is in the fight (`Needs::inFight`); the game layer reads those its own rules for who is fighting no longer find (out of combat, or beyond the radius: `Combatant::fighting` false) with the engine's perception between each and the party; and core decides (`StaysInTheFight`, `src/core/Rules.h`; `TurnMachine::Merge`):

- **an enemy stays while a party member perceives it or it perceives a party member** (`IsPerceiving`, either way): either keeps the two in one fight by the game's own reckoning. One the party perceives that has noticed nobody stays, unaware, its turns skipped (above);
- **an ally of the party's stays while it perceives someone hostile to it** (`IsPerceivingHostiles`), the test the game keeps it in combat by. Left to the radius, an ally that followed an enemy past it would stand frozen under the blows of an enemy that now stays in the fight;
- **one for which that no longer holds has left**, once it has been so for four seconds: one scan without it is not that. The log says `Genlock is no longer fighting: out of combat or out of range, and neither it nor the party perceives the other`. When the last enemy has so left, the fight is over (`no enemy left standing`), as it is when the game itself goes back to explore mode first (`the game ended combat`);
- **what brings a creature into a fight is unchanged**: in combat within `combat_radius` and fighting the party or seen within `join_radius`, or drawn in unawares. One those rules still find stays as it did, perceived or not, so that nobody's card goes and comes; and being perceived brings nobody in, nor back in.

So `combat_radius` is how far from its leader a fight takes a creature *in* from, and nothing else. One that stays by perception alone is said once, `Genlock is not found fighting near the party any more (out of combat, or further than 50 m from its leader), and stays in the fight: it and the party perceive each other` (or `the party perceives it`, `it perceives the party`, and for an ally `it perceives an enemy`).

One that left alive stays out, down in the order, until a scan finds it fighting again; its health alone does not bring it back. Back, it stops whatever it took up while it was out, as a joiner does (found by the random fights, 2026-10-04: one set walking by a conversation's scripts while out of the fight walked on through another's turn once it was back in). Before, the status read every frame said "alive" and brought it up again until the next scan put it down: in the user's eight fights a guard and a soldier were "up again" every three seconds for ten minutes, 311 times against 39 falls (read off Eluvian's bus), losing the turns that came while they were down.

## A shut door in the way (built 2026-10-01)

Told to attack something behind a shut door, the game ends the attack "path action required" and queues the door's use and the attack again. From close by that works: the door opens, the creature goes through and strikes. From 12 m it does not: the creature walks a few metres and stands at "use object" for good, the door shut. That is the game's own, seen the same with the mod's path check off, with turn-based off, and with `turnbased.dll` out of the game's folder (the ninth play test).

- **The player's attack with a door in the way is refused**, "A door is in the way", before a step is taken and with the action kept: the game's pathfinder is asked why there is no path (DAOLib's `Pathfind`, answer 6). Opening the door costs no action, so the player opens it and attacks. The close case the game would have managed is refused too: one rule the player can read.
- **A creature standing at a walk it does not take is stopped** after two and a half seconds, where `max_action_ms` is twelve of the world standing and the turn lost: at a move, or at the use of a door or the like, whose walk is a step of the command. On the player's turn the turn goes on, and an action that waited behind the walk and never began is given back; an AI's turn ends. A talent stands still while it is used and is not judged so.
- **An attack that stands still is judged by where the engine has its command** (issue 11, 2026-10-04). It swings or shoots standing, so standing alone says nothing. The game layer reads each frame which sub-action the attack's command is at (DAOLib's `dao::commands::RunningSubAction`: 4 for a blow's approach, 15 for a shot's; the machine's `ActorObservation::approaching`). One still at its approach that has not taken a step in a second of the wall's time (`kStuckMs`), counted for that command alone, cannot get to its target: others stand in the way, or its place on the target's ring is past a doorway (the player's character stood so at a stone golem, 2.6 m off, four times in one fight). The engine gives up only after ten seconds of the world's time, so the mod stops it: its action is given back, the player told ("No way through"), and an AI chooses again, once. One at its blow is never stopped by the mod, however long it stands: there it turns to face its target, draws, aims and strikes (an enemy archer's first shot of a turn takes four to six and a half seconds), and whether it will is the engine's to say. Until then the mod told the two apart by what was left of a walk it had reckoned itself, and that walk was wrong: see "An attack's reach and walk are the engine's own". A watch for "a swing in reach that does not begin", tried that day, stopped fourteen swings that were landing in one fight and was taken out.
- **These patiences are of the wall's time** (issue 16, 2026-10-03). The machine's own clock runs with the fight's speed, so that its own waits shorten with it; what it waits for the engine to do does not all shorten. A walk in the open goes 1.9 times as fast at twice the speed and 2.7 at three, but a creature working its way through a crowd creeps on at about the wall clock's pace at any speed. On the fast clock `max_action_ms` was four seconds of wall clock at three times the speed, and 122 of a night's 1,394 turns at Redcliffe ended "took too long" where none of 1,220 had at twice. So `max_action_ms` and the two standing-still limits are multiplied by the fight's speed on the machine's clock (`Patience`), never made shorter than given.

## The AI's turn

**An action that failed without acting is tried again, once.** A command that ends in a failure that did nothing (no path, no line of sight, no room at the target: `COMMAND_FAILED_NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING`, the engine's limit on how many may stand round one creature) gives its action back, and an AI is then told its turn has come once more, where its turn used to end 48 ms later with the action unused: an ally guard stood through a whole fight that way (the user, 2026-09-30, read off Eluvian's bus). Seen in play (the seventh play test, 2026-10-01, a staged crowd of seventeen): with its action back the game's own AI carries on by itself. A soldier with no room at its target at once tries to move round to it (`move to object`, which ends `COMMAND_FAILED_INVALID_PATH`, the crowd standing in the way), waits about a second, attacks again, fails again, and chooses to wait, which ends its turn. So the mod's telling it again is the fallback for an AI that queues nothing, and did not fire once in twenty such failures. That a creature with no room stands and waits is the engine's own rule, in real time too. Since issue 27 (2026-10-04) an attack the engine has no way to make when it is asked is given back before a step, and there the telling again is no fallback: nothing of its own was queued, so nothing ends to wake its AI ("What the engine answers no to is refused before a step", above; seen in play that day, three times of three). Given back twice in a turn, by a failure or before a step, an AI has nothing it can get to and its turn ends. Its second attack on the same target after a failure is put to the engine like any other: a rule that refused it unasked was tried and withdrawn (the same section).

**The player is told why, over the character and on the bar's caption** (issue 21, 2026-10-03; built and tested without the game, not yet seen in play). A party member's order that the engine refuses once it is under way is given back the same way, and the turn goes on. In real time the game shuffles a blocked fighter round or the player sees the character stand; in turns each try is a decision. In the playthrough (issue 3) Sten's attack was refused eighteen times in one fight at a doorway, six of his turns ending with nothing spent, and Morrigan's spell thirteen times at a shut door, with only a floaty over the character to say why, a moment's word that a player may miss in a fight and a tool that drives the game does not see, and the same words ("No way through") for a door, for no room and for no path. The words are now by the engine's reason (`src/core/GivenBack.h`): "A door is in the way" (`COMMAND_FAILED_PATH_ACTION_REQUIRED`), "No room at the target" (`..._NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING`), "No way through" (`..._INVALID_PATH`, and an attack stopped standing short of its target), "No line of sight" (`..._NO_LINE_OF_SIGHT`), "Nothing happened" (`..._INVALID_DATA`, and an action over at once with nothing done). They float over the character in gold as before, and the bar's caption has the whole line in gold, "No room at the target: Sten's attack is given back", in place of what the turn has left, for five seconds of the real clock (the world stands still while the player reads) or until the player's next order is let through. `turnbased.state` has the line as `notice` while it shows, for a tool that drives the game. Not said for a creature whose AI plays its turn: it is told to choose again. Where the engine can be asked beforehand and answers no (no place, no path, no line for a shot), the order is refused before a step in the same words, and the caption says "is refused" (issue 27; "What the engine answers no to is refused before a step", above).

The game's AI is event-driven (DAOLib's `dev/TURN_BASED.md`). When a creature's command completes, the engine sends it `EVENT_TYPE_COMMAND_COMPLETE`; `rules_core.nss` runs `AI_DetermineCombatRound`, which picks the next command and queues it with `AddCommand`. There is no round. The mod turns that loop on and off:

- **Off-turn**, every command a combatant chooses is refused at the queue (`src/game/Gate.cpp`). A creature with nothing queued gets no `COMMAND_COMPLETE`, so its AI stops choosing, and nothing it does costs anything.
- **Its turn** starts with the mod sending it a `COMMAND_COMPLETE` of its own (a wait that succeeded), processed at once. The creature's own script sees it first, then `rules_core`, then its AI: custom boss AI included, which is why this is the default rather than the scripts' `EVENT_TYPE_DEBUG_KICKSTART_AI` (`turns.ai_poke`). Its command comes back through the queue, is allowed, and runs. Not a hostile that perceives nobody to fight: it is sent nothing, and its turn is skipped ("An unaware hostile's turn").
- When that command completes, the AI chooses again. A move is allowed while movement is left; a second action is refused, and a wait is the AI passing (with a turn a bar of time it costs the time it waits, and the AI chooses again: below). After `turns.ai_follow_up_ms` with nothing more, the turn ends.
- An AI that chooses nothing within `turns.ai_think_ms` passes. More than `turns.max_commands_per_turn` commands in a turn ends it, which guards against a loop.

**An AI's wait costs the time it waits** (issue 31; with a turn a bar of time; built and seen in play 2026-10-04: against the high dragon above Haven the gate read 1,000 ms from the wait's own parameter, 93 waits were paid, and of the dragon's 62 turns 35 waited first and then acted and none was lost to a wait alone, where 18 of 77 had done nothing; the 7 that still did nothing were its AI choosing nothing at all, issue 35). The game's AI waits between two things it does, `CommandWait(AI_DO_NOTHING_DELAY)`, 2 s, or the quick one, 1 s (`ai_main_h_2.nss`, `ai_constants_h.nss`), and chooses again as the wait ends. Taken for a pass, a wait ended the turn with the rest of its bar unspent: a creature with a bar of 7 s that waited 2 lost all 7, and in the playthrough (issue 3) the high dragon above Haven ended 20 of its 77 turns so, 5 to 7 s left each time. The user, on reading that: "Shouldn't this cost the time equal to how long it waits? If it's less than the amount of time it has to take actions in a turn, it can still take more actions". So a wait takes its own length of its creature's bar, as an action takes its time, and its creature's own clock moves by it (its cooldowns, the effects on it). With time left its AI is told to choose again at once, by the event the engine itself sends as a wait ends (the mod's `COMMAND_COMPLETE` of a wait that succeeded, above). A wait longer than what is left takes nothing and ends the turn as every wait did, what is left rolling over by the usual rule. The wait itself is still refused at the queue: there is nothing of it to see, and the world does not run two seconds for a creature standing. **Its length is the engine's own number**, read from the command before it would run (`src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WaitSecondsOf`): `CommandWait` keeps its seconds as milliseconds in the command's first integer (DAOLib's `dao::Command::WaitMilliseconds`, read from the script command's constructor, and seen in play in DAOLib's example plugin's log, `wait 2000 ms` for an AI's wait). One of no length, or whose length could not be read, would cost nothing and could be chosen for ever: it ends the turn as before. A creature with nothing to do waits again and again and has no rule of its own: its bar bounds it (a bar of 5 s is two waits of 2 s, and the third ends the turn), and so does `max_commands_per_turn`, a turn's waits counted with its commands. A turn the player plays is not touched (its character's own AI's wait is refused it: "The party's turn"), nor is a turn of one action and its meters (`clock = actions`), where a wait is still the pass. The log says `Hurlock waits 2.00 s; 3.00 s and 15.0 m left`, then `Hurlock chooses again: 3.00 s and 15.0 m left`; for one that does not fit, `Hurlock's wait of 2.00 s is longer than the 1.00 s it has left: its turn ends` and `Hurlock's turn ends: waits; 1.00 s rolls over`.

## The party's turn

Only the player's orders spend a party member's turn. A controlled character's own AI carries on attacking by itself in real time (`AI_DetermineCombatRound_Partial`); on the player's turns its attacks and moves are refused, so the party's AI is off in turn-based fights except under `party = tactics` (the user, 2026-09-30). Its weapons and its modes are refused it too, free as they are: a follower that is not the controlled one goes to melee weapons with an enemy at hand and turns its bow's mode off for it (`AI_DetermineCombatRound`; the controlled one's partial AI never does), and a turn that opens with a kept action landing runs that full AI before its character is selected, so that in the playthrough of issue 3 a rogue and a warrior left their bows for good, unasked (issue 4, 2026-10-02). The player's own change of weapons, by the button or the key, is free as ever. Its wait is refused it as well: with no enemy left to choose, that full AI queues a wait of a second and another as each ends (`AI_ExecuteDefaultAction`, `_AI_DoNothing`; the controlled one's never waits), and let through they held a turn at its command for good. A turn opened by a kept shot that felled the fight's last enemy stood eleven minutes so, never handed to the player, End doing nothing (issue 10, the Tower of Ishal, 2026-10-03).

The mod selects the party member (`SetPrimaryControlled`) and waits, paused. An order arrives at the queue as the base game gives it, is checked against the turn's action and movement, and runs. Whether the engine queues an order given while paused at once, or only once the game runs, has not been seen. So the mod also watches explore mode's order handlers: if an order comes and no command follows within a tenth of a second, it runs the world for up to half a second, everyone else still held, until the command arrives. When the character is idle again, the world settles for `turns.settle_ms` (arrows land, bodies fall) and pauses for the next order. A controlled character's automatic "keep attacking" is a second action and is refused.

With `turns.party = tactics`, the companions play their turns through their tactics like the AI, and only the hero waits for the player. The hero is kept selected on the companions' turns, since the game gives a controlled character only its "keep attacking" AI (`AI_DetermineCombatRound_Partial`).

## What stays the engine's

Each of the game's 43 command types (its `commands.gda`, as DAOLib names them) has a cost (`src/core/Rules.cpp`, `Classify`):

| Cost | Types |
|---|---|
| The action, a combat action | attack, use ability (a talent, a spell), use item (a potion), fly |
| Movement | move to a point or an object, move through points, move away, follow, random walk, the player steering |
| Free, on one's own turn | use object (a door, a chest, a lever), interact, lock, unlock, give, take, pick up; switch or draw weapons, equip, unequip, drop, turn to face |
| A pass, from a script | wait: an AI choosing to do nothing ends its turn; with a turn a bar of time it costs the time it waits, and ends the turn only when that is more than is left ("The AI's turn") |
| Always let through | an effect executing, a death blow, jumps, barks, animations, sounds, conversations, appearing and vanishing, shops, and a wait nobody chose (the scripts' `SetActive` queues one) |

The last row is what happens to a creature, not what it chooses, and goes through at any time. A creature knocked down on its turn gets up first, then acts.

Whose a command is comes from the command itself: the player's orders carry a flag (`Command::PlayerIssued`), a script's come through `AddCommand`, and the rest are the engine's (DAOLib's reading of the queue's ten callers). A refused command is freed by the mod, since the queue always takes what it is given and no caller frees it after.

**Conversations and cutscenes** mid-fight (a surrender, a boss's speech) stand the mod aside. Their scripts move people about, so nothing is refused while they play. When combat resumes, whatever anyone was left doing stops, and the current turn goes on with what it had left: its action spent or not, its movement, and no second turn-start event.

**Menus** (inventory, character sheet) over the fight hold everything as it is. The menu pauses the game itself, and the mod picks up where it was after.

**The game's auto-pause** on combat is a script (`module_core.nss`, `EVENT_TYPE_AUTOPAUSE`) that toggles pause half a second after combat starts. It would unpause a turn-based fight, so the script command is ignored during one.

**Loading** a save or an area drops any fight without touching its objects, since they are gone.

## Turning it on and off

Turn-based combat is turned on or off only outside combat ("can't change during battle"), by the box in the game's Options or by a key. The answer floats over the hero and is written to `turnbased.ini`. The key was `Ctrl+T` until 2026-10-03; it has none now until the player binds one (the user: "I don't think we necessarily need one for toggling turn-based on / off. There's already a checkbox in game settings", and of its row kept in the controls with no key, "Yeah we can keep it unbound I guess"). The key is one of the game's own since 2026-10-01 (DAOLib's `dao/Input.h`): listed in the options' Controls tab under "Turn-based" as "Toggle turn-based combat", bound there like any key, and kept in the game's `KeyBindings.ini` (`TurnBasedToggle_0`). `[keys] toggle` is the key it has until the player binds another. A key the game's bindings are not known to take (the number pad, Pause, a key by its code) is polled as before and is not in the controls. The rest of the settings are in that file, which is re-read when it changes. The switch itself is in the game's Options too: a "Turn-based" section on the Game tab, headed by the game's own gold bar, with a box, "Turn-based combat", the user's call of 2026-10-01 ("not controls, gameplay tab i think, its own section w/ a toggle"), through DAOLib's `dao/Options.h`. The box is the game's own checkbox, ticked by what the setting is when the screen opens; a click changes the setting at once (not at the screen's OK) and writes `turnbased.ini`, as the key does; in a fight the click is refused, the box goes back, and its tooltip says "Changes outside a fight only".

## Also needed, and what turn-based players will expect

Filling in what a turn-based mode usually has, by how much it matters:

- **An end-turn control.** Built as a button on the turn bar and a key of its own (Enter); the pause key, which was it at first, pauses again.
- **Knowing it is your turn.** Built as floating text and selection. The bar and a sound would be better; the game's GUI sounds are reachable from a movie (`FSCommand:PlayGUISound`).
- **Seeing what is left.** Action and movement remaining, under the bar or on the portrait. The data is in `TurnView`.
- **The camera going to whoever acts.** `camera_follow`, through DAOLib's camera (`src/game/Camera.cpp`, `src/core/Easing.h`). In the tactical view it pans to whoever acts, eased, and never turns: only the player turns it. Close in, it stays with the player's character through others' turns (following an enemy put the camera 3.8 m behind it) and comes round behind each party member as their turn begins. Not yet seen in play.
- **The fight's speed** (`[turns] speed`, 1; 0.25 to 4; the Fight speed slider in the game's options, whose stops are 0.5, 1, 2 and 3). A big fight is long when every creature animates in full: the game runs faster or slower through a turn-based fight, everyone's turns alike, through DAOLib's `dao::clock::SetSpeed` (the world's timer and the frame's, so logic, animation and sound together; the Flash UI keeps its own clock). Conversations and everything outside a fight run at 1. The mod writes the speed only when it changes, so at 1 never. Built 2026-09-30 as `ai_turn_speed`, for an AI's turns alone, as BG3 speeds enemies' turns up; the user, 2026-10-02: "shouldn't turn speed apply to everyone? we have one setting for combat speed right?", and that setting is gone.
- **Delay.** Built 2026-10-02 and kept by the user: a party member puts its turn off to the round's end, before it has spent anything, once a round; the turn it then takes is whole (`dev/REBALANCE.md`). A button above End on the turn bar. Since 2026-10-03 it has a key of the game's own too, as End turn has ("Delay turn" in the controls under Turn-based, `[keys] delay`), with none until the player binds one (issue 18).
- **Surprise.** A party that starts a fight from stealth might get a free round, as in D&D; enemies who ambush likewise. Stealth is readable from the scripts.
- **Opportunity attacks.** Walking away from an adjacent enemy provokes a free attack in most turn-based games. Without them, melee creatures cannot hold anyone and archers kite freely. DAO has melee rings (`GetCreaturesInMeleeRing`) to detect leaving; the free attack is an `AddCommand` out of turn the gate lets through. A good second step.
- **Sustained abilities and potions.** DAO's modal abilities (toggled on, draining mana or stamina) cost the action as built; most turn-based designs make a toggle or a potion a bonus action. That wants the ability's type from its data table (`abi_base`, readable through `dao::gda`) and a second, lesser action in the budget.
- **Cooldowns and durations in rounds.** See Time above.
- **Balance.** DAO is tuned for real time: some fights throw many weak enemies, and regeneration assumes seconds pass. Expect `movement.meters`, `round_min_seconds` and initiative to want tuning in play.

## Proposed, not decided (2026-10-01)

Two questions put to the user and not yet answered. Nothing here is built unless it says so.

### Casting time (decided and built 2026-10-01)

The user's rule: "the ritual spells which take 6 s should charge for 2 turns, since 1 turn is 4 s. cast on the 3rd turn. instant & fast & normal can cast on the turn of. slow spells take 1 turn to charge."

So an ability whose conjuring time is a turn's worth or more (`cooldown_seconds_per_turn`, 4 s) charges that many of its caster's turns, rounded up, the turn it is ordered on the first, and is cast at the start of the turn after: slow (4 s) one turn and cast on the second, ritual (6 s) two and on the third. Less than a turn's worth (instant, fast 1 s, normal 2 s) is cast on the turn, as before.

- **Begun:** the action is spent and the turn ends; "Casting" over the caster. The command is not queued: the game layer keeps what it was (the ability, its target, its place).
- **A charging turn** (a ritual's second): spent on the charge, nothing to order.
- **Cast:** as its caster's turn begins the kept cast is given to it again, with a short conjuring (the turns were its time), for nothing: the caster then has its turn, its action its own. It goes on cooldown as any ability used.
- **Interrupted:** a caster held (a stun, a knockdown, paralysis, sleep), fallen or gone before then loses the charge, "Interrupted", nothing spent and nothing on cooldown; the fight's end loses it too. Damage alone does not: what the engine does in real time has not been read. This and the caster keeping its turn were not the user's words, and are theirs to change.
- **Enemies too:** an enemy's slow spell is said a turn ahead, and stunning the caster stops it.
- **Its target gone:** a cast whose target fell while it charged is not given: nothing spent, nothing on cooldown, and the turn goes on. In play it was cast at nothing and put on cooldown (the twelfth play test). It is logged and not shown: "Its target is gone" floated over the character until 2026-10-03, eight to twelve times a fight with a turn a bar of time, where most turns end on a kept attack; nothing is lost when it happens, so the player is not told (the user, issue 8).

## Action points (being designed, 2026-10-01; not built)

The one-action rule is a budget of one. Action points make it a budget of several, spent on things of different cost. The user's sketch, 2026-10-01, in their words: "each turn an actor should get N base action points. not sure what N should be. maybe it should represent 1s, so 4? this doesn't mean they get to attack 4x or use 4 talents. time to use ability should affects action point consumption. longer cast time = more action points. 1s cast = 1 action point, 4 s = 4 action points. i think action points should allow you to do different things. using a less health poulstice takes an action point. larger ones take more. so you can use health poulstice, drink potion, set trap, etc. and all that takes action points. attacking takes an action point; can't attack again same turn (unless maybe we allow multi-attack w/ a talent or something). talents take action points and lock out other skills so you can't spam multiple skills in a turn. don't want to force end turn after attack / talent, so things like drink potion can come after, though that would be easiest. still needs further thinking an refinement."

**What is settled by that** (the user's, unless marked):

- **4 points a turn, a point a second.** It is the number the rest already stands on: a turn is 4 s for cooldowns (`cooldown_seconds_per_turn`) and for casting time, so a slow spell (4 s) is a whole turn's points and a ritual (6 s) more than a turn's. Charging (above) is then not a rule of its own but a cost greater than a turn.
- **A cost by time.** An ability costs its conjuring time in seconds, at least 1.
- **One talent or spell a turn**: using one locks the others out for the turn, so cooldowns in turns keep their meaning. Proposed with it: sustained modes are outside the lockout, a point to turn on and nothing to turn off (they cost nothing either way today).
- **Items cost points, more for the greater**: a lesser poultice 1, larger ones more; traps, bombs and the like their own.
- **The turn is not ended by an attack or a talent**: what points are left may still be spent (a potion after the blow). The turn ends when its owner ends it, or when neither points nor movement are left.
- **Movement stays its own budget**, in meters, as now (proposed; the sketch does not fold it into the points).

**What the game's data gives for costs** (read with DAOLib's `tools/gda.py`):

- Abilities: the conjuring speeds in use are instant (0 s), fast (1 s) and normal (2 s): `abi_base`'s `Speed` is 0 for 177 rows, 1 for 330, 2 for 37, 3 for 7. So by time alone nearly every talent and spell is 1 point and a few are 2: casting time gives little to choose between them.
- Weapons: the item table (`bitm_base`) has a `Speed` class: dagger 3; longsword, mace, waraxe 2; greatsword, maul, battleaxe, staff 1; bows 0. It is readable in play as the weapon's range already is (`Table::Items`, read in the gate for an attack's reach), so a cost by weapon needs nothing new of DAOLib.
- Items: the game has no "time" for a poultice or a trap, so their costs would be a small table in `turnbased.ini` with defaults.

**Proposed to the user, and theirs to decide:**

1. **An attack's cost from its weapon's speed, and attacks repeatable while points last**: say a dagger 1, a one-handed weapon 2, a two-handed weapon, staff or bow 3. A flat "one attack, one point" has a greatsword strike as often as a dagger for far more damage, which takes away the game's balance of fast against heavy, and leaves a fighter whose talents are on cooldown spending one point of four. By weapon, a turn of plain attacks is four dagger blows, two sword blows or one of a greatsword: near what four seconds give in real time.
2. **If attacks stay one a turn, something to spend the rest on**: a dash, 1 point for a quarter more movement.
3. **Doors, chests and pickups 1 point.** They are free today only because the other choice was the whole turn.
4. **Haste and slow as a point more or fewer**: they are effects on time.
5. **No banking**: points not spent are lost at the turn's end, to begin with.
6. **Enemies on the same budget**: an AI is told to choose again after each command until its points are gone or it passes. Enemy turns get longer in real time; `ai_turn_speed` is the offset.
7. **Shown**: the points on the turn bar's caption beside the movement, and refusals that give the numbers ("needs 2, 1 left").

**Asked of the user, 2026-10-01, and not yet answered:** attacks one a turn at 1 point, or by weapon speed and repeatable; doors and chests 1 point or free; a dash or not.

**How it would be built**, in two stages: first the budget (`TurnMachine::Budget`'s `actions` becomes points), the costs (`Classify` answers a cost with its kind; the gate gives the machine the weapon's speed and the ability's conjuring time, as it gives the latter now), the one-talent lockout, the AI told again, and the caption; then the items' table, the dash and haste. The gate's hooks and the rest stay as they are.
