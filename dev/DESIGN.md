# Turn-based combat: the design

Dragon Age: Origins fights in real time with pause. This mod makes a fight a sequence of turns: everyone in it acts one at a time, in an order rolled when the fight begins, and the world waits between actions. Outside fights nothing changes. This page is what the mod does and why, for the code (`src/core/TurnMachine.cpp` implements it phase by phase) and for someone who has not played many turn-based games.

**What a turn is.** A turn is a bar of time: 4 s (`[turns] seconds_per_turn`) that actions and walking both spend, each at its own length. An attack costs its swing, a spell its conjuring and cast, a walk its meters at the creature's pace, a door or a chest half a second; a dagger strikes more often than a greatsword. What does not fit what is left is begun now and lands on the creature's next turn, which pays the rest first. A little of what a turn's actor leaves unspent rolls over to its next. A cooldown, an effect's duration and a condition's hold count in the same seconds: a turn of a creature's is 4 s of its own clock, a round 4 s of the one everyone shares. `dev/REBALANCE.md`'s "Current specification" has these rules and their reasons, and `dev/REBALANCE_IMPLEMENTATION.md` what is built of them; this page is the rest: who fights, the order, the rounds, what holds the world, what the player sees.

There is one clock. Until issue 50 (2026-10-05) there was a second, `[turns] clock = actions`, the mod's first rule: one action and an allowance of meters a turn, with cooldowns, conditions and casts counted in whole turns. Every fight since play test 25 was played on the bar, and the user took the other out: "we should get rid of that since we've replaced that now and are not using it". Its settings went with it (`[turns] clock`, `end_turn_when_action_used`, `cooldown_seconds_per_turn`, `effect_seconds_per_round`; `[movement] rule`, `meters`, `per_dexterity`, `min`, `max`): a file that still has one is told `unknown setting` in the log, as for any key the mod does not know, and plays on the bar. Where this page still tells how a rule was first built in counted turns, that is its history; what is so now is the bar.

## The words

- **Combatant**: a creature in the fight. Party members, allies that are not in the party (soldiers at Ostagar), enemies.
- **Initiative**: a number rolled for each combatant when the fight starts. Highest acts first. The order is fixed for the fight; newcomers are slotted in.
- **Turn**: one combatant acting. It has a **bar** of time to spend on **actions** and **movement**, then passes the turn on.
- **Round**: everyone's turn, once. Round 2 starts when the last combatant in the order has had its turn.
- **Bar**: a turn's time, 4 s, a little more with Combat Tactics and with what rolled over from the turn before.
- **Action**: what a turn is for: an attack, an ability or spell, drinking a potion. Each costs the time the game takes for it, and a turn holds as many as fit its bar. Opening a door or a chest, picking something up and the like cost half a second (`[costs] object`).
- **Movement**: meters a combatant walks in its turn, each paid for in time at its pace as it goes, before or after an action or between two. The first meter of a turn is free.
- **Free**: costs nothing (switching weapon sets, a mode turned on or off).
- **End turn**: giving up what is left: the turn bar's End, or its key (Enter until bound in the game's controls, under Turn-based). The game's pause key did it until 2026-10-02; it pauses now, as it does everywhere else in the game (the user: "space bar should still pause the game i think, instead of end turn. that's what players would expect"). The machine holds the game's pause through a fight, so it takes the key and pauses for the player; an order given while paused runs the game again, since its action has to play out. End pressed while an action of the turn is still running stops that action and ends the turn at once (issue 51: "No clock on the player's turn", under "The party's turn").

## What the player sees

1. A fight starts (the game switches to combat mode). The world freezes, everyone's initiative is rolled, and the order is logged. "Turn-based combat" floats over the hero (over another of the party standing, where a fight begins with her down).
2. **A party member's turn.** The game stays paused and that character is selected ("Alistair's turn" in gold over them). The player gives an order exactly as in the base game: click an enemy, pick an ability, click the ground. The game unpauses while it plays out, then pauses again. The player can move and act in any order until the bar is spent, or end the turn early (End, or its key). The turn waits for as long as the player likes: no clock of the mod's ends a turn the player plays ("No clock on the player's turn", under "The party's turn"). Ordering anyone else gets "Not Morrigan's turn".
3. **An AI combatant's turn** (enemies, allies, and companions whose tactics play their turns: "The party's turn"). Its name floats over it (red for enemies), the game runs, its AI chooses and acts, and the turn passes. Nothing else moves meanwhile. An enemy that has not noticed the party has "Unaware" over it instead, for a second, and its turn is skipped: the party has the initiative until it notices ("Who is in the fight", below).
4. A combatant who falls loses its turns. Fallen enemies leave the order at the end of the round; fallen party members stay in it, marked down, since they can be revived. A creature that joins mid-fight (reinforcements) stops whatever it was doing and waits for its first turn: this round if its initiative place is still to come, next round if it has passed.
5. When no enemy is left standing, the fight ends, the game unpauses, and real time resumes, the hero selected again (the last turn's companion stayed selected in play, 2026-09-30). The game then leaves combat mode on its own, as it always does. A fight the party runs from ends as the game's own does: when neither side perceives the other any more ("Who is in the fight", below).

## Movement: how far

A creature walks as far as its time takes it: **a second of its bar walks a person's 4.66 m (`[turns] pace`) times how fast the creature moves against a person**, so what the game already treats as speed matters, and a meter walked is time not spent on something else. A whole turn of 4 s walks a person 18.6 m, and the first meter of every turn is free on top (`[turns] free_step`), for shuffling in a melee ring. The pace comes from the creature's appearance: its combat speed in the appearance table (`Game_CbtSpeed`) over a person's 4.66 m/s. People, genlocks and hurlocks are at 1, a mabari 1.07, a shriek or werewolf 1.58, a dragon 1.47, an ogre 0.84. Haste and slows multiply it ("Haste and slows", below). The scripts' movement speed property is no use: it is the flanking angle in the shipped table (the first session in play, 2026-09-30).

**Haste and slows** (issue 49, built 2026-10-05; not yet seen in play). The pace is the creature's build times its movement rate effects **as they are now**: what a second of the bar walks is `[turns] pace` times that, and what is left to walk is the time left times the pace now. The effects are the game's own, `EFFECT_TYPE_MOVEMENT_RATE` (9) and `EFFECT_TYPE_MOVEMENT_RATE_DEBUFF` (11), each with a potency that is a plain rate: Haste 1.3 (on the whole party while the mode is on), a Swift Salve 1.2, a Rock Salve 0.6; a caltrop trap 0.6, grease 0.5, Winter's Grasp 0.8, Cone of Cold 0.6, Weakness 0.8, a pinning shot 0.5 (the scripts' constants). They multiply, every one of both types, which is the engine's own sum: as an effect is put on or taken off it sets the creature's rate to the product of the potencies of all of them on it (read in the executable, `src/core/Rules.h`, `MovementRate`). So Haste with a Swift Salve is 1.56, and Haste in a caltrop trap 0.78. A hasted person walks 6.06 m for each second of the bar; in grease 2.33.

- **The bar stays as long as it was, and what is hasted is what a thing costs of it** (decided: the user, 2026-10-05, asked whether a speed-up should give more time or make moves cost less: "keeping the bar constant and reducing the cost makes sense. I think that also fits better mechanically"). The bar is the same length for everyone, and what an action costs is what the game makes it take. So Haste shortens a swing and a walk and leaves a spell's conjuring as it is, as in the game; a slow is the same rule the other way. A longer bar would have sped everything alike, a conjuring with a walk.
- **The pace is the creature's as it is now, read again through its turn** (decided: the user, 2026-10-05, of reading it again within the turn where it had been read once as the turn began: "I think that would be more accurate"). The machine reads everyone's Haste and slows with its look at everyone, which it takes as every turn begins, as each action ends and before the turn goes on, and every half second between. So a Swift Salve drunk in the middle of a turn lengthens what can still be walked in that turn; Haste turned on by another takes in the hasted one's turn under way, at the next look; and a creature that walks into caltrops pays the slow from the look that finds it, half a second into the trap at the latest, what it walked until then charged at the pace till then. Nothing is given back and nothing is taken: the time already spent stays spent, and the meters of an attack's walk, paid for as the attack was let through, stay paid for whatever they would cost now. A walk judged to fit that no longer does is cut where its time runs out, as any walk past its bar is. The bar's caption, the path's preview, what an order would cost and "Too far" all go by this one pace: each reads the turn's own budget, and none keeps a pace of its own.
- **What it is not.** The attack speed half of Haste and of the salve is a swing's cost, not this ("Time").
- **Why it was not so until then.** The scripts write the potency with `SetEffectEngineFloat`, into a second list of parameters each effect carries for the engine, and no script command reads that list: `GetEffectFloat`, which the mod asked, reads the scripts' own list, empty for these effects, and answered 0. A potency of 0 is no rate and is left out, so every creature moved at its build's pace whatever was on it, in every fight, and the line that would have said otherwise was never written. The mod now reads the engine's list (`src/game/Bridge.cpp`, `EngineParameters`), where the engine itself reads it. `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md` asks DAOLib for that read, and for the rate the engine keeps on the creature.

Movement is measured, not predicted: the distance walked while the creature is moving under its own command, frame by frame, teleports ignored. When the budget runs out mid-walk, the creature is stopped and "Out of movement" floats over it. If it was walking to an attack that it never reached, the action is given back, once a turn: it may still attack something in reach. An AI creature that runs out ends its turn.

**A player's attack that cannot reach its target this turn is refused before a step is taken**: "Too far" floats over the attacker, and the action is not spent, as BG3 refuses a red target. Whether it reaches, and how far its creature would walk first, is the engine's own answer, asked for the pair as they stand ("An attack's reach and walk are the engine's own", below). It is asked only when the order reaches the queue on the scripts' thread, where script commands may be called and which in play is the game's own; the log says which the first time.

**An attack's reach and walk are the engine's own** (issue 11, 2026-10-04; `src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WalkToTarget`; DAOLib's `dao::attack`, its issue 16). The mod keeps no rule of its own for either. The user's direction that day, after the mod's own rule had been wrong: "We should invoke the engine functions where necessary. If we absolutely can't, we need to decompile the code and replicate the logic ourselves very carefully." The attack command queues an approach and then the blow, and the approach decides on its first run whether there is anything to walk. For a blow the target gives the attacker a place on its melee ring, and the attacker is in reach when the centres are at most both personal spaces and 1.2 m apart and it stands within 1.25 m of that place; else it walks to the place. For a shot (a bow, a crossbow, a staff) it is in range when the distance on the ground is at most the weapon's range, 60 m at most, and at least both personal spaces, and it has a line of sight; else it walks toward the target until that holds. The gate asks the engine those questions through DAOLib (`ApproachOf`: in reach or not, where the approach goes and how near it stops; `Walk`: the engine's path there, its length) and charges and refuses by the answers: in reach there is nothing to walk, nothing to pay for a walk and nothing to refuse. The movement preview shows the same number. Where the engine's path must not be searched (its creature is at a command already) the walk is the line to where the approach goes; a shot in range with no line of sight walks for one, and how far is not known before it has. Until then the mod had a sum of its own, written from notes of the exe: the path's length to the target less a reach it worked out. The path goes round whoever and whatever stands between, and the engine does not go by it. On the highway where Zevran lies in wait the archers stand on slopes above the road, 20 m from their targets and 75 m by the path: each was told it had 30 m to walk, shot from where it stood, and was stopped a second into a shot of four to six as an attacker "standing short of where its walk was to end", 67 times in one fight; the party's own mage was refused "Too far" at 15, 19 and 25 m with a staff that reaches 45. In melee the same: a skeleton 1.8 m from its target, 3.1 m by the path round the others, stopped four times in one turn a second into a swing of two and a half. The notes were wrong besides: the personal spaces are not added to a shot's range, and a blow's reach asks for the ring's place too.

**What the engine answers no to is refused before a step** (issue 27, 2026-10-04; `src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WalkToTarget`; `TurnMachine::NoAttackToMake`; an AI's seen in play that day, the player's and the no-path and no-line answers not yet). Both of those questions have a "no" among their answers, and until then the gate went on past it with a guess of its own, which is what the user's rule forbids: where the engine itself decides, ask it and act on its answer, and put no figure of the mod's in its place. The game layer now tells the machine which no it had (`CommandRequest::noAttack`), and the machine refuses the attack before it is let through, nothing spent:

- **No room at the target.** A blow needs a place on its target's melee ring, and whether the engine's attack will find one is asked of the engine through DAOLib ("Whether the attack will find room is the engine's own answer too", below). No room (the approach's `findsRoom` false) is one of two things. The ring as it stands has no place for the attacker, and the engine's approach fails at its first run. Or the first ask would have a place and the attack would still find none: it does not strike in its first update, and the ring once filled has no room. The first, the gate used to let through as "the engine has no approach for it; its walk is not known", and the engine failed it within two milliseconds, `COMMAND_FAILED_NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING`: 6 of 6 times in the caverns above Haven, dragonlings swarming one party member. The second nobody could tell beforehand until DAOLib found how the ring works. (`ApproachOf` answers nothing only where it was not asked, the two not creatures both: an attack on a crate or a door, let through as ever with its walk not known.)
- **No path.** Out of reach, the path searched (its creature at rest) and `Walk` answers nothing, with no door in the way: the engine has no path to where its approach goes. The gate used to say "the game finds no path to it (1); judged by the straight line", reckon a walk by that line and let it through, and the engine failed it four tenths of a second on, `COMMAND_FAILED_INVALID_PATH`: 15 times for one party member who stood through four fights on the forest road at a spot the pathfinder found no way from, and 4 more of his orders were refused "Too far" by the reckoned walk, the wrong reason. Why he had no path from there has not been read; the log now has the search's own account of itself beside the answer.
- **No line for a shot.** The same answer from `Walk` for a bow or a staff whose path to its target does arrive: the engine would walk its archer toward the target for a line of sight, find none from anywhere along the way, and give the shot up beside it (DAOLib follows the move update's own tests along the path). Not in the issue's evidence, and rare by what it takes (no line even from beside the target: one on a ledge above, say); refused by the same rule, in its own words.

The player's own order is told why, in the words the failure under way would have had: "No room at the target", "No way through", "No line of sight" over the character in grey, as "Too far" is, the `refused` event with the engine's answer as its note, and on the bar's caption for five seconds "No room at the target: Sten's attack is refused" (refused, not given back: nothing was taken). An AI's is given back before a step, and what it does then is its own, the mod not choosing for it: it is told to choose again, once, as after a failure under way, and a second action given back in the turn, before a step or under way, ends it, "nothing it can get to" ("The AI's turn", below), with nothing of its bar rolled over: the mod ended that turn for it, and time rolls over only from a turn its actor ended (issue 29; "Time rolls over only from a turn its actor ended", below). A hostile that has noticed nobody to fight is not told its turn has come (issue 29), so it queues no attack to refuse. What its own script queues in its beat while the game still has it out of combat is the game's and is not asked about at all (issue 43; "A creature the game does not have in combat has no turn", below). One the game has taken into combat since the machine last looked at it, its beat still on, that the engine has no way to make is refused and no more: not counted as given back, and its AI not told again before the look has said it noticed. It is told by the next step, as issue 29 has it, and its choice is then asked of the engine like any. Seen in play (2026-10-04: twelve dragonlings staged in a ring round one party member, 49 of their turns): three attacks given back so, each followed by `Dragonling chooses again, its action given back`, with no attack let through and no failure of the engine's between. Not yet seen: the player's refusal on screen (in five fights no order of the party's met a ring the question called full, and none was refused wrongly), the no-path and no-line answers, and the refusal by the fuller question the gate asks since (`findsRoom`, below).

**Every attack is put to the engine afresh, and nothing of a no is remembered.** The engine's no is for where the two stood when it was asked; once its creature's script has moved it, the engine is asked again. The first build of this kept a memory beside the question: a target the engine had refused, or whose attack it had failed under way for want of a place or a path, was "ruled out for the rest of its turn", and an AI's next attack on it that turn was refused unasked. It was built, tried in the staged fight and withdrawn the same day: it was the mod's own reckoning standing in for the engine's answer, the very thing this rule is against, and wrong about four times in ten. The game's own AI answers a failed attack with a move closer and the same target again (`ai_main_h_2.nss`, `AI_ExecuteAttack`), and that second attack is often good: in three logs from before the rule, 17 turns had a failure for want of a place, and in 7 of them a later attack on the same target that turn went through and did not fail (let through, failed, moved, let through, done). The rule refused exactly those. The commonest failing turn of the staged fight, 9 of 49, read: let through; failed 45 to 180 ms on, at the blow; `move to object`; the same target "given back before a step (ruled out this turn)"; `turn ends: nothing it can get to`, with the gate's own line just above the refusal saying "in its reach by the engine's melee approach: nothing to walk". Nor did it get an AI onto another target: its script chooses by threat, and after a rule-out it chose the same one 13 times of 16. What is kept of it is the failed command's target, passed to the machine for the log's line alone (`Dragonling's attack on Lyna failed (COMMAND_FAILED_NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING): its action is given back`; right in each of 18 such lines of that fight): in a crowd the names do not say which attack on whom.

**Whether the attack will find room is the engine's own answer too** (DAOLib issue 19, `151e624`: `Approach::findsRoom`; built against it 2026-10-04, the gate's use of it not yet seen in play). The first build took a place answered by `ApproachOf` for the engine's promise, and the staged fight showed it was not one: the gate's question refused 3 attacks, rightly, and the engine failed 16 more that it had let through as "in its reach: nothing to walk" or "0.5 m to walk", 45 to 180 ms on, 7 at the blow (the command's sub-action 1) and 9 with the command still at its approach (4). The cause, found by DAOLib in the exe from those lines: a ring nobody has claimed is emptied by its creature's update every frame, not filled, so the attack's first ask claims on an empty ring and finds room; the claim makes the ring active, the next frame's refresh records who stands round the target and wipes the claim, and the approach's next ask or the blow's then finds no free run of places. An attacker that strikes in its command's first update (in reach, already facing its target within a degree, the target within 60 degrees of level and in sight) strikes on its fresh claim, and lands however crowded the ring is. So DAOLib asks both, by the engine's own code and claiming nothing: whether the blow comes at once (`strikesAtOnce`), and whether the ring has a place for the attacker once filled as it will be a frame after the claim (`roomOnceFilled`); `findsRoom` is either (and false where the ring as it stands has no place at all), and the walk's goal is the place on the filled ring. The gate refuses a blow that will find no room, as no place, before a step. In the same staged crowd on that DAOLib (its own validation, 184 attacks): no room 24 times, and all 24 then failed for want of a place; room 160 times, and none did; the 39 that strike at once all landed. What still fails under way is what nothing asked beforehand foresees: 3 of the 160 ended `COMMAND_FAILED_INVALID_PATH`, a walker stuck against bodies after a path was given, which by DAOLib's reading cannot be known before the walk (the engine's own stuck timer: under 2 cm an update for a second of the walker's own time). Those are given back as before ("The AI's turn", below) and answered by the AI's own script, and a turn that keeps failing is ended by the count of actions given back (two, where its creature comes to rest between them), by the limit on a turn an AI plays where its script queues the next thing as each ends and the turn never comes to rest (`max_turn_ms`, "The AI's turn" below: until issue 38 a cap on a turn's commands ended that one at three attacks and three moves), and since issue 31 by the wait its script queues after a failure, which costs its own time. A kept attack turned on another, its own target fallen, goes by the same answer: only on one in its reach at which it will find room (`src/game/Bridge.cpp`, `Measure`).

**A kept attack is asked as it is given** (`TurnMachine::KeptAttackNotMade`; the gap seen in play 2026-10-04 on the build before, closed that day, not yet seen in play). An attack that does not fit its turn's bar is kept, and given to its creature as its next turn begins, through the gate; and the gate let it through whatever the engine answered: paid for, it was to land. In the staged crowd on that build 328 attacks were let through with room and none of them failed for want of it; the only two that did were kept attacks, each with the gate's own line above it saying "no room once its ring is filled". The engine failed them 50 ms on, and what their turns had paid for them (0.01 s and 0.84 s) stayed spent: a landed attack's time is its turns', not that command's to give back. So a kept attack the engine has no way to make as its turn comes (no room, no path, no line for a shot) is not made. It is dropped as one whose target is gone is ("Casting time", below): what this turn paid for it is given back and the turn is handed over whole, with the next look at everyone; what the turn before spent on it is spent, as it is then too. The player is told as for a refused order, the words over the character and on the caption "No room at the target: Sten's attack is not made". An AI's counts as an action given back, so that the same answer to what it chooses next ends its turn, and its being handed the turn is its being told. The event is `action_dropped`, its `detail` the engine's answer. A kept talent or spell carries no answer of the engine's and is given as before.

The straight line is kept for what it was meant for, where the engine has not been asked: its creature is at a command already, so no path may be searched, or `path_check` is off; and where it was asked and gave no answer (a search DAOLib stopped when its 40 ms ran out). A shut door in the way stays its own rule ("A shut door in the way", below).

An AI's attack at something far away is not refused: it walks what it can and is stopped, which is how an AI closes in over a turn or two. Each later try of an AI to reach something out of reach still takes one step before it is stopped, and so may a player's where the walk could not be worked out. The once-a-turn refund bounds that: a creature can end at most the stopping distance plus a few frames' walk (a fraction of a meter) past its budget, and the random fights (`tests/test_fights.cpp`) check the bound.

Abilities and spells are not checked for reach yet: an ability's range is in its own table (`abi_base`), not read so far.

The engine's pathfinder is reachable since DAOLib `8819b7c` (`world::FindPath`, for an idle creature; built 2026-09-30, not yet seen in play): a click on the ground whose path is longer than the meters left is refused before a step ("Too far"), as an attack out of reach is, and the walk of an attack out of reach is measured along the engine's path to where its approach goes rather than a straight line (in reach it has none: above). `[movement] path_check` turns it off, back to a straight line and a move that walks until its meters run out. The gold/red arc preview still needs a way to draw in the world (`dev/UI.md`, `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`).

## Time

The world runs only while someone acts. Everything that ticks in seconds ticks only then: effect durations, damage over time, cooldowns, stamina and mana regeneration. So a round of eight combatants, each acting for about a second and a half, is about twelve seconds of game time, and a 30-second cooldown comes back in two or three rounds. That keeps the base game's numbers meaning roughly what they meant.

One distortion follows: a round in a big fight lasts longer than a round in a small one, so cooldowns recover faster, per round, in big fights. `turns.round_min_seconds` evens it out by giving every round at least that much game time: if the turns ran short, the world runs on, nobody acting, before the next round. Off by default. Converting durations and cooldowns to rounds outright needs DAOLib (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Rules").

### Time rolls over only from a turn its actor ended (issue 29, built 2026-10-04)

What a turn leaves unspent rolls over to its creature's next, up to its cap. Until 2026-10-04 it did so whoever had ended the turn: a skeleton that never noticed the party read `Skeleton's turn ends: chose nothing; 1.00 s rolls over` every round, and a held companion `Alistair's turn ends: held by an effect; 1.00 s rolls over`. The user: "When turn is skipped, due to unawareness, incapacity, etc, there should be no time rollover, because the actor did not explicitly save any time."

So time rolls over only when the actor itself left it unspent. Every reason the machine ends a turn for is in one of two classes, in one place (`src/core/TurnEnd.h`, `EndedByItsActor`; `TurnMachine::EndTurn` asks it and nothing else decides), and a reason that table does not have rolls nothing over, with a warning in the log (`tools/test_turnend.py` reads the machine's source and fails on one that is not classed):

| Why the turn ended (the log's words, and `turn_ended`'s `detail`) | Class | What rolls over |
|---|---|---|
| `ended by the player` (End, its key, a tool) | its actor's | what is left, to the cap |
| `done` (an AI that acted and chose to wait, or chose nothing more as its action settled) | its actor's | what is left, to the cap |
| `waits` (an AI told its turn had come that chose to wait) | its actor's | what is left, to the cap |
| `nothing left to spend`, `its action pending`, `charging`, `its spell conjuring`, `its spell cast at once` | its actor's | nothing is left: the actor spent the bar |
| `keeping up its mode` (its creature at the command of a mode of its own that keeps it there, Captivating Song: the player pressed End and the song goes on, or its tactics have the mode on; issue 51, "No clock on the player's turn", under "The party's turn") | its actor's | nothing is left: the turn is spent on the mode |
| `unaware` (a hostile that has noticed nobody to fight: "An unaware hostile's turn", under "Who is in the fight") | skipped | nothing |
| `out of combat` (one not of the party that the game does not have in combat, found so in its own turn: it has no turn; "A creature the game does not have in combat has no turn", under "Who is in the fight") | skipped | nothing |
| `inactive` (one the game has made inactive, as its turn comes or in the middle of it: out of the world, it has no turn; "A creature the game has made inactive has no turn", under "Who is in the fight") | skipped | nothing |
| `chose nothing` (its AI queued nothing in `ai_think_ms`), `took too long` (on a turn an AI plays: an action past `max_action_ms`, or the turn past `max_turn_ms`; never a turn the player plays, issue 51), `nothing it can get to`, `out of movement` | ended for it | nothing |
| `fell`, `gone`, `down` | ended for it | nothing |
| `stunned`, `held by its condition`, `held by another's action`, `held by an effect` | lost | nothing |

The log says which: `Genlock's turn ends: done; 1.00 s rolls over`, or `Skeleton's turn ends: chose nothing; nothing rolls over, its actor not having ended it (1.00 s would have)`; with nothing left, `...; nothing rolls over`. ### Conditions in turns (built 2026-09-30)

The user's request after the first fights in play: a stun, measured in seconds, lasted more turns than it read as, and should be measured in turns, derived from its time, with a saving throw at the start of each turn. As BG3 and D&D do it:

- **A condition's length in turns** is its duration against a turn's seconds (4, `[turns] seconds_per_turn`): the mabari howl's 5 s stun is one turn and a second of the next, Pinning Shot's 8.5 s paralysis two and half a second, Holy Smite's 10 s two and a half.
- **It costs the creature its turns.** At the start of each of its turns while it lasts, a disabled creature (stunned, paralysed, asleep: `GetEffectsFlags` has `EFFECT_FLAG_DISABLE_COMBAT`) loses the turn at once, with "Stunned" over it and on its card, and the world does not run for it.
- **A save at the start of the turn** can end it early: a roll against the creature's physical or mental resistance (the attributes the game rolls its own resistances with; which one by the effect's kind), as BG3 rolls a save at the end of each turn.
- **It ends on the creature's own turn.** When its turns are spent, or the save succeeds, the mod removes the effect (`RemoveEffect`, wrapped) at the start of the creature's turn, so it acts that turn. The engine's own timer no longer matters.
- **Knockdowns and pins** are conditions like the rest (below).

Built on the hold's own seconds, read from the effect as it lands: the creature owes that much of its time. Where the seconds are not read it owes `condition_turns` turns' worth (1), the fallback first built for every condition, in whole turns, while each disabling effect's duration was out of reach (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Rules", item 2); saves are still to build. What is so:

- a stun, paralysis, sleep, pin (Pinning Shot's root) or knockdown is seen at the next status read, every half second and at every turn start, and the creature owes its time; a turn's worth of it or more takes the turn whole as it starts, "Name loses the turn" floating, the world still, and what is left under a turn's worth comes off the bar of the turn it then has;
- a creature stunned in its own turn loses the rest of it at once, and whatever was queued for it since is cleared; it still owes the hold's time, so a hold landing mid-turn costs the rest of that turn and its own length after, where one landing just before costs its length alone. Intended (the user, 2026-09-30, through the fourth play test: "if you fuck up on your own turn, you are extra punished");
- a hold that ends before its victim's turn: run out on the clock (effects' time moves only as a round begins, so a stun shorter than a round ends there) it keeps the time it owes, or a short stun would cost nothing; taken off by something else (a cure, a dispel; with effects' time held, any time but just after a round's aging), it gives it back (the fourth play test's addendum: a lifted stun still cost the turn);
- a pin was first built to take only the movement, the action kept, as the root's script reads (`effect_root_h`: the AI's stationary flag). In play (2026-09-30, 13:25) the engine held the pinned creature in the effect's command (`ExecuteEffect`, queued at the front as the pin landed: DAOLib's probe log); its attack queued behind and never ran, and with effects' time held the command outlasted the turn, which ended on the 12 s limit. So a pin costs the turn, as the user had put it ("turns of no action"); a knockdown too, which the engine handles the same way (not yet seen). Daze is only a penalty (`effect_daze_h`) and costs nothing; a grab (`EFFECT_TYPE_GRABBED`, ogres) is none of these conditions, and its victim is held as anyone at an effect's command is (below);
- End pressed while the engine holds the creature in something the gate did not see (an effect's command) ends the turn at once, and that command is left to the engine. Pressed while an action of its own runs it ends the turn at once too, and stops the action (issue 51; until then "its own action it still finishes first": "No clock on the player's turn", under "The party's turn");
- **what holds a creature at an effect's command costs the time the engine holds it** (issue 30, built 2026-10-04 and seen in play that day, below; the user, on reading a dragon's knockback as one more kind of hold: "we should not make a special case for it, but fix the way effects cost time by reading the actual amount of time they cost"). A creature at an effect's command it did not choose, as its turn comes or before it has acted in it, is held, whatever the effect: a knockback, an ogre's grab, the getting up from a stun the machine ended. No list of kinds says so; the engine does, by where it has the creature. (One command of an effect's is the creature's own doing and no hold: the command of a mode of its own that keeps it there, Captivating Song. Issue 51: "No clock on the player's turn", under "The party's turn", has how the two are told apart, and what its turn is then.) The hold runs on its turn, the world running, and the time it runs is the turn's. It takes that share of the bar by the turn's ratio, as a condition's seconds are taken, and the creature's own clock moves with it, so that an effect which lasts while its time lasts wears off as its creature pays for it; where the fight holds one clock for everyone's effects, that clock runs with the hold, a quarter second at a time and off the round's worth, as it does with an action's own effect. Let go, the creature has the rest of its turn: an action it kept is given then (an attack asked of the engine as it is given, like any: one the engine has no way to make by then is not made, and what this turn paid for it is given back, issue 27; the hold's time is the hold's all the same), an AI is told to choose, a party member's turn waits for an order (let go is two frames idle, as any command's end is: one frame can fall between two commands the engine queues, a fall and then a getting up if those are two, and what was kept, given into it, would queue behind the second). **The turn is lost only when the hold outlasts the bar**, and nothing of the bar is then left to roll over. The time is the game's: under a menu or the player's pause, where the game stands and the effect's command with it, nothing is taken. Meanwhile its orders are refused ("Held"), since they would queue behind the effect and run in someone else's turn; an AI is not told its turn has come (the machine looks at the creature in the turn-start frame), an attack of its script's that comes all the same is refused as held and not put to the engine's answer (it is not one given back, two of which end a turn: issue 27), and a wait of its AI's that comes so, run by something that happened to it, is refused with the rest and takes nothing of the bar, the hold having that time already (a wait costs its time only of a creature free to choose: issue 31; one paid before its creature was thrown is time before the hold, and each is taken once); an action it kept is not given (given then, it queued behind the effect, and the machine took the effect for the action's own and ran its time with it at three times the patience: a bandit archer's turn standing 26 s, issue 11, 2026-10-03 13:07); and `max_action_ms` does not stop it, the effect's command being the engine's to end. A hostile that has noticed nobody to fight is not held at all: its turn is skipped after its beat ("Who is in the fight", issue 29), whatever the engine has it at, and a hold is not stood out for a turn that is skipped anyway;
- until then such a creature was given 2 s (`kEffectGraceMs`) and then lost the turn whole, bar and all, and one getting up from a condition the machine had ended was let be for 5 s (`kGetUpMs`), its rise costing nothing. Against the high dragon the first cost the party 37 of 176 turns in one fight and 19 in 25 rounds of another, each to a knockback of 1.2 s by its effect's own count. The engine times only part of a hold ahead: in play (2026-10-04) a knockback's count was nothing, its one sub-action spent and the fall and rise on no list (DAOLib `57e1540` read that as a known 0.00; since `5e0108a` it answers "at least"), and after the roar it was about 2.0 where the hold then ran 2.25 to 2.53. And a creature whose stun the machine has just ended stands a frame more at a command whose count means nothing. Losing a turn at once where the count says the hold outlasts the bar, in place of standing the bar out, waits on a count that play shows right (a count that is at least what the bar has left would do for it, being short at worst);
- **the five conditions keep their own read of their time** (stun, paralysis, sleep, pin, knockdown, above), the one list of kinds left. ### Cooldowns in turns (built 2026-09-30)

DAO's cooldowns run in real time, paused or not: passing turns quickly left a talent cooling, and waiting brought it back (the user). An ability used is held on cooldown in the engine (3600 s, where waiting cannot reach) once its action has run, and its own seconds from the ability table then run on its user's clock, which moves as its bar is spent and a turn's seconds (4) with each of its turns, a lost one too: Shield Bash and Pinning Shot come back after about 4 turns, the mabari howl about 10, Overwhelm about 15. It is released as those seconds are run, within a turn or as one ends. (First built in whole turns, the cooldown over `cooldown_seconds_per_turn` rounded up and released as its user's turn began; the setting went with the action clock, issue 50.) An action taken with the movement spent is at something in reach, and its creature may shift its feet up to a metre to strike before "out of movement" stops it: Eleanor's Shield Bash at a soldier 2 m away was cleared 9 ms after it was let through, the action gone (the seventh play test). An ability its user was walking to use when the movement ran out never ran, so it is not held (the user, 2026-09-30: chosen, not reached, and wasted on cooldown). A turn was 10 s of a cooldown until the fourth play test, where the archers' stun came back every other turn and the dog acted every other round; at 4 a stun keeps about the share of the time it has in real time (the howl's 5 s stun on a 40 s cooldown, an eighth; the user's call, 2026-09-30). At the fight's end what still waits goes back to seconds. **The quickbar's sweep shows what is left** (the user, 2026-09-30: with 2 of 3 turns left to wait, a third recovered): as its user's clock moves a held ability is set to the time it has left over the time it was held for, on the hold's 3600 s scale, through DAOLib's `dao::abilities::SetCooldown(creature, ability, remaining, total)`; the scripts' `SetCooldown` sets both numbers as one, which showed a full sweep that never moved. That the slot draws that ratio is lib-agent's reading of the cooldown record, not yet seen on screen.

**Sustained abilities** (the ability table's `UseType` 2: Shield Wall, Powerful Swings, Flaming Weapons, Rock Armor, Stealth) cost nothing to turn on or off, and do not end the turn; turned off, one waits for its user's next turn, so it cannot be turned on, off and on again in one turn (the user's rule). Action points will put a price on them. Two of them take their user's every other action while they are on, the game keeping it at a command of the mode's own (Captivating Song, and by the scripts Feign Death): what a turn is then is "No clock on the player's turn, and a mode that keeps its user at a command", under "The party's turn" (issue 51). Nothing the mod does switches a mode off: not a turn's end, and not the stopping of what its creature was at when a turn is ended by force (the user, 2026-10-05: "Yeah, sustained abilities should not be ended").


### Everything in turns: the overhaul still to do

The user's rule (2026-09-30): in turn-based mode everything runs on turns (or, later, action points), nothing on time. Built so far: conditions (stun, paralysis, sleep, pins) and cooldowns. Still on the game's clock, running whenever the world runs:

- **Effect durations**: buffs, debuffs, damage and healing over time (burning, poison, bleeding, regeneration potions, auras), each a duration in seconds on a `CGameEffect`.
- **Regeneration**: stamina, mana and health coming back each second, in and out of combat.
- **Sustained abilities' upkeep**: their drain, if any, per second, and their stamina or mana reserved.
- **Areas that last**: spell areas on the ground (Grease, Blizzard, Inferno), traps' areas and lures, a duration each.
- **Summons and transformations** with a duration.
- **Casting and attack timing**: an action's own animation time, which is fine as it is (the world runs while an action plays).

**Built for effects (2026-09-30, DAOLib `4fee0fa`):** every temporary effect ages through one engine function, which DAOLib hooks (`dao/Effects.h`). A fight holds that clock from its start (`HoldTime`), spends a turn's seconds (`seconds_per_turn`, 4) on every effect at each new round (`AgeTime`), and lets it go when the fight ends or is dropped: buffs, debuffs, damage and healing over time, timed summons and, it is thought, lasting areas count rounds. With `own_clocks` (the default) what is on a creature runs on that creature's own clock instead, as its bar is spent and a turn's seconds a turn, and the shared clock is the areas' and the scripts' timers' (`dev/REBALANCE.md`, "The creature's own clock"). Regeneration too, since DAOLib `a42be67`: each creature's stats tick it every 2 s in combat (`UpdateRegeneration`), and `dao/Clock.h` holds and moves on effects and regeneration together; a 4 s round is two ticks. Still on the clock: timers scripts set themselves with `DelayEvent`. Not yet seen in play.

**An action's own effect gets its time** (built 2026-10-01). Effects' time is held through a fight and spent a round's worth at each round. But some actions are themselves an effect that must run out: a giant spider's Overwhelm is one effect on the spider and one on its prey, each ended by its time, and held, the pounce never ended: the world stood twelve seconds until `max_action_ms` stopped it and the turn ended "took too long" (the eighth play test). So while the acting creature is at an effect's command after its own action, effects' time runs with the world, and what it took comes off that round's worth. An effect that held a creature before its turn began costs the time it holds it, effects' time running with it the same way (issue 30: "Conditions in turns", above; until 2026-10-04 it cost the turn, and effects' time stood still for it); a stun in one's own turn still ends it. And the command of a mode of its own that the creature has just turned on (Captivating Song) is not its action's own effect: nothing runs with it and the turn does not wait to see its end (issue 51; until 2026-10-05 it was taken for one, and the turn stood at it to three times `max_action_ms`).

The direction: the world's clock stops being the fight's clock. A round (or each creature's turn) advances what belongs to its creatures by a fixed amount, as conditions and cooldowns do now: durations counted in turns and ended at the start of the owner's turn; regeneration given per turn; areas lasting so many rounds. Two ways to get there, which the DAOLib work decides between:

- **Hold the clock and step it**: the game's time scale, or its effect timers, frozen outside actions, and advanced by a round's worth at each round (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Time and input": the time scale, advancing game time without running the world).
- **Own each timer**: read and set an effect's remaining duration (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "Rules": an effect's duration and remaining time), hold it as cooldowns are held, and count it down per turn; set regeneration to nothing in fights and give it per turn.

Until then, `pause_on_player_turn = true` keeps what ticks from ticking while the player chooses; off (the default, the user's choice), the world idles on and those timers run.

**The scripts' own timers count rounds too** (issue 26, 2026-10-04; `script_timers_from`, 1 s; DAOLib `dao::timers::HoldFrom`). A round is 4 s for each fighter, but as many times 4 s of the world's clock as there are fighters, since the world runs through each one's turn in sequence. So a plot's timer left on the world's clock runs that many times too fast against what the fighters do. In the Harrowing Chamber Uldred turns the captive mages into abominations one at a time: a light comes on the mage, and the Litany of Adralla read while it is on stops it. His script (`cir240cr_uldred.nss`, read in the toolset's source) begins a turning when he is hurt below 90, 65, 40 and 20 per cent, or on a timer of his own every 35 s, and gives it ten seconds. On the world's clock all four turnings fell in the first three rounds, with one or two fighters' turns between the light and the death: a mage was lost across one abomination's turn before any of the party had a turn to read in. The user, 2026-10-04: "We should probably figure out a way to suspend the time so that no matter where an actor is in the round, they get to react to it. Recall that a round is supposed to be the same 4 seconds for everyone for things that apply over time or are environmental. Direct damage like attacks take place immediately, which is affected by order in the round. But something like interruption with the litany of adralla should be allowed any position in the round." So through a fight every script event sent with a delay of a second or more waits on the clock effects wait on, and comes as the rounds spend it, whatever its kind; one already waiting in the engine's queue as the fight begins is taken by the same rule. Under a second the scripts are putting one thing after another (Uldred's change of shape, 0.01 s) and run as they do, and the core scripts' return to explore mode after the last enemy falls is left out by DAOLib, or no fight would end. Seen in that fight (DAOLib's trace of delayed events in the log, `script_timers_trace`): the 35 s came due in round 10, each turning's ten seconds were two to three rounds, the Litany was read in the round after each of four turnings, and nobody was turned, where five earlier plays had lost one to four mages. **What it cannot mend** is the script's own race, which real time has too: the Litany's break is one flag, and a turning that begins and is broken while an earlier broken turning's ten seconds are still to come has its break used up by the earlier one. In one test with these timers held by hand two mages died that way. `script_timers_from = 0` is the old way: only a damage over time's ticks and a lasting area's pulses count rounds.

## Who is in the fight (built 2026-09-30)

The game says who is in combat, not with whom. In Castle Cousland Howe's men and the castle's guards fight in rooms the party has not reached, all "in combat" and within `combat_radius`: a fight took in nine enemies, the turn order filled with creatures nobody could see, and the camera went to rooms that are not drawn (the user's picture, 2026-09-30). The user did not want a rule of distance alone: it cuts out enemies who should be in the fight and takes in ones who should not.

So an enemy or ally in combat within the radius takes turns only once it is **fighting where the player can see it** (`join_by_sight`, on): it stands in a room the game shows, or it or a party member is the other's attack target. The room is the engine's own answer and the user's own rule ("suspend the ones in areas that aren't loaded"): an area is made of rooms, and the game hides every room no party member has yet seen, drawing it black and leaving its creatures inactive, even through an open door, until one of the party walks in (lib-agent's reading and play test, DAOLib `1d99f54`, `dao::world::RoomOf(creature)->Shown()`). What is seen goes by the party, not the camera, so the mod's camera going to such a room shows nothing. The first rule built here was a party member's line of sight, which is clear through that open door; it remains only for an area with no rooms, or one where the party's own room (the room of the one who stands for it, below) does not read as shown (the rooms cannot be gone by there). Until then it **waits outside the fight**, where it stands: its commands are held like a joiner's, it is not in the turn order, and nothing of it is shown. When the party sees its room, or one attacks the other, it joins as any creature joining mid-way does, with a roll of initiative. **Once in, it stays in**: stepping behind a pillar does not take a creature out of the fight. The fight is over when the last enemy in it falls; one still waiting outside is the game's again, in real time, and begins turns again when it finds the party.

**And near, if it is not on the party** (`join_radius`, 30 m; the user, 2026-10-03: "50/30 split seems reasonable to me"). Out of doors the room is no answer: every room within the radius is one the party has seen. In the Korcari Wilds the party set on four darkspawn, and a hurlock, an archer and four wolves fighting each other 45 m off, in plain sight, joined: thirteen in the order, eighteen rounds (issue 6, the playthrough of issue 3). The game's own aggro had set creatures on the party from 17 to 23 m in those fights, so it is not the radius that brings enemies in, and a smaller radius would not do: a creature in combat beyond it is held, and an archer shooting from 35 m would stand frozen under the party's own bows. So the two are split: one that **attacks a party member or is attacked by one** joins from anywhere in `combat_radius`, seen or not, as before; one that is only in combat joins if it is seen **and within `join_radius` of a party member**. The rest wait outside as the unseen do, a bystanders' battle standing as it is until the party's fight is over, when it goes on in real time. Those of the fight itself that still stand when it ends, enemy or ally, are told so too: an idle AI lives on a loop of waits, each one's end running it again, and in a fight its wait is its pass and is blocked, so with the turns over it had nothing queued and nothing to wake it. At Redcliffe's windmill the game ended combat with one walking corpse alive up the path; it stood there, and the night's second battle, which waits on the first's dead, never began (issue 12, 2026-10-03). The facts are the game layer's (`Sight`, `src/game/Bridge.cpp`), the rule core's (`InThePartysFight`, `src/core/Rules.cpp`).

**The party is where its living members are** (issue 42, built 2026-10-04; not yet seen in play). Everything above speaks of "the party", and until then the scan asked all of it of one creature, the party's leader: the engine was asked for the hostiles nearest the leader, `combat_radius` was measured from the leader, the party was in combat if the leader was, and the rooms rule went by the leader's room. A party is not where one of it is. Twice in one evening in Ortan Thaig (the playthrough of issue 3) a long fight drifted, and three hostiles stood 2 to 9 m from a party member, in combat and perceiving him, and were never given a turn: the fight the mod did have ended `no enemy left standing`, the turn bar went, and the game stayed in combat with nothing happening. The first time the leader had fallen 83 m from the last one standing (asked by script, `GetPartyLeader` was the hero, `IsDead` 1, her combat state 0, while `GetMainControlled` was the survivor), so everything was asked of a body. The second time, from the same save, nobody was dead: the leader, alive and the one controlled, stood 70 m from the three others. Both calls answer as designed (DAOLib's session read the engine for it, 2026-10-04): `GetPartyLeader` is the party's leader field, the one conversations take for PLAYER, which only `SetPartyLeader` changes, not death and not the player selecting someone else; `GetMainControlled` is the creature the player controls. Neither is where the party is. The game's own measure is the party's: it is in combat while any member perceives a hostile (`IsPartyPerceivingHostiles`, `combat_h.nss`), whoever leads. So:

- **a creature is as far from the party as from the nearest of its living members**, and `combat_radius` and `join_radius` are measured so (`MetersFromTheParty`, `WithinReach`, `src/core/Rules.h`). A fallen member counts for nothing here: nobody is near the party for being near a body, a body's line of sight is nobody's, and the attack target it had is not a fight;
- **who is near is asked round each living member** (the game's own question, the nearest 24 living hostiles, and the same of those not hostile), each creature taken once. The engine answers with the nearest to the creature it is asked of, so asked of one alone it names nobody beside a member who stands far from that one, or behind a crowd nearer to it. More than 24 can so be near a party that has spread out; none is turned away for the count;
- **the party is in combat while any living member is** (`PartyIsInCombat`), which is what lets a hostile that has not noticed be drawn in (above);

## The AI's turn

**An action that failed without acting is tried again, once.** A command that ends in a failure that did nothing (no path, no line of sight, no room at the target: `COMMAND_FAILED_NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING`, the engine's limit on how many may stand round one creature) gives its action back, and an AI is then told its turn has come once more, where its turn used to end 48 ms later with the action unused: an ally guard stood through a whole fight that way (the user, 2026-09-30, read off Eluvian's bus). Seen in play (the seventh play test, 2026-10-01, a staged crowd of seventeen): with its action back the game's own AI carries on by itself. A soldier with no room at its target at once tries to move round to it (`move to object`, which ends `COMMAND_FAILED_INVALID_PATH`, the crowd standing in the way), waits about a second, attacks again, fails again, and chooses to wait, which ends its turn. So the mod's telling it again is the fallback for an AI that queues nothing, and did not fire once in twenty such failures. That a creature with no room stands and waits is the engine's own rule, in real time too. Since issue 27 (2026-10-04) an attack the engine has no way to make when it is asked is given back before a step, and there the telling again is no fallback: nothing of its own was queued, so nothing ends to wake its AI ("What the engine answers no to is refused before a step", above; seen in play that day, three times of three). Given back twice in a turn, by a failure or before a step, an AI has nothing it can get to and its turn ends. Its second attack on the same target after a failure is put to the engine like any other: a rule that refused it unasked was tried and withdrawn (the same section).

**The player is told why, over the character and on the bar's caption** (issue 21, 2026-10-03; built and tested without the game, not yet seen in play). A party member's order that the engine refuses once it is under way is given back the same way, and the turn goes on. In real time the game shuffles a blocked fighter round or the player sees the character stand; in turns each try is a decision. In the playthrough (issue 3) Sten's attack was refused eighteen times in one fight at a doorway, six of his turns ending with nothing spent, and Morrigan's spell thirteen times at a shut door, with only a floaty over the character to say why, a moment's word that a player may miss in a fight and a tool that drives the game does not see, and the same words ("No way through") for a door, for no room and for no path. The words are now by the engine's reason (`src/core/GivenBack.h`): "A door is in the way" (`COMMAND_FAILED_PATH_ACTION_REQUIRED`), "No room at the target" (`..._NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING`), "No way through" (`..._INVALID_PATH`, and an attack stopped standing short of its target), "No line of sight" (`..._NO_LINE_OF_SIGHT`), "Nothing happened" (`..._INVALID_DATA`, and an action over at once with nothing done). They float over the character in gold as before, and the bar's caption has the whole line in gold, "No room at the target: Sten's attack is given back", in place of what the turn has left, for five seconds of the real clock (the world stands still while the player reads) or until the player's next order is let through. `turnbased.state` has the line as `notice` while it shows, for a tool that drives the game. Not said for a creature whose AI plays its turn: it is told to choose again. Where the engine can be asked beforehand and answers no (no place, no path, no line for a shot), the order is refused before a step in the same words, and the caption says "is refused" (issue 27; "What the engine answers no to is refused before a step", above).

The game's AI is event-driven (DAOLib's `dev/TURN_BASED.md`). When a creature's command completes, the engine sends it `EVENT_TYPE_COMMAND_COMPLETE`; `rules_core.nss` runs `AI_DetermineCombatRound`, which picks the next command and queues it with `AddCommand`. There is no round. The mod turns that loop on and off:

- **Off-turn**, every command a combatant chooses is refused at the queue (`src/game/Gate.cpp`). A creature with nothing queued gets no `COMMAND_COMPLETE`, so its AI stops choosing, and nothing it does costs anything. (A combatant the game has in combat: what the script of one it does not has it do is the game's, and goes through. "The fight holds what the game has in combat, and nothing else", under "Who is in the fight".)
- **Its turn** starts with the mod sending it a `COMMAND_COMPLETE` of its own (a wait that succeeded), processed at once. The creature's own script sees it first, then `rules_core`, then its AI: custom boss AI included, which is why this is the default rather than the scripts' `EVENT_TYPE_DEBUG_KICKSTART_AI` (`turns.ai_poke`). Its command comes back through the queue, is allowed, and runs. Not a hostile that perceives nobody to fight: it is sent nothing, and its turn is skipped ("An unaware hostile's turn").
- When that command completes, the AI is told to choose again while time is left on its bar: a move, another action, or a wait, which costs the time it waits (below). After `turns.ai_follow_up_ms` with nothing more, the turn ends.
- An AI that chooses nothing within `turns.ai_think_ms` passes.
- **A turn an AI plays has a time limit** (issue 38, built 2026-10-04, not yet seen in play): `turns.max_turn_ms`, thirty seconds. Still going then, it is ended whatever it is doing, `took too long`, and what its creature was at is stopped (not an effect's command that holds it: that is the engine's to end). It is the same kind of thing as `max_action_ms`: of the wall's time, counted from when the turn began, and nothing rolls over from it. A turn the player plays has none, and since issue 51 no limit on an action either: no clock of the mod's ends it ("No clock on the player's turn", under "The party's turn"). A companion's has both when its tactics play it (chosen for it, or `party = tactics`: "The party's turn").
    - **What it is for.** The machine's other waits each bound one thing: `ai_think_ms` an AI's choosing, `ai_follow_up_ms` its next move after an action, `max_action_ms` one action, the standing-still limits one standing. None bounds a turn made of many steps that each take nothing of its bar: a weapon set switched again as each ends, a move that is over where it stands each time it is told, a walk it cannot make again and again, an attack the engine fails and its script tries again. Until this a count did, `max_commands_per_turn`, six, "which guards against a loop", and it cut turns that had time to spend. The user, shown one (2026-10-04): "What is the command cap? Do we need to limit the number of commands that can be issued? I thought we relied on time as the limiting factor", and: "Yeah let's get rid of the cap. Most actions should have some kind of cooldown. And I don't think the AI itself is going to do things like swap weapons endlessly." The count is gone (a settings file that still has the key is told once), nothing counts the commands an AI starts, and an AI with time left is told to choose again as often as it has time.
    - **Why thirty seconds.** To cut nothing a turn does in play. In 54 logs (5,303 turns AIs played, each counted once; 4,253 of them at three times the speed, 894 at twice, 119 at the game's own) half took 2.2 s of the wall's time and ninety-nine in a hundred under 7.4 s. The longest that was not one action stopped at `max_action_ms` was a boss's, 13.1 s: five commands, and a walk to her kept attack's target that stood and was stopped. Ten more took over 10 s. Twelve seconds, `max_action_ms`'s own, would have cut that turn and stood within two seconds of the ten. None of the loops it is for is known in the game's scripts, so what a long limit costs is how long one would spin if it came.
    - **Tried first and dropped, so that nobody builds them again** (all 2026-10-04). The count itself. "An AI is told to choose again only if something of its bar was spent since it was last told": it took the whole turn of a boss who opens by turning a sustained ability on, which costs nothing, and acts only when told again (`Jarvia: use ability (free, from script); 5.00 s and 24.3 m left`, then `Jarvia chooses again: 5.00 s and 24.3 m left`, three times in two fights). Its exception, "but once a turn it is". And "standing costs the time stood", a command that got nowhere charged the time its creature stood at it, on every turn: in play it charged a party member for a path the engine itself gave up (`Wynne stood 1.03 s at a move to location that got nowhere: 1.03 s of its bar`). The user, of the first two: "How about for non-party members we apply a timeout? Don't we already have one? I'm skeptical about hacking in all these conditions. We should keep things simple."; and of the third: "Drop it then." A blocked walk costs what it did before all of this, on every turn: nothing but the time it takes.
- **When a turn has ended, nothing more of its creature's own is accepted** (issue 38): not its script's, not the engine's, not an order. It stays the current turn only until what its creature already has in hand is over and the world has settled. One check in the gate's judgement (`TurnMachine::Judge`) for the two things that end a turn before the machine closes it: an action kept for its next turn, or a cast begun, with nothing else running (since 2026-10-01); and an AI's own wait that is longer than what it has left, or has no length (issue 38). Every other end is said as the turn is closed, and from then its creature's commands are refused as anyone's out of turn. What the mod itself gives a creature that has passed is still given: the action it kept, as its turn began, and the walk to its kept attack's target.
    - **Why the wait needed it, which was the mod's own doing.** Seen in play, in a fight on a road (2026-10-04, the one turn of its 197 AI turns in which the count spoke): an allied soldier's attack ended as the last enemy fell; its script answered with a wait of 2 s and the engine queued `sheathe or draw (free, from engine)` on it. The wait did not fit the 1.07 s it had left and was refused, `its turn ends`; the weapon command was let through, free. Both were over at once, so its script was asked again at once, and the same pair came round every 40 to 150 ms: the game's AI paces itself by how long its commands take, and a wait refused takes no time. The fourth weapon command, a quarter of a second after the first, was the turn's sixth command; the count refused what came after eleven times, and the turn ended half a second after its actor had ended it. Not seen in play but tried in the tests' stand-in for the game, with the count gone and this rule off: the same turn went on to its time limit, 1,844 weapon commands in 29.5 s, and `took too long` where its actor had ended it (`waits`, its saved second rolled over).
    - The user, shown the turn (2026-10-05): "Why are we blocking a wait? Should we not allow it and then deduct the extra time from the next turn? Or we can just end the turn without allowing another command." The second. Allowed, every pass would be watched in real time, and what did not fit would be a debt against a turn whose time rolls over.
    - **Not under the rule: a turn whose end is decided while its creature still has its last thing in hand.** There the turn is not over until that thing is, and commands are judged as before it. Three are a kept action's: one kept while another command of its creature's is still running (the turn ends as that one settles), the walk to a kept attack's target, and a spell on its way to its conjure; in these the bar is spent or spoken for, so what still gets through is what costs nothing (a weapon set switched, the engine's own weapon command). They are left as they were because whether the engine needs a command of its own let through for the thing in hand (a weapon drawn before a blow, say) is not known, and cannot be tried without the game. None has been seen to loop, and the limit above bounds the AI's. (There was a fourth until issue 51: a turn the player ended with End while the character's action was still running, which went on until that action was over. End stops the action now, and that turn is closed with the same step.)
- **What ends an AI's turn, all of it:** its bar is spent (`done`, nothing left); an action that does not fit is kept for its next turn (`its action pending`; a cast begun, `charging`, `its spell conjuring`, `its spell cast at once`); it is stopped where its movement ran out (`out of movement`); its AI has nothing more to do after a command of its own (`done`) or waits (`waits`); it chooses nothing within `ai_think_ms` (`chose nothing`); two of its actions were given back (`nothing it can get to`); one action ran past `max_action_ms`, or the turn past `max_turn_ms` (`took too long`: what it was at is stopped, its commands cleared, and no mode of its switched off); its creature is at the command of a mode of its own that keeps it there, a bard singing Captivating Song (`keeping up its mode`: the turn is spent on the mode at once, its AI not told; issue 51, "No clock on the player's turn", under "The party's turn"); it has noticed nobody to fight (`unaware`); it is held (`stunned`, `held by its condition`, `held by another's action`, `held by an effect`) or falls or is gone (`fell`, `gone`, `down`). Besides those, a creature standing 2.5 s at a walk, or a second at an attack's approach, is stopped at it: its command ends there, and its turn goes on or ends as after any command.

**An AI's wait costs the time it waits** (issue 31; built and seen in play 2026-10-04: against the high dragon above Haven the gate read 1,000 ms from the wait's own parameter, 93 waits were paid, and of the dragon's 62 turns 35 waited first and then acted and none was lost to a wait alone, where 18 of 77 had done nothing; the 7 that still did nothing were its AI choosing nothing at all, issue 35). The game's AI waits between two things it does, `CommandWait(AI_DO_NOTHING_DELAY)`, 2 s, or the quick one, 1 s (`ai_main_h_2.nss`, `ai_constants_h.nss`), and chooses again as the wait ends. Taken for a pass, a wait ended the turn with the rest of its bar unspent: a creature with a bar of 7 s that waited 2 lost all 7, and in the playthrough (issue 3) the high dragon above Haven ended 20 of its 77 turns so, 5 to 7 s left each time. The user, on reading that: "Shouldn't this cost the time equal to how long it waits? If it's less than the amount of time it has to take actions in a turn, it can still take more actions". So a wait takes its own length of its creature's bar, as an action takes its time, and its creature's own clock moves by it (its cooldowns, the effects on it). With time left its AI is told to choose again at once, by the event the engine itself sends as a wait ends (the mod's `COMMAND_COMPLETE` of a wait that succeeded, above). A wait longer than what is left takes nothing and ends the turn as every wait did, what is left rolling over by the usual rule; and an ended turn accepts nothing more of its creature's own ("The AI's turn", above; the user, 2026-10-05: "Or we can just end the turn without allowing another command"). A wait that fits does not end the turn: it is charged, not run, and its AI is asked again. The user, asked whether that one should end the turn too (2026-10-05): "Yeah, that's the right decision. The AI is currently not aware of the bar, so we should not end the turn immediately on wait." The wait itself is still refused at the queue: there is nothing of it to see, and the world does not run two seconds for a creature standing. **Its length is the engine's own number**, read from the command before it would run (`src/game/Gate.cpp`, `WaitSecondsOf`): `CommandWait` keeps its seconds as milliseconds in the command's first integer (DAOLib's `dao::Command::WaitMilliseconds`, read from the script command's constructor, and seen in play in DAOLib's example plugin's log, `wait 2000 ms` for an AI's wait). One of no length, or whose length could not be read, would cost nothing and could be chosen for ever: it ends the turn as before. A creature with nothing to do waits again and again and has no rule of its own: its bar bounds it (a bar of 5 s is two waits of 2 s, and the third ends the turn), and nothing else: its waits are not counted (issue 38: they were, with its commands, against `max_commands_per_turn`). A turn the player plays is not touched (its character's own AI's wait is refused it: "The party's turn"). The log says `Hurlock waits 2.00 s; 3.00 s and 15.0 m left`, then `Hurlock chooses again: 3.00 s and 15.0 m left`; for one that does not fit, `Hurlock's wait of 2.00 s is longer than the 1.00 s it has left: its turn ends` and `Hurlock's turn ends: waits; 1.00 s rolls over`.

## The party's turn

Only the player's orders spend a party member's turn. A controlled character's own AI carries on attacking by itself in real time (`AI_DetermineCombatRound_Partial`); on the player's turns its attacks and moves are refused, so the party's AI is off in turn-based fights except under `party = tactics` (the user, 2026-09-30). Its weapons and its modes are refused it too, free as they are: a follower that is not the controlled one goes to melee weapons with an enemy at hand and turns its bow's mode off for it (`AI_DetermineCombatRound`; the controlled one's partial AI never does), and a turn that opens with a kept action landing runs that full AI before its character is selected, so that in the playthrough of issue 3 a rogue and a warrior left their bows for good, unasked (issue 4, 2026-10-02). The player's own change of weapons, by the button or the key, is free as ever. Its wait is refused it as well: with no enemy left to choose, that full AI queues a wait of a second and another as each ends (`AI_ExecuteDefaultAction`, `_AI_DoNothing`; the controlled one's never waits), and let through they held a turn at its command for good. A turn opened by a kept shot that felled the fight's last enemy stood eleven minutes so, never handed to the player, End doing nothing (issue 10, the Tower of Ishal, 2026-10-03).

The mod selects the party member (`SetPrimaryControlled`) and waits, paused. An order arrives at the queue as the base game gives it, is checked against the turn's action and movement, and runs. Whether the engine queues an order given while paused at once, or only once the game runs, has not been seen. So the mod also watches explore mode's order handlers: if an order comes and no command follows within a tenth of a second, it runs the world for up to half a second, everyone else still held, until the command arrives. When the character is idle again, the world settles for `turns.settle_ms` (arrows land, bodies fall) and pauses for the next order. A controlled character's automatic "keep attacking" is a second action and is refused.

With `turns.party = tactics`, the companions play their turns through their tactics like the AI, and only the hero waits for the player. The hero is kept selected on the companions' turns, since the game gives a controlled character only its "keep attacking" AI (`AI_DetermineCombatRound_Partial`); with the hero down, another of the party standing is ("Who is in the fight", "The party is where its living members are").

### No clock on the player's turn, and a mode that keeps its user at a command (issue 51; built 2026-10-05 and tested without the game, not yet seen in play)

A party member singing the Bard's Captivating Song had no real turn. The song is a mode in form, turned on and kept up, and the one mode that takes its singer's every other action while it is on, by the game's own design ("the bard cannot move or take any other action while singing"): the talent's script puts on the singer, beside the song's area, an effect whose command the engine keeps her at (`talent_modal.nss`: `EFFECT_TYPE_SINGING`, permanent, on the caster and by the caster, through `Ability_ApplyUpkeepEffects`). Song of Valor and Song of Courage hold nobody. The mod read that command as any hold, a knockdown's or a grab's ("Conditions in turns"): the time it held was taken of her bar as the world ran, and the turn was lost four seconds of the game's time later, 1.3 s of the wall's at three times the speed, before the player had chosen anything. In the ability sweep of 2026-10-05 (Soldier's Peak) Lyna, given the song, lost 90 turns in a row: `Lyna is held by an effect's command past its bar (4.08 s held this turn): its turn is lost`. Ordered off at the very start of a turn the song did go off, and what was left of the bar was spent by then: stopping cost the turn too. And turned on by the player in her own turn, the song's command was taken for that turn's action and never ended: the turn stood at `executing` to the action's limit, `still at a execute effect after 108000 ms: stopped`, `Lyna's turn ends: took too long`, twelve seconds of the wall's time with the fight standing still.

**Decided by the user (2026-10-05), and wider than the song:** "I think there probably should not be a time out for the player, unless they're letting tactics run the actor. This will give the player the opportunity to turn off the song at their leisure, or end their turn." Of the way out of an action that never ends: "timeout should end any actions that are still being taken when it forces an end". And of what a forced end does to a mode: "Yeah, sustained abilities should not be ended". So:

- **A turn the player plays is never ended by a clock of the mod's.** It has no time limit of its own (`max_turn_ms` was always an AI's: "The AI's turn"), and since this issue no action of it is stopped at `max_action_ms`: one the engine never finishes is the player's to end, with End (below). The log says once where such a turn stands: `Hero is still at a attack after 12000 ms: its turn is the player's, and is not ended for it (End stops it)`. A turn its tactics play keeps both limits: an enemy's, an ally's, and a companion's whose tactics play its turns, by how that turn began ("Whose tactics play its turns", below). What stops a command on the player's turn without ending the turn is as it was: a creature standing at a walk it does not take, an attack that cannot get to its target ("A shut door in the way"), the movement running out.
- **What takes a turn and is no clock stays as it was, on the player's turns too:** the five conditions (a stun, paralysis, sleep, a pin, a knockdown), and another's hold at an effect's command (a knockback, a grab), its time taken of the bar as it runs and the turn lost once it outlasts the bar ("Conditions in turns"). That is the game taking the turn.
- **A creature's own mode's command is not a hold** (`TurnMachine::AtItsOwnMode`). On a turn the player plays the turn waits for the player as on any turn, for as long as the player likes, the bar not running: it is never `executing` for the mode, and nothing of its time or of its creature's clock is taken. The log says once a turn why it waits: `Lyna is at its own mode's command (ability 1000), not held: its turn waits for the player, its bar not running (4.00 s and 19.6 m left); End goes on with the mode, the turn spent on it, and the mode turned off is free`. The player then has, once a turn, the choice the game gives a bard at every moment:
    - **The mode turned off is free**, as turning any mode is ("Sustained abilities", under "Cooldowns in turns"), and leaves the turn to act with, its bar as it was.
    - **End goes on with the mode, and the turn is spent on it**, as the game spends a bard's time on her song: `Lyna's turn is spent on its own mode (ability 1000), which keeps it at its command: End was pressed, and the mode goes on`, then `Lyna's turn ends: keeping up its mode; 0.00 s rolls over`. Nothing rolls over, and her own clock moves a turn's seconds as for any turn that ends (her cooldowns, the song's upkeep).
    - **The mode turned on in its own turn** is free too, and is not an action the turn waits to see the end of: as its command has the creature the turn settles, and waits for the player again with its bar as it was.
    - **An action or a move ordered meanwhile is refused before anything is spent** (`TurnMachine::Judge`): "Turn its mode off first" over the character in grey and on the bar's caption (`Turn its mode off first: Lyna's attack is refused`), and the `refused` event with the mode's ability in its note. **This one is not the user's words: it is the mod's reading of them, and theirs to change.** The game itself queues such an order behind the mode's command and carries it out only when the mode ends (in real time, turn mode off, 2026-10-05: "A move order then moved her 0.0 m and an attack order struck nothing", and the attack was made as the song was ordered off). Let through in turns, it would take its time of the bar for nothing and leave a command to arrive later, in whoever's turn the mode ends in. What is free goes through: the mode's own turning off above all, which is what the words ask for, and the rest (a weapon set, another mode), which waits behind the mode as it does in the game.
- **On a turn its tactics play, the turn is spent on the mode at once**: as its creature is found at its own mode's command (as the turn begins, while its AI thinks, or after its own command that turned the mode on), within two frames, no bar run down in real time and never `took too long`. Its AI is not told to choose: the game's scripts run no AI for a creature that is at a command, and a command it did queue would wait behind the mode's. The lines are the player's with another reason: `Genlock's turn is spent on its own mode (ability 1000), which keeps it at its command: its tactics have it on, and its AI is not told to choose`, `Genlock's turn ends: keeping up its mode; 0.00 s rolls over`. So an AI's bard sings for as long as the mode is on, turn after turn; in the game too nothing but the song's end runs its AI again.

**How its own mode's command is told from another's hold** (`src/game/Bridge.cpp`, `Bridge::Actor`; `src/core/Rules.h`, `OwnModeAtItsCommand`; the machine is told the mode's ability, `ActorObservation::ownMode`). By an effect standing on the creature (DAOLib's `dao::effects::On`) of which three things are so, all of them: the creature put it on itself; by an ability that is a mode of its own (the ability table's `UseType` 2) and is on (the scripts' `IsModalAbilityActive`); and it is of a kind that keeps its creature at a command, `EFFECT_TYPE_SINGING` (1060: Captivating Song, ability 1000) or `EFFECT_TYPE_FEIGN_DEATH` (1041: Feign Death, ability 3023). **Feign Death is the same case by the scripts** (`talent_modal.nss`, `ABILITY_TALENT_FEIGN_DEATH`: `EffectFeignDeath()` put on its user through the same `Ability_ApplyUpkeepEffects`; in the sweep its user's next turn was lost to a hold as the singer's were), and has not been seen in play under this rule. The third is a list of kinds, the mod's second (the five conditions are the first), and it is needed. Every mode puts effects of its own making on its user (its upkeep, its property modifiers), so by the first two alone a warrior with Shield Wall on, thrown by a dragon, would read as at his own mode's command: on a turn his tactics play the whole turn spent at once, the loss issue 30 mended, and on the player's the knockback costing nothing. Nothing else read of the command tells the two apart: a knockback's and the song's both run sub-action 51, and of both the log reads `no temporary effect stands on it` (the song's effect is permanent; a knockback's has run out by the getting up). When DAOLib reads which effect an `ExecuteEffect` command runs (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`, "The command queue", 7), the command says whose it is and the list goes. **What the list cannot say meanwhile:** a singer thrown by another reads as at her own mode's command through the throw too, the song's effect standing all the while; on the player's turn the throw then costs nothing, and on a turn her tactics play the turn was the song's anyway. The five conditions are read apart from all this and take a singer's turn as anyone's. The game layer says what it found once a turn, at info: `0x82 is at its own mode's command, not held: its ability 1000 (Captivating Song) is a mode and is on, and the effect of type 1060 (EFFECT_TYPE_SINGING) that it put on itself by it keeps it there`.

**Whatever ends a turn by force stops what its creature was at** (the user's second sentence above). One rule: its creature's commands are cleared, so that nothing of the turn is left to arrive in another's.

- **A time limit, on a turn its tactics play**: an action past `max_action_ms`, or the turn past `max_turn_ms`. Each clears its creature's commands and ends the turn `took too long`, as they did. (Not an effect's command that holds the creature: that is the engine's to end, and `max_action_ms` does not run for a hold at all.)
- **The player's End pressed while an action of the turn still runs**: the action is stopped and the turn ends with that step, `ended by the player`. The log says `Lyna's attack is stopped: its turn was ended while it ran, and what it spent on it stays spent`. Until this issue End waited for the action ("its own action it finishes first"): pressed in the middle of a swing or a walk, nothing was seen to happen until the action was over, and one the engine never finished held the turn to `max_action_ms`, which the player's turn no longer has. A command let through and not yet begun is cleared with the rest. At a command the engine put on the creature (an effect's) the turn ends at once as it always did, and that command is left to the engine.
- **What happens to the action.** One only begun (let through, its walk or its swing under way, and cleared by the forced end) is lost: its time stays spent, nothing of it is given back, and a cooldown it set is held, as for an action stopped at a time limit. What the bar still held beside it rolls over by the usual rule: after End, to the cap; after a time limit, nothing. A spell given to the engine and still on its way to its conjure is such an action: stopped, it is lost ("Spell lost"), nothing of the bar having been spent on it yet. An action *kept* (one that did not fit, begun and to land on a later turn once paid: "Casting time") is not a running action and is not touched: it lands as it would have.
- **A forced end leaves every sustained ability on. Decided:** "Yeah, sustained abilities should not be ended". The machine clears commands and has no way to switch a mode off; in play the song went on after `clear commands` (2026-10-05).

### Whose tactics play its turns: a choice a companion (issue 46; built 2026-10-05, and its box on the game's tactics page the same day; seen in play 2026-10-05 in Soldier's Peak: the tool's choices, a change in the middle of a fight, and the file written and read back with a manual save and an autosave; and the box seen in play that day)

The user, 2026-10-05: "what if there was an auto option for a party member so that instead of the player having to pick they just let the tactics do the work for that turn (or every turn of that character as the case may be). ... the general combat can be done by the combat AI, just bound by a turn." Of where the choice lives: "Should probably add a checkbox to the tactics page, 'use when turn-based', off by default, per character. Should be changeable even during battle, applies to character's next turn." Of the player's own character: "We can skip the hero for now if that requires additional development." And of where it is kept: "I think we have functionality for a cosave right?", "This is also a good opportunity to exercise the cosave".

So, with one mechanism for it (`src/core/PartyTactics.h`; the machine asks it where it asked the setting):

- **A choice a companion: its tactics play its turns, or they wait for the player.** Chosen on, its turn is an AI's in every way a companion's was under `party = tactics`: told to choose, the hero kept selected meanwhile, its own commands let through and charged to its turn, told again while it has time, and its turn bound by `max_turn_ms`. The log's first line of the turn says which, as it did: `round 2: Alistair's turn (party, AI)` or `(party, yours)`.
- **Off by default, and `[turns] party` is what a companion starts as.** A companion nothing was chosen for in the playthrough goes by the setting, read as it is at the time: `manual` (the default), its turns wait for the player; `tactics`, its tactics play them. A choice made for it wins over the setting, either way: on under `manual`, and off under `tactics`. So a playthrough with nothing chosen plays exactly as it did before there were choices.
- **A choice applies from that companion's next turn.** A turn begins where the machine hands it out (`TurnMachine::BeginTurn`, the frame the log says `round N: X's turn (party, ...)`), and it is played to its end by what was chosen for its creature as it began: chosen on in a turn the player is playing, that turn still waits for orders, with End and Delay as before, and the next is its tactics'; chosen off in a turn its tactics are playing, they play it out, and the next waits. A choice made between two of its turns is the next one's, in the same round if that turn is still to come. A turn put off (Delay) is handed out again when it comes at the round's end and begins then, by what is chosen by then: nothing of it was spent. A turn a conversation interrupted goes on the way it began.
- **A change of the setting itself is still taken at once** for a companion that goes by it: its turn under way is handed to whoever plays it now, as since the second play test (2026-09-30: a turn left waiting on an order for a companion its AI now played stalled the fight). So the two differ in when they take: a companion's own choice, from its next turn, as the user decided; the setting, in the turn under way, as it always did. One with a choice of its own is not touched by the setting, in a turn or between two. **The user's to decide** whether the setting should wait for the next turn too, now that a turn under way knows how it began and can no longer stall: one rule, and a script that sets `party = tactics` in the middle of a companion's waiting turn would then have to end that turn itself.
- **Never the hero.** The hero is the player's own character, the scripts' `GetHero`, as the scan marks it (`Combatant::hero`): not the creature the player controls just now (`GetMainControlled`) nor the party's leader (`GetPartyLeader`), either of which may be a companion. Its turns are the player's whatever is chosen or set; nothing is kept for it, a choice for it is refused, and a choice a save holds for its tag is asked for nobody. A companion the player happens to control is a companion still: its choice applies, and the hero is the one selected on its turn. The hero's own turn on tactics is the issue's second step (the game runs only a partial round for the creature the player controls), not built.
- **Kept with the playthrough, in the game's saves** (`src/game/CoSave.cpp`, over DAOLib's `dao::saves`): each time the game has written a save the choices are written into a file of the mod's in that save's folder, `turnbased.daolib`, and when a playthrough comes into the world they are read from the save loaded, in place of whatever was chosen in the session before. A save with no such file (one from before this, or from a game without the mod), a file this build does not read, and a new game all mean nothing chosen. The file is text, by the follower's tag (the scripts' `GetTag`), with the format's version first and `end` last:

    ```
    turnbased 1
    tactics on gen00fl_alistair
    tactics off gen00fl_wynne
    end
    ```

    It is read whole or not at all: another version, a line of any other kind, a tag twice, bytes that are no text, anything after `end`, or no `end` (a file cut short) and the save is taken as one with nothing chosen, the log saying why. It is written with nothing chosen too (`turnbased 1`, `end`), so that a save made over another in the same slot never keeps the older one's file. Nothing of this is in `turnbased.ini`.
- **The player chooses on the game's tactics page: a box, "Use when turn-based"** (built 2026-10-05 on DAOLib's issue 27 and seen in play the same day: the box on each companion's page as the save had it, none on the hero's, and a click making the choice; a click in a fight is still to see; `dev/UI.md`, "The tactics page's box"). One on each companion's page, beside the tactics it switches on: ticked, that companion's tactics play its turns. It is the mod's own element on that screen, the screen's own toggle with a caption, and changes nothing of the game's. What it shows is what will apply at the companion's next turn, chosen or not: not ticked for one nothing was chosen for under `party = manual`, ticked under `party = tactics`; so the box is how the choice looks, and `[turns] party` only what the boxes start as. A click is the choice, made at once and kept with the next save; the page opens in a fight too (the game pauses for it), and a click there, in the companion's own turn or another's, is its next turn's, as above. **None on the hero's page**, nor on the page of one whose tag a choice could not be kept by. A companion left at the camp has its box there too: the choice is by its tag, whether or not it is one of the four out.
- **A test tool makes the same choice** (`turnbased_auto`, through Eluvian; `dev/TESTING.md`), by a companion's id or name, as the box's click does; a page that is open shows it at the next frame. `inspect turnbased.state` says `auto` on each party member's card.

**Not built, and what happens today** (issue 46, "Taking a turn back": "an order from the player during that character's turn still has to win"). The gate has no rule for it: in a turn a companion's tactics play, the player's order for that companion is one more command of the turn, let through and charged to its bar as its AI's own would be, with an AI's leave to close in on what is out of reach and none of the refusals a player's order is told ("Too far", "No action left"); the turn stays its tactics', which are told to choose again after it with what is left. To give such an order the player selects the companion, and from then the game's scripts give it only the controlled creature's partial AI for the rest of that turn (by the scripts as read; not tried). End does nothing on a turn the player does not play, and unticking is the next turn's. Taking the turn over at the gate would touch what the gate holds an AI's turn by in a score of places (an AI that has passed, a kept action, the turn's time limit, which refusals are said), so it is left as a step of its own: a scripted case holds today's behaviour so that changing it is a decision (`tests/test_partytactics.cpp`).

## What stays the engine's

Each of the game's 43 command types (its `commands.gda`, as DAOLib names them) has a cost (`src/core/Rules.cpp`, `Classify`):

| Cost | Types |
|---|---|
| The action, a combat action | attack, use ability (a talent, a spell), use item (a potion), fly |
| Movement | move to a point or an object, move through points, move away, follow, random walk, the player steering |
| Free, on one's own turn | use object (a door, a chest, a lever), interact, lock, unlock, give, take, pick up; switch or draw weapons, equip, unequip, drop, turn to face |
| A pass, from a script | wait: an AI choosing to do nothing; it costs the time it waits, and ends the turn only when that is more than is left ("The AI's turn") |
| Always let through | an effect executing, a death blow, jumps, barks, animations, sounds, conversations, appearing and vanishing, shops, and a wait nobody chose (the scripts' `SetActive` queues one) |

The last row is what happens to a creature, not what it chooses, and goes through at any time. A creature knocked down on its turn gets up first, then acts.

Whose a command is comes from the command itself: the player's orders carry a flag (`Command::PlayerIssued`), a script's come through `AddCommand`, and the rest are the engine's (DAOLib's reading of the queue's ten callers). A refused command is freed by the mod, since the queue always takes what it is given and no caller frees it after.

**Conversations and cutscenes** mid-fight (a surrender, a boss's speech) stand the mod aside. Their scripts move people about, so nothing is refused while they play. When combat resumes, whatever anyone was left doing stops, and the current turn goes on with what it had left: its action spent or not, its movement, and no second turn-start event.

**Menus** (inventory, character sheet) over the fight hold everything as it is. The menu pauses the game itself, and the mod picks up where it was after.

**The game's auto-pause** on combat is a script (`module_core.nss`, `EVENT_TYPE_AUTOPAUSE`) that toggles pause half a second after combat starts. It would unpause a turn-based fight, so the script command is ignored during one.

**Loading** a save or an area drops any fight without touching its objects, since they are gone.

## Turning it on and off

Turn-based combat is turned on or off only outside combat ("can't change during battle"), by the box in the game's Options or by a key. The answer floats over the one the player controls (while it lives; else over who stands for the party: "Who is in the fight") and is written to `turnbased.ini`. The key was `Ctrl+T` until 2026-10-03; it has none now until the player binds one (the user: "I don't think we necessarily need one for toggling turn-based on / off. There's already a checkbox in game settings", and of its row kept in the controls with no key, "Yeah we can keep it unbound I guess"). The key is one of the game's own since 2026-10-01 (DAOLib's `dao/Input.h`): listed in the options' Controls tab under "Turn-based" as "Toggle turn-based combat", bound there like any key, and kept in the game's `KeyBindings.ini` (`TurnBasedToggle_0`). `[keys] toggle` is the key it has until the player binds another. A key the game's bindings are not known to take (the number pad, Pause, a key by its code) is polled as before and is not in the controls. The rest of the settings are in that file, which is re-read when it changes. The switch itself is in the game's Options too: a "Turn-based" section on the Game tab, headed by the game's own gold bar, with a box, "Turn-based combat", the user's call of 2026-10-01 ("not controls, gameplay tab i think, its own section w/ a toggle"), through DAOLib's `dao/Options.h`. The box is the game's own checkbox, ticked by what the setting is when the screen opens; a click changes the setting at once (not at the screen's OK) and writes `turnbased.ini`, as the key does; in a fight the click is refused, the box goes back, and its tooltip says "Changes outside a fight only".

## Also needed, and what turn-based players will expect

Filling in what a turn-based mode usually has, by how much it matters:

- **An end-turn control.** Built as a button on the turn bar and a key of its own (Enter); the pause key, which was it at first, pauses again.
- **Knowing it is your turn.** Built as floating text and selection. The bar and a sound would be better; the game's GUI sounds are reachable from a movie (`FSCommand:PlayGUISound`).
- **Seeing what is left.** Action and movement remaining, under the bar or on the portrait. The data is in `TurnView`.
- **The camera going to whoever acts.** `camera_follow`, through DAOLib's camera (`src/game/Camera.cpp`). In the tactical view it pans to whoever acts, eased, and never turns: only the player turns it. Close in, it follows whoever acts, enemies too: it comes round behind each party member as their turn begins, and looks at an enemy from one of six places round it, three behind it and three in front, or stays with the party and turns to it where it has no place to stand (issue 33; the rules below).
    - **It stands back by the creature's size** (issue 33; core's `CloseDistanceOf`, asked for each creature by `CameraDirector::SizeAsks`). On the turn of a creature that is not of the party, close in, the camera's distance at the player's own zoom is the game's 3.0 m times the larger of two measures of the creature's model's box, each against a person's own: its length (the larger of its two sides across) against a person's across, 1.2 m, and its height against a person's height, 1.8 m (a person's box is 1.05 to 1.24 m across with its arms out and 1.60 to 1.83 m high). Never under the game's own and never past 14 m, since the game's camera tests no wall and no ground at a distance it was not made for. The box is the engine's (DAOLib's `dao::world::ModelBoxOf`: the one the renderer keeps for the model, in its rest pose). One plain rule for every body, with no case for any creature: a long, low body stands the camera back by its length, a tall one by its height. By the boxes DAOLib saw in play: a person the game's own (3.1 m at the widest of them), a giant spider 5.75 m, a deepstalker 6.35 m, a bronto 9.5 m, an ogre 10.0 m (by its arms' span, its box being its rest pose), and the high dragon, whose box is its spread wings, the 14 m. Too far is the mild failure (a creature small in the picture, the fight round it in view) and too close the bad one. The distance is set apart from the zoom (`dao::camera::SetCloseDistance`), so the zoom stays the player's and the tactical view never comes of it; on a party member's turn, and when the fight ends, it is the game's own again. A model that gives no box goes by its feet to its head node, and one with neither has the game's own. **What came before, each seen in play:** the feet to the head alone (the high dragon whole in the frame at 12.6 m; a giant spider, by that measure no bigger than a person, with its abdomen across the frame); then the largest of the box's three sides against a person's height (commit `3f0f0f0`, played 2026-10-05 in the Dead Trenches with the creatures staged: a bronto at 6.3 m good, all of it from behind and the party member beside it in view; a giant spider's 3.8 m not enough, its abdomen across half the picture in one frame of its turn, since a length against a person's height undersells a long body). **The rule as it stands, seen in play 2026-10-05** (commit `d1ff8f4`): a lone giant spider's turns at 5.8 m show all of it with its web and the two it faces in view, about a third of the frame wide; an ogre's turn at 10 m in a hall shows all of it, its ram and the party member before it. A bigger person stands a little further back than a small one (a man in armour 3.25 m, by the party's boxes): that is the rule doing its work, and the two constants stay.
    - **And no further than its line to the creature is clear** (issue 33; core's `ChooseStand`; built 2026-10-05). The camera may stand in three places behind an enemy, liked in this order: 40 degrees round to its right, 40 degrees round to its left, and straight behind it (and in three in front of it where none of those is a place to stand: the item after next). For each, the engine is asked what a line from the creature's head to where the camera would stand at the whole distance its size asks, at the player's zoom, first meets (DAOLib's `dao::world::FirstHit`), among the ground's two collision groups (0x8 and 0x20) and placeables (0x4). The camera takes the first place whose line is clear the whole distance; if none is, the place whose line is clear the furthest (the earlier of two alike), at that distance less half a meter, and never nearer than the game's own distance. One line a place, three a turn, with no stepping along them. What it is for: until then a side was either clear at the whole distance or not, and with neither side clear the camera went behind, untested. On one of an ogre's turns at 10 m (`d1ff8f4`, played 2026-10-05) both sides were blocked and 10 m straight behind it was the far side of a wall: the frame was of the wall, the ogre's name floating in the middle of it and the ogre not in the picture. The engine's orbit tests no wall and no ground. **Which groups, and why** (`kViewGroups` in `src/game/Camera.cpp`): the lines were first asked of the area's line of sight (`dao::world::LineOfSight`, commit `aa280db`), which is made for creatures seeing creatures and asks only placeables and group 0x20; a shape in group 0x8 alone is not there for it. The log's line says what cut each line that was cut: the shape's groups, and whether it is ground or which object. **What the line does not see:** scenery with no collision at all (the Broodmother's cave, below), which is what the ground under a place is asked for; it is one line from the creature's head, where the picture is as wide as the screen, which is what the half meter is for; ground rising behind the creature cuts the line higher than the camera then stands. Anything of the groups asked cuts a line, a placeable that blocks no creature's sight too (a statue; a low chest is under a line that starts at the head). The distance is set as the turn begins and stays through it, wherever the creature walks.
    - **What play showed of the lines** (2026-10-05, commit `96d6445`, which for that build asked each line of every collision group as well, where the ground's and placeables' met nothing). In a built hall the measure works. A lone ogre's four turns, 10 m asked: from its left at the whole 10.0 m (its right clear to 3.2 m, its left to 10.0 m, behind to 5.7 m); from behind at 10.0 m (its right clear to 8.8 m, its left to 2.9 m, behind to 10.0 m); from behind again (4.4 m, 6.8 m and 10.0 m); and from its right with all three clear. The frame of the third is the ogre from behind with the hall and the party member before it, no wall in the way. The Broodmother's cave has no collision in any group: on nine of her turns all three lines were clear to the whole 14 m, with every group asked too, and the frame was from inside the rock as before. No ray meets that rock, so no line sees it: the next item is what is done for it.
    - **A place to stand: in front where there is none behind, and with the party where there is none at all** (issue 33; core's `ChooseStand`, `IsPlace` and `YawToward`; built 2026-10-05 and seen in play that day at the Broodmother, whose rock reads `over no ground` behind her and on both sides, so that the camera stands in front of her to her right: `dev/images/turn-camera-broodmother-front-2026-10-05.jpg`). The user, 2026-10-05, of a creature set into scenery: "Yeah don't move the camera stay with the party"; and then, correcting it: "When is the creatures turn the camera should try to keep the creature in frame." and "I think the broodmother is a special case. What if we just turn the camera front instead of back with an offset? Nothing blocking her from the front I think". So, with no case for the Broodmother or for any creature: **a place to stand** is one with ground a creature could walk on beneath where the camera would be, no further under the creature's own feet than 5 m (a storey: ground further down is a pit's bottom, a level below, or the floor of somewhere else under scenery with no collision). The engine is asked by a ray straight down from where the camera would stand at that place, its line's shortening counted (`dao::world::Raycast` among the ground's groups: the first thing it meets). A place without it is no place at all, whatever its line says. **The places are six, in this order:** behind the creature to its right, behind it to its left and straight behind it, chosen among as above; then, only where none of those three is a place to stand at all (a view from behind that is shortened is still a view from behind), in front of it to its right by the same 40 degrees mirrored, in front of it to its left, and straight in front, by the same rule among the three. **Where there is no place at all**, the camera does not go to the creature: it stays with the party, on the one the player acts as and at the game's own distance, by the same path as on a party member's turn, and turns so that it looks from there toward the creature. The Broodmother is the first case: the three places behind her are inside her cave's rock. Cost: a line and a ray down a place, so six raycasts a turn of a creature not of the party, and twelve on a turn where the front is asked. Known and accepted: a creature on a bridge or a ledge with a drop on every side keeps the camera with the party. Not known until played: what a ray down from inside rock that has no collision reads as (no ground, ground that is not walkable, or the floor of somewhere else far below, each of which is no place; walkable ground close below would be a place, and the rule could not see the rock), and whether the floor close under a hall's walls reads as walkable, which a place taken short of a wall needs. Issue 33 stays open for the tilt.
- **The fight's speed** (`[turns] speed`, 1; 0.25 to 4; the Fight speed slider in the game's options, whose stops are 0.5, 1, 2 and 3). A big fight is long when every creature animates in full: the game runs faster or slower through a turn-based fight, everyone's turns alike, through DAOLib's `dao::clock::SetSpeed` (the world's timer and the frame's, so logic, animation and sound together; the Flash UI keeps its own clock). Conversations and everything outside a fight run at 1. The mod writes the speed only when it changes, so at 1 never. Built 2026-09-30 as `ai_turn_speed`, for an AI's turns alone, as BG3 speeds enemies' turns up; the user, 2026-10-02: "shouldn't turn speed apply to everyone? we have one setting for combat speed right?", and that setting is gone.
- **Delay.** Built 2026-10-02 and kept by the user: a party member puts its turn off to the round's end, before it has spent anything, once a round; the turn it then takes is whole (`dev/REBALANCE.md`). A button above End on the turn bar. Since 2026-10-03 it has a key of the game's own too, as End turn has ("Delay turn" in the controls under Turn-based, `[keys] delay`), with none until the player binds one (issue 18).
- **Surprise.** A party that starts a fight from stealth might get a free round, as in D&D; enemies who ambush likewise. Stealth is readable from the scripts.
- **Opportunity attacks.** Walking away from an adjacent enemy provokes a free attack in most turn-based games. Without them, melee creatures cannot hold anyone and archers kite freely. DAO has melee rings (`GetCreaturesInMeleeRing`) to detect leaving; the free attack is an `AddCommand` out of turn the gate lets through. A good second step.
- **Sustained abilities and potions.** Most turn-based designs make a toggle or a potion a bonus action. Here a mode costs nothing to turn on or off ("Cooldowns in turns"), and a potion costs the time the game takes to drink it, which leaves the rest of the bar.
- **Cooldowns and durations in rounds.** See Time above.
- **Balance.** DAO is tuned for real time: some fights throw many weak enemies, and regeneration assumes seconds pass. Expect `seconds_per_turn`, `pace`, `round_min_seconds` and initiative to want tuning in play.

## Proposed, not decided (2026-10-01)

Two questions put to the user and not yet answered. Nothing here is built unless it says so.

### Casting time (decided and built 2026-10-01)

The user's rule: "the ritual spells which take 6 s should charge for 2 turns, since 1 turn is 4 s. cast on the 3rd turn. instant & fast & normal can cast on the turn of. slow spells take 1 turn to charge."

That rule is the bar's own arithmetic now, and no rule of its own: a spell costs its conjuring and its cast, and what does not fit what is left of the bar is begun now and lands as its caster's later turns have paid the rest. A slow spell (4 s) ordered with a whole bar is cast on the turn; ordered with two seconds left it lands two seconds into the next. A ritual (6 s) takes the rest of this turn and what it still owes of the next. `[turns] charge` makes an ability cost so many turns' worth whatever its own time. (First built in whole turns, by the conjuring time over `cooldown_seconds_per_turn`, rounded up: a slow spell charged one turn and was cast on the second whatever the caster had left. That went with the action clock, issue 50.)

- **Begun:** what is left of the bar goes to it and the turn ends; "Casting" over the caster. A spell that conjures is the engine's own cast from then, held in its conjure (`engine_conjure`); anything else is not queued, and the game layer keeps what it was (the ability, its target, its place).
- **A turn spent on it whole** (a ritual's second, begun late): nothing to order.
- **Cast:** as its caster's turn begins and has paid what was owed, the kept cast is given to it again (or the engine's conjure let go), and the caster has what is left of that turn. It goes on cooldown as any ability used.
- **Interrupted:** a caster held (a stun, a knockdown, paralysis, sleep), fallen or gone before then loses the charge, "Interrupted", nothing spent and nothing on cooldown; the fight's end loses it too. Damage alone does not: what the engine does in real time has not been read. This and the caster keeping its turn were not the user's words, and are theirs to change.
- **Enemies too:** an enemy's slow spell is said a turn ahead, and stunning the caster stops it.
- **Its target gone:** a cast whose target fell while it charged is not given: nothing spent, nothing on cooldown, and the turn goes on. In play it was cast at nothing and put on cooldown (the twelfth play test). It is logged and not shown: "Its target is gone" floated over the character until 2026-10-03, eight to twelve times a fight, most turns ending on a kept attack; nothing is lost when it happens, so the player is not told (the user, issue 8).
- **A kept plain attack is turned on the nearest enemy in reach** (an attack that does not fit is kept, as a spell is; decided by the user, 2026-10-03, issue 22: "A kept plain attack whose target fell is turned on the nearest enemy in reach at no cost (the swing was already begun), as the game's own auto-attack would", for everyone, the party and the AI). Dropped, the turn began with the player's decision lost, 8 to 24 times a fight at Redcliffe in the playthrough. As the turn it lands on begins, the machine names the attacker's enemies still standing in the fight (the other side: for a hostile, the party and its allies), the game layer measures each (alive, hostile to the attacker by the game's own word, not in stealth and with a clear line of sight, how far, and whether in the attacker's reach by the engine's own approach for the pair), and the nearest it can strike without a step is given the attack (`NearestInReach`, `src/core/Rules.h`); it lands for nothing more, what it owed having been paid as the turn began. In reach is what the gate judges an attack's walk by, the engine's own answer: a shot only with a line to its target (an archer with none walks for one, or the engine ends its attack: issue 11). With nobody in reach it is dropped as before: what the turn paid is given back and the turn is handed over as a fresh one is, so an AI is told to choose at once and chooses anew, and a party member waits for an order (the user asked: "what if there are no enemies within range? Does the AI need some kind of a kick to continue acting and not just stand there?"; it gets that kick within two frames). A kept talent or spell stays dropped: its target was its caster's choice. The log says `X's pending attack is turned on Y: its target has fallen` (or `its target is out of the world`, for one the game has made inactive since: issue 44, under "Who is in the fight"), and the event `action_landed` has `detail` `turned on another` and `note` whom. Built 2026-10-03, not yet seen in play.

What the game's data says (`abi_base.gda`, Awakening's `abi_gxa.gda` and `spellconjuringspeeds.gda`, read with DAOLib's `tools/gda.py`): the ability table's `Speed` is a row of the conjuring speeds: 1 instant (0 s), 2 fast (1 s), 3 normal (2 s), 4 slow (4 s), 5 ritual (6 s); that it is used so is read from the names and values, not from a script. No ability of the base game or of Awakening is slow or ritual (one plot monster's summon aside), and the downloadable contents' tables are in archives the tool does not read. So by the rule nothing charges in the game as it ships; `[turns] charge = 10002:1, 13000:1` makes an ability charge so many turns whatever its conjuring time, which is also how it is tested. With action points (below) this is the same thing seen from the other side: a cost of more than a turn's points.

### A move farther than its movement (decided 2026-10-01)

Refused: "refusing too far is right i think" (the user). A click on ground whose path is longer than the movement left is refused, "Too far", before a step, as an attack out of reach is; the walk is the game's own path's length (`path_check`, DAOLib's `FindPath`), found for a creature at rest. ## Action points (the sketch of 2026-10-01; what came of it is the bar of time)

This section is the record of a question, not what is built. It was written when a turn was one action and an allowance of meters, and asks how to make that budget several. The answer the user came to the next day is the bar of time ("a turn is four seconds of time": `dev/REBALANCE.md`), which is what a turn is now, and the one-action rule this starts from is gone (issue 50). Of what follows, a cost by time, attacks repeatable by their weapon's speed, a price on doors and chests, the AI told to choose again and Haste as a matter of time are in the bar; points, the one-talent lockout and a dash are not.

The one-action rule is a budget of one. Action points make it a budget of several, spent on things of different cost. The user's sketch, 2026-10-01, in their words: "each turn an actor should get N base action points. not sure what N should be. maybe it should represent 1s, so 4? this doesn't mean they get to attack 4x or use 4 talents. time to use ability should affects action point consumption. longer cast time = more action points. 1s cast = 1 action point, 4 s = 4 action points. i think action points should allow you to do different things. using a less health poulstice takes an action point. larger ones take more. so you can use health poulstice, drink potion, set trap, etc. and all that takes action points. attacking takes an action point; can't attack again same turn (unless maybe we allow multi-attack w/ a talent or something). talents take action points and lock out other skills so you can't spam multiple skills in a turn. don't want to force end turn after attack / talent, so things like drink potion can come after, though that would be easiest. still needs further thinking an refinement."

**What is settled by that** (the user's, unless marked):

- **4 points a turn, a point a second.** It is the number the rest already stands on: a turn is 4 s for cooldowns (then `cooldown_seconds_per_turn`) and for casting time, so a slow spell (4 s) is a whole turn's points and a ritual (6 s) more than a turn's. Charging (above) is then not a rule of its own but a cost greater than a turn.
- **A cost by time.** An ability costs its conjuring time in seconds, at least 1.
- **One talent or spell a turn**: using one locks the others out for the turn, so cooldowns in turns keep their meaning. Proposed with it: sustained modes are outside the lockout, a point to turn on and nothing to turn off (they cost nothing either way today).
- **Items cost points, more for the greater**: a lesser poultice 1, larger ones more; traps, bombs and the like their own.
- **The turn is not ended by an attack or a talent**: what points are left may still be spent (a potion after the blow). The turn ends when its owner ends it, or when neither points nor movement are left.
- **Movement stays its own budget**, in meters, as now (proposed; the sketch does not fold it into the points).

**What the game's data gives for costs** (read with DAOLib's `tools/gda.py`):

- Abilities: the conjuring speeds in use are instant (0 s), fast (1 s) and normal (2 s): `abi_base`'s `Speed` is 0 for 177 rows, 1 for 330, 2 for 37, 3 for 7. So by time alone nearly every talent and spell is 1 point and a few are 2: casting time gives little to choose between them.
- Weapons: the item table (`bitm_base`) has a `Speed` class: dagger 3; longsword, mace, waraxe 2; greatsword, maul, battleaxe, staff 1; bows 0. It is readable in play as the weapon's range already is (`Table::Items`, read in the gate for an attack's reach), so a cost by weapon needs nothing new of DAOLib.
- Items: the game has no "time" for a poultice or a trap, so their costs would be a small table in `turnbased.ini` with defaults.

**Proposed to the user, and theirs to decide:**

1. **An attack's cost from its weapon's speed, and attacks repeatable while points last**: say a dagger 1, a one-handed weapon 2, a two-handed weapon, staff or bow 3. A flat "one attack, one point" has a greatsword strike as often as a dagger for far more damage, which takes away the game's balance of fast against heavy, and leaves a fighter whose talents are on cooldown spending one point of four. By weapon, a turn of plain attacks is four dagger blows, two sword blows or one of a greatsword: near what four seconds give in real time.
2. **If attacks stay one a turn, something to spend the rest on**: a dash, 1 point for a quarter more movement.
3. **Doors, chests and pickups 1 point.** They are free today only because the other choice was the whole turn.
4. **Haste and slow as a point more or fewer**: they are effects on time.
5. **No banking**: points not spent are lost at the turn's end, to begin with.
6. **Enemies on the same budget**: an AI is told to choose again after each command until its points are gone or it passes. Enemy turns get longer in real time; `ai_turn_speed` is the offset.
7. **Shown**: the points on the turn bar's caption beside the movement, and refusals that give the numbers ("needs 2, 1 left").

**Asked of the user, 2026-10-01, and not yet answered:** attacks one a turn at 1 point, or by weapon speed and repeatable; doors and chests 1 point or free; a dash or not.

**How it would be built**, in two stages: first the budget (`TurnMachine::Budget`'s `actions` becomes points), the costs (`Classify` answers a cost with its kind; the gate gives the machine the weapon's speed and the ability's conjuring time, as it gives the latter now), the one-talent lockout, the AI told again, and the caption; then the items' table, the dash and haste. The gate's hooks and the rest stay as they are.
