# Testing

Two kinds. The rules and the state machine are tested without the game, on every build. Everything that touches the engine is tested by playing and reading `turnbased.log` after. The second half of this page is a session's checklist. Nothing here has been seen in play yet (2026-09-30).

## Without the game

`src/core` has no engine in it, so it is tested whole, in `tests/`, by `tb_tests` (Catch2):

- **Scripted cases**, one behaviour each: the rules (`test_rules.cpp`), the order of turns (`test_turnorder.cpp`), the settings file (`test_settings.cpp`), keys, text, the bar's string (`test_bar.cpp`), whose portrait to get next (`test_portraits.cpp`), and the machine through short fights in a stand-in for the game (`test_machine.cpp` over `World.h`: creatures, a pause flag, a mode, and the machine's effects applied the way `src/game/Bridge.cpp` applies them).
- **Random fights** (`test_fights.cpp`), SkyrimFollowerTactics' "random sequences, with something that knows the answer". An arena makes the creatures act: AIs choose when told to and whenever a command ends; commands walk and then work; attacks kill, knock down; creatures bleed out, vanish, try to act out of turn; the player orders, ends turns, gives orders nothing comes of, opens menus; conversations interrupt; reinforcements join. Every frame and every command, eight promises are checked: only the current combatant may start or run anything of its own; the pause follows the phase; movement holds, give or take a bounded creep; one action a turn plus one refund; one turn a round each; AI turns end, fights end when enemies are down; effects are well-formed; the pause key is taken exactly while a fight holds the world. Eighty fights run with every build (about four seconds, three and a half hours of play); `tb_tests "[fights-long]"` runs a thousand. A failure names its seed and the machine's last forty lines. Floors on what they reach (turns, cuts, refunds, dispatches, conversations, joiners, deaths) fail the run if a change to the arena stops reaching a state.

What the random fights found while being written (2026-09-30), each now fixed and held by them:

- after a conversation mid-fight, creatures a cutscene had set walking went on through others' turns, the current one included;
- a creature joining the fight kept what it was doing as it joined;
- an action given back after a movement cut could be given back again, letting a creature creep on a step at a time;
- a resumed turn started afresh, with its action back and a second turn-start event.

What the first session in play found (2026-09-30), now fixed and held by scripted tests:

- a click the gate refused ("No action left", "Too far") left its order waiting, and a tenth of a second later the world ran for half a second looking for the command; every click after the action was spent did it;
- a party member's turn stayed open after its action while it had movement left, which read as a pause before the next character; ending it there is now the default (`end_turn_when_action_used = true`);
- every creature's "movement speed" read 60: the scripts' property id is the flanking angle in the shipped table; the pace now comes from the appearance table (`SpeedFactor`);
- every enemy chose nothing on its turn once camera follow was on: centring the camera on an enemy just before its AI was poked left the poke unanswered (with it off, the next enemy attacked at once); the camera now moves after the AI has chosen, and an AI that chooses nothing has what it saw logged (`chose nothing: combat state ...`);
- a stunned or pinned enemy's turn waited the condition out and then acted (the machine took the stun for an action under way): conditions now cost turns;
- cooldowns ran in real time while the game was paused for a turn: they are held for turns;
- (the arena, drawing `ai_turn_speed` too) a conversation in combat's first frame, before the fight had started, sent the machine back to a turn start rather than the start: the fight's setup was skipped and effects' time never held (seed 256 of the long run); it starts afresh now;
- a pinned character's turn waited 12 s on the pin's command, End pressed or not, and the attack the player gave it never ran: the engine holds a pinned creature in the effect's command, which effects' time being held no longer lets end. The arena's stunned and pinned creatures idled; they are held in the effect's command now, as the engine does, and an invariant says a held creature's turn never waits on its condition. A fifth of the arena's holds are unnamed (the statuses report nothing, as for a grab); the long run then found an AI poked though held, which chose at once and would have queued behind the effect (seed 127): the machine now looks at the creature before handing it the turn. Twice the arena's own bookkeeping was wrong first (a hold judged when the order first named the creature, during a padded round, not when the machine began its turn), which the failure's last lines showed;
- a character the player controls carried on attacking by itself (its own AI), spending the player's turn before they chose; on the player's turns only the player's orders spend it now (the user: "turn off party AI in turn-based");
- the turn bar's portraits drew grey, since the movie asked for `img://portrait_player0` where the game's loader wants `portrait_player0.dds` (`TextureName`; `dev/images/turnorder-ingame-grey.jpg` is the bar before the fix).

```powershell
.\tools\build.ps1 -Preset core -Test          # src/core and the tests only: no DAOLib, no plugin
.\tools\build.ps1 -Preset core-cov -Coverage  # 64-bit clang-cl; per-file coverage, HTML in build\core-cov\coverage\html
python tools\mutants.py                        # break the core 89 ways, one at a time; each must fail a test
```

**Coverage** (2026-09-30): 90% of `src/core`'s regions, 89% of its lines, before the tests coverage prompted; what it showed unrun led to the arena's knockdowns, deaths mid-turn and vanishing creatures, and to a key-formatting bug (a key with no name was written `0x` and its decimal code). **Mutants** (`tools/mutants.py`): each breaks one promise (the gate letting anyone act, an action never spent, the pause never taken, the turn never passing on, a joiner's place ignored, ...), builds, and runs the suite; a survivor is a promise no test holds. 89 of 89 caught (2026-09-30). A new promise gets a mutant of its own.

A lesson from the second pass: the reach check changed the arena's random draws, and two mutants that the fights had caught survived. The refund limit and a joiner being stopped had been held only by rare sequences, which the new streams no longer reached in seeds 1-80. So a promise the random fights happen to hold gets a scripted test as well (`test_machine.cpp`: "an action stopped short is given back once a turn", "a creature that joins the fight stops what it was doing"), and the mutants are run after any change to the arena, not only to the core. It happened again with the unnamed holds: "a conversation between two turns resumes a turn never begun" survived, and got its scripted test.

**The turn bar's movie** is checked two ways without the game. `tools/test_movie.py` (run by ctest) reads back the SWF shell `tools/movie.py` writes: the header, the stage, the tags, and the font imports byte for byte against `portraits.gfx`'s. `.\tools\snapshot.ps1` plays the built movie in Ruffle, which draws its sample fight outside the game, and saves a PNG to look at (`dev/images/turnorder-sample.png`); it needs a desktop session.

What these do not establish is the engine side: that `src/game` reads and does what the stand-in assumes. That is the checklist below.

# Testing in play

## Setting up

```powershell
.\tools\build.ps1 -Preset debug -Test
.\tools\deploy.ps1          # the game closed: turnbased.dll and DAOLib's loader into bin_ship
```

Logs and settings are in `Documents\BioWare\Dragon Age\daolib\` (on this machine under OneDrive): `turnbased.log`, `turnbased.ini`, `loader.log`, and `crash-<time>.log` if the game crashes. Set `DAOLIB_LOG_LEVEL=debug` in the environment before starting the game for the gate's refusals and every effect applied; info shows the turns.

Beside the log, `turnbased.events.jsonl` has the machine's events as JSON, a line each (`src/core/Events.h`): `fight_began`, `round_started`, `turn_started` (`detail` yours or AI, `value` tenths of a meter to move), `turn_ended` (`detail` why), `turn_lost`, `turn_resumed`, `refused` (`detail` what the player was told, `command` the command refused, `note` its numbers: `12.1 m to walk, 9.0 m left`), `joined`, `fell`, `gone`, `up_again`, `condition_held`, `condition_ended`, `condition_still_held`, `cooldown_held`, `cooldown_released`, `speed_set`, `turn_handed_over` (party control changed mid-turn: `detail` the player's or its AI's), `settings_changed`, `condition_expired` (a hold ran out by itself), `action_given_back` (a command failed without acting: `detail` the failure, `command`), `fight_over`. A `turn_lost` opens a turn that its `turn_ended` closes, with no `turn_started`. Each has `ms` (real), `world_ms` (the fight's clock), `round`, and `id` (unpadded, `0x82`, as the log and Eluvian write ids) and `name` when about a creature. A game's start appends a `session_started` line with its process id; the file starts over past 5 MB. Assert on these rather than the log's prose: they are what a test server (Eluvian) will carry.

DAOLib's probe can stay deployed beside the mod: both hook `QueueCommand` and the pause key, and chain.

## A session, step by step

Each step names what to look for; a line quoted is the log's.

1. **Start the game.** `loader.log` shows `turnbased.dll` loaded. `turnbased.log` has, in order: `turnbased 0.1.0 on DAOLib 0.1.0`, `wrote the default settings to ...` (first run only), `settings from ...`, `detoured CCObject::QueueCommand at 0x0073cd10`, `detoured GameModeController::HandleMessage(UserTogglePauseMessage) at ...`, `ready; ...`. An `error` line here means a hook failed, and the mod stays off.
2. **The toggle, outside combat.** Load a save and press `Ctrl+T`: "Turn-based combat off" floats over the hero, and `turnbased.ini` says `enabled = false`. Again: on. In a fight it must refuse: "... changed only out of combat". The key is the game's own: the log says at start `the toggle's key is one of the game's own (Options, Controls, under Turn-based; TurnBasedToggle in KeyBindings.ini): Ctrl+T until bound there`; Options, Controls has a "Turn-based" header after the game's last section with a row "Toggle turn-based combat" showing Control + T; bound to another key there, the new key toggles and Ctrl+T does not, and `KeyBindings.ini` has `TurnBasedToggle_0`; with a menu, the map or a conversation up the key does nothing. With `[keys] toggle = NumPad5` the log says `... is not one the game's bindings are known to take: polled`, and the key works with no row in the controls. Seen in play 2026-10-01 (play tests 13 and 14): the row, one toggle a press, the refusal in a fight, silence under menus, a rebind kept over a restart, and beside another plugin with keys of its own. **The box in the options** (seen in play the same day, play test 14, all of this): the log says nothing at start unless DAOLib refuses the section (`DAOLib refused the Turn-based section`); the first time the options are opened, `the options' Game tab has the Turn-based section`; the Game tab has a gold bar "Turn-based" under its left column, like the game's "Gameplay", and a box "Turn-based combat" under it, ticked as `turnbased.ini`'s `enabled` is; a click outside a fight unticks it, `turn-based combat off`, and the ini follows at once, Cancel or not; in a fight (Esc, Options) a click leaves the box as it was and the log says `toggle refused: in combat`. A warning `had no room` or `was not as DAOLib read it` is a finding.
3. **A fight starts.** The world freezes when combat begins; "Turn-based combat" floats over the hero. The log has `combat began: turn-based`, a line per creature (`... rank 2, dexterity 14, combat speed 4.66 m/s, 1.00 a person's`), and `the fight: N combatants; *Name 17 (party), ...`: the order, the current first.
    - Check the **pace** values: 1.00 for people, genlocks and hurlocks, more for a wild mabari (1.07) or a shriek (1.58), less for an ogre (0.84); the party's dog has an appearance of its own, 6.99 m/s, 1.50 (13.5 m a turn), seen in the desktop play test (2026-09-30), which took it for a misreading. 0 m/s for a creature means its appearance has no speed in the table, and it moves at a person's pace.
    - With the game's auto-pause on, `a script's ToggleGamePause during a turn-based fight: ignored` about half a second of running time in. If the game unpauses by itself early in the first fight, this is where to look.
4. **Your turn.** "Name's turn" floats in gold and that character should be selected. **Check:** is it selected like a portrait click (check 4 in `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`)? Order an attack. The log says `Name: attack (action, from player)`: the player's orders carry the game's own player-issued flag. `from engine` for a click would mean the flag is not what DAOLib read. A warning `QueueCommand called from 0x..., not one of the ten callers DAOLib knows` names a caller DAOLib's reading missed; send it. With `DAOLIB_LOG_LEVEL=debug`, each `order: ...` line (the click as explore mode took it) is followed by the command it queued, which ties orders to callers. The game runs while the swing plays, then pauses again.
    - `an order but no command while paused: running a moment for it` on every order means the engine queues orders only once the game runs. The mod copes by running it for a moment, but say so: it is worth knowing. `the order queued no command` after an order means it produced nothing at all.
    - The first order also logs `the player's orders reach the queue on the scripts' thread` (or `off`). Send which: off, the reach check below cannot run.
    - **Too far.** On a fresh turn, click an enemy well beyond 9 m and a bit: "Too far" floats, nothing moves, and the log has `Name: attack refused, 14.2 m to walk with 9.0 m left`. An enemy a step or two inside the movement is attacked. At debug, `attack on 0x...: 16.1 m away, reach 3.5 m` gives the distance and the reach: an ogre or a dragon should show more reach, a bow 45 m.
5. **Out of turn.** Select another party member and order it anything: "Not Name's turn", and nothing happens.
6. **Moving.** After the attack, click to move well past 9 m. The character stops and "Out of movement" floats; the log has `Name is out of movement (9.3 of 9.0 m)`. **Check:** it stops at once, not after its walk. Attacking something far away first should stop the walk the same way, keep the action (`the action it was moving for is kept`), and leave an attack on something in reach possible.
7. **End turn.** The pause key ends a party member's turn: `Name's turn: end turn pressed`, then `Name's turn ends: ended by the player`. It must not pause or unpause the game.
8. **An enemy's turn.** Its name floats in red; it acts once, and nobody else moves. **Check:**
    - `Genlock: attack (action, from script)` soon after its turn starts. If the log says `Genlock's turn ends: chose nothing` for every enemy, the synthetic `COMMAND_COMPLETE` is not reaching its AI (check 2 in `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`): set `ai_poke = kickstart` and try again.
    - Enemies that are not acting stand still, including right after being hit.
9. **Rounds.** `round 2: ...` after everyone's turn. A creature that joins mid-fight (reinforcements) gets `Name joins the fight (enemy, initiative 12)`.
10. **Falling.** A party member at 0 health is skipped (`...'s turn ends: fell`, or never begins). A dead enemy leaves the order at the round's end.
11. **The end.** With the last enemy down, `the turn-based fight is over after N rounds: no enemy left standing`; the game runs in real time again and leaves combat on its own.

## The turn bar

Once a fight plays as above. Build the movie (`python tools\movie.py`), deploy (`tools\deploy.ps1` puts `turnorder.swf` in `Documents\BioWare\Dragon Age\packages\core\override\`), and set `turn_bar = true` under `[feedback]` in `turnbased.ini` (read again when it changes).

1. At the main menu or on loading a save: `the turn bar's calls are answered: TB_GetView, TB_EndTurn, TB_Focus`, then `the turn bar's screen is added, and its movie found as turnorder.swf`. If it says the movie is not where the game looks, the override folder is not searched for movies: say so, and try the game's own `packages\core\override\`.
2. A fight starts: `the turn bar is shown`, and the bar is at the top of the screen, looking like `dev/images/turnorder-sample.png`. **Check:**
    - the names' text: in the game's font, or missing (the font import);
    - the party's portraits on their cards, or their initials (`GetValue` and `img://` from our movie);
    - **the enemies' portraits.** As the fight starts, a line per combatant, a tenth of a second apart, current turn first: `Hero's portrait: img://portrait_3 (the game's)` for the party, `Genlock's portrait: img://portrait_12 (rendered)` for the rest. The cards fill in the same order. Look at each: framed like the party's, or black, blank, or the wrong creature. `has no portrait` for everyone means the engine made none. A crash here names `UpdatePortrait`; set `portraits = false` under `[feedback]`, which keeps the party's, and send the crash log. Note a hitch as they render, if any (check 16 in `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`);
    - the portraits stay put while a character walks (the status line's meters change, the cards are not rebuilt);
    - it follows the turns, the current card first;
    - it hides in a conversation or a menu and comes back after, and hides when the fight ends.
3. Click a card: the camera centres on that combatant (`TB_Focus`). Click End turn on a party member's turn: the turn ends, as the pause key ends it.

## Harder cases, once the above holds

- **Inventory mid-fight**: on your turn, open the inventory and drink a potion. The menu pauses the game itself; after closing it, the potion counts as the action, and the fight carries on where it was.
- **A conversation mid-fight** (a surrender): `dialogue in the middle of the fight: standing aside until it ends`, then `back to the fight: the current turn starts again`.
- **Knockdowns and stuns** on the creature whose turn it is: it gets up (at debug, `busy with a execute effect the gate did not see; letting it run`), then acts.
- **Long fights, memory**: every refused command is freed by the mod, whoever queued it (`src/game/Gate.cpp`). Enemies' portrait renderers are freed with their creatures when the area unloads (`dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`). Over a long fight the game's memory in Task Manager should stay flat. A crash soon after a refused command points at the freeing (check 1 in `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`).
- **`party = tactics`**: companions play their own turns; the hero stays selected on theirs.
- **`camera_follow = true`** (experimental): the camera should centre on whoever's turn it is. If the game crashes on the first turn with this on, turn it off and send the crash log. `camera to X` says a pan was asked for; whether it got there follows (`src/game/Camera.cpp`), with DAOLib's reason for the pan's end (`arrived`, `the player`, `retargeted`, `replaced`, `stopped`, `no camera`): `camera on X after N ms (arrived)` at debug when it did; `the camera's pan to X ended after N ms, D m short (why): panned again` (a warning) when it ended short for anything but the player (a retarget only within 200 ms) and was asked for once more; `... ended after N ms, D m short (why, following Y)` at info otherwise. Twice at `ai_turn_speed = 2` the pan to the hero's turn never arrived (play tests 2 and 5): the game's own pull toward the party, which the pan took for the player's, lib-agent found (DAOLib `79d2baa`). A play test should show `arrived` at the hero's turn and no warning.
- **Who is in the fight** (`join_by_sight`, on): where a battle goes on rooms away (Castle Cousland's halls), the fight's line names only those in rooms the party has seen, or attacking or attacked by it, then `N more fighting near, but not the party: they wait outside the fight until they see it or attack it`; one whose room the party walks into `joins the fight`. At debug the scan says each waiting creature's room (`0x7b4 room 12 seen 0 load 1`; seen: 0 never, 1 in view, 2 before): a creature waiting should be in a room that looks black, and none in a room that is drawn. A combatant gone from the scans for four seconds `is no longer fighting`, once, and `is fighting again` if it comes back; `is up again` every few seconds for one that never fell was the bug.
- **A cooldown's sweep**: a talent used shows a full sweep on its quickbar slot, and at each of its user's turns a share recovered: 2 of 3 turns left, a third. `inspect kind=turnbased.state` has `cooldowns[].turns` and `.of`; Eluvian's `inspect kind=creature` has the engine's `cooldown {remaining, total}`.
- **An AI with no room at its target**: `X's attack failed (COMMAND_FAILED_NO_SPACE_IN_MELEE_RING): its action is given back`, then `X chooses again, its action given back`, and what it chose, or `chose nothing`.
- **Who is in a fight, swept**: `python tools\sweep.py` (eluvian-agent's script, taken into this tree; a minute and a half, no computer use; it launches the game through Eluvian's bridge and quits it, and answers 0 only if nothing was wrong; this tree's copy has not been run yet) travels the Elven Ruins from Lyna's autosave and at each fight sets the turn order beside the creatures' rooms and the camera's creature on an enemy's turn. On ec38eb8, 2026-10-01: four fights, nobody in the order without a shown room or a target in the party, no hostile in combat left out, the camera always in a seen room, the room rule's line once, no warning. Worth a rerun after a change to who joins a fight or to the camera.
- **The movement preview** (with `turn_bar = true`; `[feedback] move_preview`): on a party member's turn, with the game waiting for its order, move the cursor over the ground. A glowing gold line, white at its heart, runs from the character along the way it would walk to the cursor and ends in the game's own movement marker, the gold star a move click leaves, in the world on the ground and following the cursor, with the length over it ("6.4 m"); the log at debug says once `the preview's end is the game's movement marker (effect 0x...), left out of the cursor's pick`. A gold ring there instead means the effect could not be placed, which is a finding. The line and the star hold steady with the cursor still, the cursor's ground steady with them (Eluvian's `inspect cursor`), and a click on a gold end walks every time: the first build that drew under the cursor blinked and lost the clicks (play test 16). After a walk ordered from a preview the log sets the two side by side: `the walk taken against the path shown for 0x...: L m walked, K m shown; at most D m from the path, A m along`; D over 0.5 is said at info with `the walk strays from what was shown`, and is a finding (send both lengths and the place). A walk that ends elsewhere says `walked ... to another place than the path shown` and is not judged. Past the movement left there is no marker: the line turns red from a small gold ring on the ground, a red ring lies round its end, and the length reads "13.6 m of 9.0". The marker goes when the line goes. At debug every fifty searches are summed: `the preview's last 50 searches: N ms in all, the slowest M ms`. A click on a red end is refused "Too far", on a gold end it walks, and the two must agree. Round a corner the line bends as the walk does. Over a creature, a door or the interface there is no line; none on an enemy's turn, while the character walks, or with no movement left. Move or turn the camera: the line stays on the ground. At debug the log has `the preview's path for 0x...: N points to (x, y, z), T ms` for the first five searches and any of 20 ms or more. What to look for: the line off the ground's point by a constant offset or scale (the movie's viewport is not as assumed), hitches while the cursor moves (the searches), the line left on the screen after the turn ends.
- **The preview's path on the ground** (the default; `turnbased_settings feedback / move_preview_ground / false` for the line): no line over the scene; a dotted line of small gold rings on the ground, one every 0.2 m, from just outside the character's own ring to just short of the star, bending as the route does and going behind whoever stands in front of them; past the movement left the rings are red (the targeted enemy's ring made small, a little larger than the gold ones), and the end has a red ring half a creature's size and no star. The plate with the length is still over the end. The log at debug says once `the preview's path is marked on the ground: N in reach, M beyond, one every 0.40 m`. Checks: the cursor's ground steady with the marks under and near it (Eluvian's `inspect cursor`) and clicks walk, since each mark is a selection reticle left out of the pick; the marks follow the cursor without trailing or flickering; none left behind after a click, a turn's end, the fight's end or a load; the frame rate in a sweep; a long too-far path (its marks spread to sixty at most). What to judge by eye, with pictures: the marks' size and spacing, whether they read as a path, and how the red ones look as enemy rings made small.
- **A cast that charges**: nothing in the game as it ships charges by the rule, so set one: `turnbased_settings turns / charge / <ability>:1` (or `:2`). Use it on its caster's turn: `X begins to cast ability N: it charges 1 turn and is cast on the next`, "Casting" over it, the turn over, `turnbased.cast_charging`, and `inspect kind=turnbased.state` has it under `charging`. At its next turn: `X's charged cast of ability N is due`, the spell cast, `turnbased.cast_landed`, then its turn as usual with its action. With `:2` the turn between reads `X's turn is spent charging ability N (1 more)`. Stun or knock the caster down between: `X's cast of ability N is interrupted: held`, "Interrupted", `turnbased.cast_interrupted`, and no cast after. A warning `no cast kept for it to be given` or `could not make the command` is a finding.
- **An attack through a shut door**: refused, `X: attack refused, a door is in the way of the walk`, the text "A door is in the way", the action kept; at debug `attack on 0x...: the game finds no path to it (6); a door is in the way`. With `path_check = false` nothing is searched and the game's own answer runs: `use object (free, from script)`, the attack again, and if the creature then stands, `X stood 2500 ms at a use object without a step: stopped; the action waiting behind it is given back`, the turn still the player's. An AI standing at a move it cannot make ends its turn in as long.
- **A door in a fight**: `order use` on a door mid-fight left it shut three times, where the same order opens it outside a fight (the seventh play test), and the try costs the action. The command's end says nothing either way: a use of an object ends `use object ended result -1` whether the door opened or not (all five in that test, two of which opened it). Whether the game lets a door be opened in a fight at all is not known: try the same door in a real-time fight, `[turnbased] enabled = false`. If it opens there, the mod is what stops it.
- **`round_min_seconds = 6`**: the world runs on briefly at the end of short rounds, nobody acting.

## Through Eluvian

Eluvian (`C:\project\DAOEluvianMCP`, eluvian-agent's) is the development bench's MCP server inside the game: with `eluvian.dll` in `daolib\plugins`, an agent drives a fight by HTTP rather than the mouse. Its general tools (inspect creatures, order, script, events, wait_for, scenario) are in its `dev/PLAYBOOK.md`; the mod registers its own on the first frame (`src/plugin/Plugin.cpp`), and `turnbased.log` says `registered with Eluvian (build N): inspect turnbased.state; ...; events turnbased.<kind>, 25 described; 0 refused`. From Eluvian 0.2.0, `events describe=true` lists each `turnbased.*` topic with what it means and what its fields say (core's `Describe`, `src/core/Events.cpp`). Ids everywhere are unpadded strings, `"0x82"`, as Eluvian's own.

**`inspect kind=turnbased.state`**, the fight now (`src/core/State.h`):

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `active`, `phase`, `round`, `world_ms` | whether a fight is on; the machine's phase (`awaiting order`, `AI thinking`, `executing`, `settling`, `turn start`, `idle`, ...); the round; the fight's clock in the world's milliseconds |
| `current` | `{id, name, side, yours}` of whose turn it is (`side` party, ally or enemy; `yours` when the player plays it), null between turns |
| `actions_left`, `movement_left`, `movement_max` | what the current creature has left |
| `speed`, `speed_took` | the game's speed the mod last set (`ai_turn_speed` in an AI's turn, else 1), and whether the game took it (null before any) |
| `held_by_effect`, `last_refusal` | the current creature held in an effect's command; the last refusal logged |
| `order` | the turns to come, the current first: `{id, name, side, rank, health, energy, mana, down, current, yours, round}` (health and energy 0 to 1, `mana` for a mage's) |
| `held` | creatures a condition holds: `{id, name, condition, turns_owed, on_clock}` |
| `cooldowns` | abilities held for turns: `{id, ability, turns}` |

`player_turn` mirrors `current.yours` for one build more, then goes.

**Tools.** `turnbased_end_turn` (no arguments): ends the player's turn, as Space or End; an error when it is not a turn the player plays. `turnbased_seed {"seed": 42}`: initiative's rolls from now on (a joiner's, the next fight's). `turnbased_settings`: no arguments reads `turnbased.ini`; `{"section": "turns", "key": "ai_turn_speed", "value": "2"}` sets one and applies it at once, answering the parse's warnings. The game's speed is the mod's: set it through `ai_turn_speed`, not Eluvian.

**Events**, on Eluvian's bus as `turnbased.<event>`, the same as `turnbased.events.jsonl`'s lines (above): each `{ms, world_ms, round, event, id?, name?, detail?, command?, note?, value}`. `turnbased.refused` is `{id, name, detail, command, note}`: `detail` the player's floaty (`Too far`, `Not Dog's turn`, `No action left`, `No movement left`, `Held`), `command` the command refused, `note` the numbers (`12.1 m to walk, 9.0 m left`). Wait on `turnbased.turn_started` with `detail` `yours` for the player's turn, `turnbased.turn_ended` for its end, `turnbased.fight_over` for the end.

**What a real mouse still has to do** (Eluvian's `order` goes through the queued command, not explore mode's click): the order lines and "the order queued no command"; a talent asking for its target (the quickbar's message); Space and the bar's End button themselves (the tool reaches the same end of turn).
