# Testing

Two kinds. The rules and the state machine are tested without the game, on every build. Everything that touches the engine is tested by playing and reading `turnbased.log` after. The second half of this page is a session's checklist. Nothing here has been seen in play yet (2026-09-30).

## Without the game

`src/core` has no engine in it, so it is tested whole, in `tests/`, by `tb_tests` (Catch2):

- **Scripted cases**, one behaviour each: the rules (`test_rules.cpp`), the order of turns (`test_turnorder.cpp`), the settings file (`test_settings.cpp`), keys, text, the bar's string (`test_bar.cpp`), whose portrait to get next (`test_portraits.cpp`), and the machine through short fights in a stand-in for the game (`test_machine.cpp` over `World.h`: creatures, a pause flag, a mode, and the machine's effects applied the way `src/game/Bridge.cpp` applies them).
- **Random fights** (`test_fights.cpp`), SkyrimFollowerTactics' "random sequences, with something that knows the answer". An arena makes the creatures act: AIs choose when told to and whenever a command ends; commands walk and then work; attacks kill, knock down; creatures bleed out, vanish, try to act out of turn; the player orders, ends turns, gives orders nothing comes of, opens menus; conversations interrupt; reinforcements join. Every frame and every command, the design's promises are checked: only the current combatant may start or run anything of its own; the pause follows the phase; movement holds, give or take a bounded creep; no action begins with no time left on its bar; one turn a round each; AI turns end, fights end when enemies are down; effects are well-formed; the pause key is taken exactly while a fight holds the world; a wait takes its own length of a bar of time or nothing, an AI's on its own turn alone, and is never let through in a turn (issue 31: the arena's waits have the AI's two lengths, 2 s and 1 s, and now and then none); a creature an effect's command holds as its turn comes waits no longer than a turn's worth on it and, let go before, has the time the hold ran gone from its bar (issue 30); and a turn begins with time rolled over only if its creature's last turn was ended by its actor, every turn ending for a reason that has its class (issue 29); and no clock of the mod's ends a turn the player plays, a creature at its own mode's command is not held, and End ends a turn the player plays and stops what was still running (issue 51). A second test plays twenty-five fights with what the engine's perception decides (issue 29; the stand-in's creatures have the perception the game layer would read): about half the enemies have not noticed the party, and notice when struck or by themselves; and now and then a combatant goes where the scan's own rules no longer find it fighting, perceived or not, and comes back or is lost for good. One that has noticed nobody is never told to choose, and its turn is skipped only after its beat; nobody is taken out of a fight while perception keeps it there, and nobody not found fighting is brought into one. These are a test of their own, on a stream of their own draws, so that the eighty default fights are draw for draw what they were: put into three in ten of those, the fights took other courses and a floor was no longer reached. Eighty fights run with every build (some hours of play, in seconds); `tb_tests "[fights-long]"` runs two thousand more: a thousand, five hundred with hostiles unaware and combatants out of reach, and two hundred and fifty with the party's tactics chosen and changed in them (issue 46, below), then as many of those among hostiles unaware and combatants out of reach as well. Until issue 50 (2026-10-05) there was a second clock, one action and meters a turn (the action clock), with lots of its own beside these: eighty, twenty-five and thirty with every build, and in the long run a thousand, five hundred with hostiles unaware and two hundred and fifty with the party's tactics chosen, 1,750 of its 3,500. They went with it. Every fight on the bar that was played then is draw for draw the fight it was; the long run's two hundred and fifty with tactics chosen and nobody unaware are new to it, on the bar where the action clock's stood. (Draw for draw until issue 51, the same day: since then End pressed in the middle of an action stops it and no clock ends a turn the player plays, and every lot takes other courses; "No clock on the player's turn", below.) A seed or a figure this page gives as the action clock's is of a lot that is gone, and replays nothing now. A failure names its seed and the machine's last forty lines. Floors on what they reach (turns, cuts, refunds, dispatches, conversations, joiners, deaths) fail the run if a change to the arena stops reaching a state.

What the random fights found while being written (2026-09-30), each now fixed and held by them:

- after a conversation mid-fight, creatures a cutscene had set walking went on through others' turns, the current one included;
- a creature joining the fight kept what it was doing as it joined;
- an action given back after a movement cut could be given back again, letting a creature creep on a step at a time;
- a resumed turn started afresh, with its action back and a second turn-start event.

What the first session in play found (2026-09-30), now fixed and held by scripted tests:

- a click the gate refused ("No action left", "Too far") left its order waiting, and a tenth of a second later the world ran for half a second looking for the command; every click after the action was spent did it;
- a party member's turn stayed open after its action while it had movement left, which read as a pause before the next character; ending it there became the default (`end_turn_when_action_used = true`; the setting went with the action clock, issue 50: on the bar a turn ends by itself when nothing of it is left to spend);
- every creature's "movement speed" read 60: the scripts' property id is the flanking angle in the shipped table; the pace now comes from the appearance table (`SpeedFactor`);
- every enemy chose nothing on its turn once camera follow was on: centring the camera on an enemy just before its AI was poked left the poke unanswered (with it off, the next enemy attacked at once); the camera now moves after the AI has chosen, and an AI that chooses nothing has what it saw logged (`chose nothing: combat state ...`);
- a stunned or pinned enemy's turn waited the condition out and then acted (the machine took the stun for an action under way): conditions now cost turns;
- cooldowns ran in real time while the game was paused for a turn: they are held for turns;
- (the arena, drawing `ai_turn_speed` too) a conversation in combat's first frame, before the fight had started, sent the machine back to a turn start rather than the start: the fight's setup was skipped and effects' time never held (seed 256 of the long run); it starts afresh now;
- a pinned character's turn waited 12 s on the pin's command, End pressed or not, and the attack the player gave it never ran: the engine holds a pinned creature in the effect's command, which effects' time being held no longer lets end. The arena's stunned and pinned creatures idled; they are held in the effect's command now, as the engine does, and an invariant says a held creature's turn never waits on its condition. A fifth of the arena's holds are unnamed (the statuses report nothing, as for a grab); the long run then found an AI poked though held, which chose at once and would have queued behind the effect (seed 127): the machine now looks at the creature before handing it the turn. Twice the arena's own bookkeeping was wrong first (a hold judged when the order first named the creature, during a padded round, not when the machine began its turn), which the failure's last lines showed;
- a character the player controls carried on attacking by itself (its own AI), spending the player's turn before they chose; on the player's turns only the player's orders spend it now (the user: "turn off party AI in turn-based");
- the turn bar's portraits drew grey, since the movie asked for `img://portrait_player0` where the game's loader wants `portrait_player0.dds` (`TextureName`; `dev/images/turnorder-ingame-grey.jpg` is the bar before the fix).

**Coverage** (2026-09-30): 90% of `src/core`'s regions, 89% of its lines, before the tests coverage prompted; what it showed unrun led to the arena's knockdowns, deaths mid-turn and vanishing creatures, and to a key-formatting bug (a key with no name was written `0x` and its decimal code). **Mutants** (`tools/mutants.py`): each breaks one promise (the gate letting anyone act, an action never spent, the pause never taken, the turn never passing on, a joiner's place ignored, ...), builds, and runs the suite; a survivor is a promise no test holds. 89 of 89 caught (2026-09-30). A new promise gets a mutant of its own.

A lesson from the second pass: the reach check changed the arena's random draws, and two mutants that the fights had caught survived. The refund limit and a joiner being stopped had been held only by rare sequences, which the new streams no longer reached in seeds 1-80. So a promise the random fights happen to hold gets a scripted test as well (`test_machine.cpp`: "an action stopped short is given back once a turn", "a creature that joins the fight stops what it was doing"), and the mutants are run after any change to the arena, not only to the core. It happened again with the unnamed holds: "a conversation between two turns resumes a turn never begun" survived, and got its scripted test.

**A hold costs the time it holds** (issue 30, 2026-10-04). The arena's holds were already of two kinds: an effect's command that lasts while its effect does (a fifth of them with no condition the statuses name), and a knockdown's, which ends by itself after 0.3 to 2.5 s of the world's time. What changed in it: an effect on a creature on its own clock now wears off as that clock moves and is passed by when the shared clock moves, as DAOLib's does, so that a hold its creature's bar pays for ends within the turn; and promise 13 replaced "a turn never waits past the grace on an effect no condition names". Of the eighty default fights, 170 holds let go with the turn going on (148 of them held from the turn's start, their time checked against the bar) and 91 turns were lost to one that outlasted the bar (the action clock's eighty: 153 and 8), where every one found so had lost the turn after two seconds. The long run then found a walk to a kept attack's target given after its creature's turn was over (seed 354): the attack kept and the bar spent on it, the walk asked for with the next step's effects, and its creature thrown before that step, so that the hold had nothing of the bar to take and the turn was lost in the step that handed the walk out. A turn's end now drops a walk not yet given (scripted too: "thrown as it was to walk to its kept attack's target..."). The new draws also reached a case the arena's own check had wrong all along (seed 300): a step of an action's own effect moving its target's clock in the frame End was pressed on it, the turn over by the time the step was applied. Issue 29's long run met the same case by another road (its seed 86, stopped as too long) and mended the check first: it takes the target as the frame began. Twenty mutants for the rule, and three gone with the two numbers it replaced (`kEffectGraceMs`, `kGetUpMs`). Three of the twenty survived their first run and got their tests: a hold taken for its creature's own action's effect as well, effects' time running twice with it (the effect wore off in half the time, and nothing counted the frames); and a creature thrown after it had acted, while its turn waited or its AI thought, which a turn begun held does not reach. And the lesson above once more: with the arena's draws changed, "after its action is kept, a creature may still start something" survived, 422 of 423. Only the random fights had held it (seed 47), and none of the eighty reached it any more; it has its scripted test now ("its action kept for its next turn, a creature starts nothing more in this one").

**Where a hold and an AI's wait meet** (issues 30 and 31, rebased one over the other, 2026-10-04). A wait is taken of the bar at the gate, at once and with the world still; a hold as it runs. Read against each other: a held creature's wait never reaches the wait's rule, the gate refusing it with every other order behind the effect, so it takes nothing and passes nothing, and the hold alone has that stretch; a wait paid before its creature was thrown is time before the hold; and a wait is no command of its own let through, so a creature that has only waited and is then thrown is held, not taken for one at its action's own effect (which a count of its waits among its commands would have made it, and then nobody's: neither held nor charged, standing until `max_action_ms`). Nothing was found wrong; two scripted cases hold it (`test_time.cpp`, "an AI that waits and is then thrown pays for each once...", "a held creature's AI's wait takes nothing and ends nothing...", the second on the action clock too until issue 50, where a wait was a pass), and a mutant each. In the random fights the arena's own check of a wait ("its own length of the bar or nothing") passes over a held creature's as nothing.

**Where a hold meets a hostile that has noticed nobody** (issues 30 and 29). Issue 29 skips such a hostile's turn after a second's beat, and placed that after the look for an effect's command: with a hold given two seconds that cost little, but a hold stood out to the bar's end would have been four seconds of the world running for a turn that is skipped anyway, and then the beat. One that has noticed nobody is not held: its beat, and its turn skipped, whatever the engine has it at; if it notices in its beat with the effect still on it, it is held from then. A scripted case (`test_time.cpp`, "an unaware hostile at an effect's command is not stood out for...") and a mutant. The long run with hostiles unaware then tripped the arena's own count (seed 26): it counted a hold from its creature's turn's start, through the beat in which it had noticed nobody, and found the bar short of it; the arena counts from the moment it notices now, as the machine does. And the rollover's rule met the hold's without a change: a turn lost to a hold rolls nothing over by its class (`core/TurnEnd.h`), and has nothing left that could, the hold having taken the bar; one let go and then ended by its actor rolls over what the hold left. Issue 29's look at everyone, not taken under a menu, met nothing of the hold's: the machine takes nothing up there and its clock stands, so a hold is not charged under a menu, nor under the player's pause, however long (a scripted case, "a hold is not charged while the game stands..."; it had none).

**Where a hold meets an attack the engine has no way to make** (issues 30 and 27). A kept attack given as a hold lets go goes through the gate as one given at a turn's start does, into the same branch, and is asked of the engine there: with no room at its target by then it is not made, the half second this turn paid for it comes back to the bar, and its creature is handed the turn with that and what the hold left; the second the hold ran stays taken. Nothing had to change for it. A scripted case (`test_time.cpp`, "a kept attack given as a hold lets go is asked of the engine like any..."). The other way round, a held creature's own attack with the engine's no: it is refused as held, before the engine's answer is looked at, and so is not counted as an action given back, two of which would end its turn "nothing it can get to" under the hold; the same case holds that, with a mutant ("a held creature's attack the engine has no way to make is one given back, not one refused as held").

The cases of issue 29 and issue 27 that held the old reading of a command in an unaware hostile's beat (`test_perception.cpp`, `test_noattack.cpp`) now hold both: the game's for one still out of combat, the turn's own for one taken into combat since the machine last looked. Beside the log, `turnbased.events.jsonl` has the machine's events as JSON, a line each (`src/core/Events.h`): `fight_began`, `round_started`, `turn_started` (`detail` yours or AI, `value` tenths of a meter to move), `turn_ended` (`detail` why: `unaware` for a hostile's turn skipped because it has noticed nobody to fight), `turn_lost`, `turn_resumed`, `refused` (`detail` what the player was told, `command` the command refused, `note` its numbers: `12.1 m to walk, 9.0 m left`), `joined`, `fell`, `gone`, `up_again`, `condition_held`, `condition_ended`, `condition_still_held`, `cooldown_held`, `cooldown_released`, `speed_set`, `turn_handed_over` (party control changed mid-turn: `detail` the player's or its AI's), `settings_changed`, `condition_expired` (a hold ran out by itself), `action_given_back` (a command failed without acting: `detail` the failure, `command`; or an AI's attack the engine has no way to make was refused before a step: `detail` the engine's answer, `note` whom it was at), `action_pending`, `action_landed` and `action_dropped` (an action kept for a later turn: begun, or a turn spent on it; given to its creature as its time is paid, with `detail` `turned on another` and `note` whom when a plain attack whose target had fallen was given at the nearest enemy in reach; not to land, `detail` why), `awaiting_order` (a turn the player plays has started waiting on an order: `detail` why, `value` the milliseconds of its bar left, `note` the meters), `fight_over`. A `turn_lost` opens a turn that its `turn_ended` closes, with no `turn_started`. Each has `ms` (real), `world_ms` (the fight's clock), `round`, and `id` (unpadded, `0x82`, as the log and Eluvian write ids) and `name` when about a creature. A game's start appends a `session_started` line with its process id; the file starts over past 5 MB. Assert on these rather than the log's prose: they are what a test server (Eluvian) will carry.

## A session, step by step

Each step names what to look for; a line quoted is the log's.

1. **Start the game.** `loader.log` shows `turnbased.dll` loaded (`turnbased.dll: loaded`; `not loaded` for it or for any other plugin means a dll still loose in `daolib\plugins\`, above). `turnbased.log` has, in order: `turnbased 0.1.0 on DAOLib 0.1.0`, `wrote the default settings to ...` (first run only), `settings from ...`, `detoured CCObject::QueueCommand at 0x0073cd10`, `detoured GameModeController::HandleMessage(UserTogglePauseMessage) at ...`, `ready; ...`. An `error` line here means a hook failed, and the mod stays off.
2. **The toggle, outside combat.** The key has no default since 2026-10-03 (`[keys] toggle = None`: its row in the controls is empty until bound, and the box in Options, Game does the same); what follows is with `Ctrl+T` bound there or written in the file, as it was until then. Load a save and press `Ctrl+T`: "Turn-based combat off" floats over the hero, and `turnbased.ini` says `enabled = false`. Again: on. In a fight it must refuse: "... changed only out of combat". The key is the game's own: the log says at start `the toggle's key is one of the game's own (Options, Controls, under Turn-based; TurnBasedToggle in KeyBindings.ini): Ctrl+T until bound there`; Options, Controls has a "Turn-based" header after the game's last section with a row "Toggle turn-based combat" showing Control + T; bound to another key there, the new key toggles and Ctrl+T does not, and `KeyBindings.ini` has `TurnBasedToggle_0`; with a menu, the map or a conversation up the key does nothing. With `[keys] toggle = NumPad5` the log says `... is not one the game's bindings are known to take: polled`, and the key works with no row in the controls. Seen in play 2026-10-01 (play tests 13 and 14): the row, one toggle a press, the refusal in a fight, silence under menus, a rebind kept over a restart, and beside another plugin with keys of its own. **The box in the options** (seen in play the same day, play test 14, all of this): the log says nothing at start unless DAOLib refuses the section (`DAOLib refused the Turn-based section`); the first time the options are opened, `the options' Game tab has the Turn-based section`; the Game tab has a gold bar "Turn-based" under its left column, like the game's "Gameplay", and a box "Turn-based combat" under it, ticked as `turnbased.ini`'s `enabled` is; a click outside a fight unticks it, `turn-based combat off`, and the ini follows at once, Cancel or not; in a fight (Esc, Options) a click leaves the box as it was and the log says `toggle refused: in combat`. A warning `had no room` or `was not as DAOLib read it` is a finding.
3. **A fight starts.** The world freezes when combat begins; "Turn-based combat" floats over the hero. The log has `combat began: turn-based`, a line per creature (`... rank 2, dexterity 14, combat speed 4.66 m/s, 1.00 a person's`), and `the fight: N combatants; *Name 17 (party), ...`: the order, the current first.
    - Check the **pace** values: 1.00 for people, genlocks and hurlocks, more for a wild mabari (1.07) or a shriek (1.58), less for an ogre (0.84); the party's dog has an appearance of its own, 6.99 m/s, 1.50 (13.5 m a turn then, when a person's turn had 9 m), seen in the desktop play test (2026-09-30), which took it for a misreading. 0 m/s for a creature means its appearance has no speed in the table, and it moves at a person's pace.
    - With the game's auto-pause on, `a script's ToggleGamePause during a turn-based fight: ignored` about half a second of running time in. If the game unpauses by itself early in the first fight, this is where to look.
4. **Your turn.** "Name's turn" floats in gold and that character should be selected. **Check:** is it selected like a portrait click (check 4 in `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`)? Order an attack. The log says `Name: attack (action, from player)`: the player's orders carry the game's own player-issued flag. `from engine` for a click would mean the flag is not what DAOLib read. A warning `QueueCommand called from 0x..., not one of the ten callers DAOLib knows` names a caller DAOLib's reading missed; send it. With `DAOLIB_LOG_LEVEL=debug`, each `order: ...` line (the click as explore mode took it) is followed by the command it queued, which ties orders to callers. The game runs while the swing plays, then pauses again.
    - `an order but no command while paused: running a moment for it` on every order means the engine queues orders only once the game runs. The mod copes by running it for a moment, but say so: it is worth knowing. `the order queued no command` after an order means it produced nothing at all.
    - The first order also logs `the player's orders reach the queue on the scripts' thread` (or `off`). Send which: off, the reach check below cannot run.
    - **Too far.** On a fresh turn, click an enemy well beyond what the bar walks (19.6 m for a person: 4 s at 4.66 m a second, and the free meter): "Too far" floats, nothing moves, and the log has `Name: attack refused, N m to walk with 19.6 m left`. An enemy a step or two inside the movement is walked to, and struck then if the bar still has the swing's time, or as its next turn begins if not (`Name begins attack (...): ... it lands on its next turn; it walks to its target now`). At debug, `attack on 0x...: 16.1 m away, out of its reach by the engine's melee approach: 14.9 m to walk, by the engine's path` gives the distance, the kind of approach and the walk, each the engine's own answer (`by the line to where its approach goes` where its creature was at a command already and no path was searched); a blow's line goes on with what it will meet on its target's ring, `; finds room (in reach no, strikes at once no, room once its ring is filled yes)` (issue 27, below). In reach it reads `in its reach by the engine's ranged approach: nothing to walk` however long the path; a shot in range with no line, `out of its reach by the engine's ranged approach (no line of sight)`.
5. **Out of turn.** Select another party member and order it anything: "Not Name's turn", and nothing happens.
6. **Moving.** After the attack, click to move well past the meters the bar has left. The character stops and "Out of movement" floats; the log has `Name is out of movement (N of M m)`. **Check:** it stops at once, not after its walk. Attacking something far away first should stop the walk the same way, keep the action (`the action it was moving for is kept`), and leave an attack on something in reach possible.
7. **End turn.** End on the turn bar, or its key (Enter until bound), ends a party member's turn: `Name's turn: end turn pressed`, then `Name's turn ends: ended by the player`. It must not pause or unpause the game. Delay has a key of its own in the same place ("Delay turn", none until bound in the controls or written as `[keys] delay`): pressed on a party member's whole turn it does what the bar's Delay does, and the log says at debug `the turn was put off by Delay's key`, or `Delay's key when the turn could not be put off`.
8. **An enemy's turn.** Its name floats in red; it acts, and again while its bar has time for more (`X chooses again: ...`), and nobody else moves. **Check:**
    - `Genlock: attack (action, from script)` soon after its turn starts. If the log says `Genlock's turn ends: chose nothing` for every enemy, the synthetic `COMMAND_COMPLETE` is not reaching its AI (check 2 in `dev/DAOLIB_NEEDS.md`): set `ai_poke = kickstart` and try again.
    - Enemies that are not acting stand still, including right after being hit.
9. **Rounds.** `round 2: ...` after everyone's turn. A creature that joins mid-fight (reinforcements) gets `Name joins the fight (enemy, initiative 12)`.
10. **Falling.** A party member at 0 health is skipped (`...'s turn ends: fell`, or never begins). A dead enemy leaves the order at the round's end.
11. **The end.** With the last enemy down, `the turn-based fight is over after N rounds: no enemy left standing`; the game runs in real time again and leaves combat on its own.

## Through Eluvian

Eluvian (`C:\project\DAOEluvianMCP`, eluvian-agent's) is the development bench's MCP server inside the game: with `eluvian.dll` in `daolib\plugins` (in a folder of its own there, `eluvian\eluvian.dll`, once the game is on DAOLib's issue 26), an agent drives a fight by HTTP rather than the mouse. Its general tools (inspect creatures, order, script, events, wait_for, scenario) are in its `dev/PLAYBOOK.md`; the mod registers its own on the first frame (`src/plugin/Plugin.cpp`), and `turnbased.log` says `registered with Eluvian (build N): inspect turnbased.state; ...; events turnbased.<kind>, 27 described; 0 refused` (a kind each of `src/core/Events.h`; thirty until the three `cast_*` events went with the action clock, issue 50). From Eluvian 0.2.0, `events describe=true` lists each `turnbased.*` topic with what it means and what its fields say (core's `Describe`, `src/core/Events.cpp`). Ids everywhere are unpadded strings, `"0x82"`, as Eluvian's own.

**`inspect kind=turnbased.state`**, the fight now (`src/core/State.h`):

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `active`, `phase`, `round`, `world_ms` | whether a fight is on; the machine's phase (`awaiting order`, `AI thinking`, `executing`, `settling`, `turn start`, `idle`, ...); the round; the fight's clock in the world's milliseconds |
| `current` | `{id, name, side, yours}` of whose turn it is (`side` party, ally or enemy; `yours` when the player plays it), null between turns |
| `time_left_ms`, `time_ms` | the current creature's bar of time: what is left of it, and the whole, in milliseconds |
| `movement_left`, `movement_max` | the meters it has left to walk, and the most its turn has |
| `speed`, `speed_took` | the game's speed the mod last set (`[turns] speed` through a fight's turns, else 1), and whether the game took it (null before any) |
| `held_by_effect`, `last_refusal` | the current creature held in an effect's command; the last refusal logged |
| `order` | the turns to come, the current first: `{id, name, side, rank, health, energy, mana, down, current, yours, round}` (health and energy 0 to 1, `mana` for a mage's), and on a party member's card `auto`: its tactics play its turns, from its next one (issue 46). On the current card `yours` is of the turn under way and `auto` of the next, so a choice made in a turn shows in `auto` at once and in `yours` when the next begins |
| `held` | creatures a condition holds: `{id, name, condition, on_clock, owed_ms}`, `owed_ms` the time of its own it still owes |
| `cooldowns` | abilities held on cooldown: `{id, ability, left_ms, of_ms}`, what is left of the time it is held for |
| `charging` | actions kept for a later turn: `{id, ability, owed_ms}`, what each still owes |

**`turnbased_portrait`**, for framing the cards' portraits by eye (issue 32; built 2026-10-04 and seen in play that day: at the camp and on the mountain top, outside a fight, some sixty calls with no crash, a render again with other values showing at once). It renders one creature's portrait now and says everything about it. Any creature by its id, in a fight or out of one, with a card or without.

| Argument | What |
|---|---|
| `id` | the creature, `"0x7d9"` as Eluvian writes ids (or a whole number). Alone: the creature is looked up in the framing table (`src/core/PortraitTable.h`): its appearance's own row, else the game's own portraits-table row for it, else its kind's default |
| `kind` | `"person"`, `"upright"` or `"on_all_fours"`: frame it by that kind's default, the rows passed over (a person's: the game's Default row; the other two: the table's defaults). How a row is set beside its default |
| `distance`, `xAdjust`, `zAdjust` | metres, in the portraits table's terms: the camera stands 3 × `distance` from the head node and the picture is 0.525 × `distance` across; the adjusts move the camera, and so the picture's middle, that many metres to its right and up, whatever the distance. Nothing further than 40 m from the camera is drawn: a `distance` over 13.3 gives a blank picture, and the answer says so |
| `pitch`, `rotation` | degrees: the pitch tilts the camera about the head; the rotation turns the view round the creature, the turn of a side profile, and the head slides sideways in the picture as it does: an `xAdjust` of the other sign brings it back |
| `lightX`, `lightY`, `lightZ` | the light rig's offset: the row's where it has its own, else the game's table's, unless given |
| `show` | `true`: the picture is put up large on the left of the screen, with the appearance's number and label and what framed it under it, and the camera. `false`, or a call without it, takes down what is up |
| `seconds` | how long it stays up by itself: 20, at most 600 |

A value given replaces the row's and the rest are the row's, whichever row the lookup gave. A kind or a value given renders a creature whose picture is its kin's ready-made one too (a giant spider, by the spider's). Not one whose own appearance the game's table names a picture for (a wolf, a bear): the engine renders nothing for those (its render looks the appearance up in the table, gives the creature the picture's name and returns before it draws; read 2026-10-04), so the answer has the table's picture and `problem` says why.

The answer: `id`, `name`, `tag`, `appearance` and `label` (the appearance table's row and its label), `personal_space`, `persons_model` (its `ModelType` is `H` or `P`), `terrain_follow_pitch`, `kind` (`person`, `upright`, `on_all_fours`) and `kind_by` (what said so, with what it goes by: `it stands up, and is bigger than a person from its feet to its head (ModelType H, TerrainFollowPitch 0, 9.21 m from feet to head)`; or `asked for`), `source` (`rendered`, `the game's`, `the portraits table's`, `none`), `framed_by` (`row` its own row of the framing table, `portraits_table` the game's own row for it, `default` its kind's default row, `person` the game's Default row), `row` (the framing table's row that framed it, by its label: `66 Dragon, High`, `a body on all fours`; empty for the game's) and `rule` (the same in words, with the row's note of where it came from), `image` and `texture`, `ready` and `lack` (below), `rendered`, `fresh` (by a renderer made now, not one it had), `shown`, `camera` (`given`: whether it was given to the engine or is what the game's table has; `pitch`, `rotation`, `distance`, `zAdjust`, `xAdjust`, `lightX`, `lightY`, `lightZ`: the names the arguments have, so an answer's camera can be given back changed), `at_most` (a default held at the furthest the table lets it stand), `distance_per_extent` and `z_per_extent` (the camera against the creature's size, as a default's factors are reckoned from rows), `picture_m` (how wide the picture is at the head), `camera_from_head_m`, `past_far_plane`, `position`, `head` (the head node, null for none), `extent` and `extent_by`, and `problem`, anything to know in words. The log has the same at info: `turnbased_portrait for 0x7d9, put up large: Dragonling's portrait: img://... (rendered; row 64 Dragonling (by eye at the camp, ...): from ...)`.

`ready` is false when nothing could be rendered, and `lack` says which: `no_head_node` (its model has no node "head" for the camera to aim at, or the creature is not set up yet: one just spawned takes a few frames, so ask again in a moment before taking it for a body with no head) or `no_render` (the engine made no portrait). A wrong id is an error.

Whose the renderer is decides what a call does, and is why it is safe under a movie showing the picture. A party member's portrait is the game's own, which its party bar shows: the answer has it (`source` `the game's`) and nothing is rendered or changed. One the bar rendered for a card, or this tool rendered before, is rendered again in place, into the same texture (`fresh` false): nothing is released under the movie. One made for a creature of the fight is the bar's from then on and goes with the fight's. One made for a creature outside a fight is released once nothing shows it: half a second on without `show` (`problem` says `nothing shows it ...: its renderer is released in a moment`; not in the frame it was rendered in, since the engine's render only marks the picture to be drawn), else when the picture is taken down and the movie has said it let it go, or two seconds on. It needs the bar's movie (`[feedback] turn_bar = true` and `turnorder.swf` deployed): without it nothing is rendered, and the answer has what was measured and how the table would frame it, with `problem` saying `not rendered: the turn bar's movie is not there to show it`.

To tune a creature by hand: `turnbased_portrait {"id": "0x28df3", "show": true}` for its row's; then `{"id": "0x28df3", "rotation": 40, "xAdjust": -2.1, "show": true}` and so on, the rest staying the row's; `{"id": "0x28df3", "kind": "upright", "show": true}` to see it by its kind's default, its row passed over. In a fight its card follows too.

**Playing a turn by events alone** (issue 7; until then a script read `turnbased.state` several times a second until its phase said `awaiting order`). `turnbased.awaiting_order` comes each time a turn the player plays starts waiting: as it is handed over, after anything kept from the turn before has landed (`detail` `the turn is its player's`), and again when an action or a walk has played out and the turn goes on (`after its action`; also `the order queued no command`, `the order was refused` for a click the machine was waiting on). It is said at the step into waiting, not while it lasts: an order refused while the turn already waits brings `refused` alone.
